# Character Creation: Lineage

Zulnut is human. So are Zahna and Mordai, and so is nearly everyone they have ever met. In Shattered Origin that is not a limitation; it is the setting saying something. Lineage is the one word on the sheet that answers *who were you born as*, and for most tables it is answered in a single syllable and never thought about again.

It matters anyway, because a setting that says otherwise needs somewhere to say it. A world of seven peoples, each carrying magic in the blood, is a world this chapter can describe in the same terms as everything else — no new subsystem, no second set of rules, just entries in a catalog the way Backgrounds and domains are entries in a catalog.

Your Lineage is who you were born as. Your **Background** is what you did about it. They are separate questions on purpose, and a lineage that starts reading like a profession has stopped being a lineage.

---

## Reading the Entries — Lineages

> **Reading the Entries — Lineages**
>
> Every lineage prints four fields, in this order. A field is never printed empty; if a lineage has no Gift, the entry says *None*.
>
> **Name:** what these people are called, with the common in-world variants in italics after it.
>
> **Description:** two or three sentences — where they are from, how they live, what the world expects of them.
>
> **Gift:** *None*, or a **domain**. A gifted lineage names the domain its blood carries, or a short list to choose from if the gift takes more than one shape. The Gift is a domain in every respect (Chapter II.3): intuitive tradition, Spirit + Attune, Minor scope until it formalizes, full scope after. It **replaces the Background's Secondary Skill**, exactly as a magic-granting Background's Domain origin does. Where a lineage is gifted only in part, the entry states the rate as fiction — *four in five carry it* — and you say which your character is.
>
> **Heritage:** one narrow fact every member of the lineage grows up with, gifted or not. It works the way a Specialty works: when it bears directly on a roll, Standard becomes Easy; when it is tangential, the MM hands you the information without a roll. It draws from the **same** one-step allowance as Specialties and Techniques (Chapter III.1, *Difficulty*) — a Heritage and a Specialty that both apply are still one step, not two.

## Human

**Human** *(folk, people)*

**Description:** The default of the core rules and of Shattered Origin. Humans are what the rest of this book assumes: no gift, no heritage, nothing on the sheet that a Background and a Facet do not put there. Every example character in this book is human.

**Gift:** None.

**Heritage:** None. Whatever a human character knows from birth, their Background already says.

> **Through the Mirror — why the core lineage is empty**
>
> A lineage with nothing in it is not a placeholder. It is the baseline every other lineage is measured against.
>
> Shattered Origin defaults to human on purpose. A new table has one fewer choice to make on the night they are already learning the dice, and — more to the point — every non-human lineage a setting adds afterward is a *counted* difference from a known shape. When a setting Facet opens with "this world has seven lineages and the gift of each is a domain," the player already knows what a domain is and what it costs, because the rest of this chapter told them. Nobody has to learn two things at once.
>
> If we had filled Human in with a bonus to make it feel worth choosing, we would have had to make every later lineage worth choosing *against that bonus*, and the arithmetic would have started mattering more than the fiction. It does not, and this is where we keep it that way.

## The Gift Is a Domain

A gifted character's domain works as Chapter II.3 describes it. Three things are settled here so the Facet chapters do not have to repeat them.

**It comes from birth, not from study.** A Background's Domain origin is a story of learning — a teacher, a library, a night something went wrong. A Lineage Gift is a fact of blood. You still answer the four origin questions in Chapter II.3 (*where were you when you first understood what you could do…*), because a gift nobody has ever noticed is not yet a domain the character can reach for. The questions are the same; the answers are about recognition rather than instruction.

**One domain at creation.** A gifted character takes a Background that grants no domain. A character whose Background grants a domain takes an ungifted lineage — or plays an ungifted member of a gifted one, which is a perfectly ordinary thing to be. Second domains arrive the way they always do (Chapter II.4c, *Second Domain*; Chapter II.3, cross-Facet Tier 1).

**Minor scope until it formalizes.** Exactly as any pre-Technique domain (Chapter II.3, *Before the Technique*): real, reliable, and small.

---

## Formalization

A Lineage Gift **formalizes at your first Facet level, in whichever Facet that level lands, and spends no Technique pick.** The reflection scene for that level is the Gift arriving in full.

A Background domain still formalizes through the Soul or Mind Tier 1 Technique, as Chapter II.4 says. The two routes never combine, because a character holds one creation domain.

> **Through the Mirror — why blood formalizes for free**
>
> A Background domain is a practice the character is still learning, and the Tier 1 Technique is the curriculum that finishes it. That is why it costs the pick any curriculum costs.
>
> A Gift is not learned. It grows with the person, and the person grows through whatever Facet they grow through. If we charged for it, a Body-Facet character born gifted would have to cross-train into another Facet to reach full scope — which for some concepts means never — and "born gifted" would end up costing *more* than "studied magic." That is the wrong way round, and it would quietly tell every table that the fiction the setting is built on is a mechanical mistake.
>
> The Technique economy is untouched. No pick is spent, nothing is added to your Techniques, and the three-pick career is still three picks.

## MM: Creating a Custom Lineage

Five steps, mirroring the custom Background in Chapter II.6.

**1. Name it, and say who they are.** Two or three sentences. A lineage is a *people*, not a profession. If the description reads like a job — what they do for money, who employs them, what they trained in — you have written a Background.

**2. Decide whether it is gifted.** If not, skip to step 4. If so, the gift is a domain, and you write it in the catalog format the appendix uses: territory, *beyond this domain's focus*, three example intents at each scope. **Focused** is the default type for a gift — a people's magic does one thing deeply. **Standard** if the gift genuinely spans a territory. Never Prismatic; a prismatic territory is a lifetime's mastery, not a birthright.

**3. State the rate as fiction.** "Nearly all." "One in five." "Vanishingly rare." The rate is texture, and it is a prompt: it tells you how the ungifted are treated, which is usually the more interesting half of the question. It is never a roll.

**4. Write the Heritage.** One narrow fact, phrased the way a Specialty is phrased — specific enough that you know when it applies and when it does not. "Knows the caravan roads and the fair price of anything" is a Heritage. "Good with people" is not.

**5. Say what the Facet adds.** Every custom lineage lives in a setting Facet, and that Facet's counted-novelty line has to name it: *this Facet adds seven lineages; the core 2d6 is unchanged.* A reader should be able to learn everything your setting changes from one sentence.

> **MM Note — a lineage is not a stat bonus**
>
> **Default:** a lineage grants a Heritage, and possibly a Gift. Nothing else.
>
> **The dial:** you can hand a lineage a number — +1 Constitution, a skill rank, an extra Endurance Pool point.
>
> **The cost:** everything. The core deliberately hands out no numbers at creation beyond the eighteen points and what the Background grants, and the moment one lineage does, every other lineage in your setting has to be priced against it. Players stop choosing who their character is and start choosing what the sheet is worth. If a lineage feels thin, it does not need a bonus — it needs a better Heritage, or a Gift, or three more sentences of description.

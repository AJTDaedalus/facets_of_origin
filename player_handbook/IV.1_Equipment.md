# Compendium: Equipment

What you carry says who you are. A Warrior's plate, a Scout's bow, a Physician's bag of bandages: before anyone reads the sheet, the kit has told them. This chapter is the kit, what it costs, and how much of it you can carry.

---

## Slots

You have **10 + Body** inventory slots. Most items fill one slot. Heavy armor and heavy weapons fill two. A handful of small things that go together (a tinderbox, a whetstone and a spoon) share a slot. Coin fills a slot per 100 coins; a few coins in a purse fill nothing.

Slots also hold two things you never packed. Each **Wound** (Chapter III.2) fills a slot, and so does each point of **Fatigue** (Chapter II.3). When you take one and have no free slot, drop something to make room.

That is the whole of encumbrance. There are no weights and no speed penalties. If it fits in your slots, you can carry it; if it doesn't, you can't.

## Weapons

**Table IV.1–1: Weapons**

| Category | Damage | Slots | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unarmed | d4 | 0 | Fists, feet, grappling. | — |
| Light | d6 | 1 | Dagger, short sword, hand crossbow, sling. | Easy to hide; can be thrown. |
| Standard | d8 | 1 | Longsword, mace, spear, axe, staff. | — |
| Heavy | d10 | 2 | Greatsword, maul, halberd. | Two hands. Loud. |
| Ranged | d8 | 1 | Longbow, crossbow. | Needs ammunition (usage die). |

Pick a category, then name the weapon. The name is yours; the category sets the die. A staff is a standard weapon, and a longbow is a ranged one.

**Weapon kinds.** Some talents care what *kind* of weapon you hold: blades, blunt, polearms, bows, or thrown. A longsword is a blade, a mace is blunt, a spear or halberd is a polearm. The kind never changes the die on its own.

## Armor

**Table IV.1–2: Armor**

| Armor | Armor | Slots | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | 0 | 0 | — |
| Light | 1 | 1 | Leather, padded, a chain shirt. |
| Heavy | 2 | 2 | Plate, full mail. Stealth is Hard; a caster's full workings cost 1 extra Fatigue. |
| Shield | +1 | 1 | Adds to worn armor. Needs a hand. |

Your armor is the total of what you wear and carry, never more than **3**. It subtracts from the damage of every hit, and every hit still does at least 1 (Chapter III.3).

## The Usage Die

Things that run out (arrows, food, light, bandages) don't get counted. They get a **usage die**.

After a scene in which you used the item, or a leg of a journey, roll its usage die. On a **1 or 2**, it steps down: **d8 to d6, d6 to d4, d4 to gone.** On anything else, you still have plenty.

A fresh bundle starts at the die in Table IV.1–3. Buying more resets it.

## Coin

You start with **2d6 × 10** coin. Prices are rough, because a market is not a spreadsheet.

**Table IV.1–4: Prices**

| Price | Coin | For example |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap | 1 | A meal, a night in a common room, a torch. |
| Fair | 10 | A weapon, rope, a week of rations, a mule for a day. |
| Costly | 100 | Heavy armor, a horse, a favor from someone important. |

Most gear on Table IV.1–3 is fair. Heavy armor is costly. Anything rarer than a horse is a conversation with the MM, and usually a story.

> **Example — filling a pack**
>
> Zahna has 10 slots (Body +0). His Thaumaturge kit fills 5: staff, books and notes, ink and chalk, light armor, rations. After the fight in the lower archive, he also carries 2 Fatigue. That leaves him 3 slots, which is exactly enough for the box of records he has decided to steal, and not enough for anything else he finds tonight.

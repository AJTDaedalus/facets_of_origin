# Character Creation: Stats

Three numbers do the work of describing what your character is good at. They are named for the three Facets, because they are the three things a person can be good at: their body, their mind, and whatever it is that makes a room turn to look at them.

---

## The Three Stats

**Body.** Strength, speed, toughness and coordination. Rolled to fight hand to hand, climb, run, endure, and to Hold On at 0 HP.

**Mind.** Knowledge, perception, reasoning and memory. Rolled to notice, recall, work out, and to cast Thaumaturgy.

**Soul.** Will, presence, intuition and luck. Rolled to persuade, read people, steady your nerve, and to cast Invocation.

A stat is a modifier. You add it to 2d6 when you roll. That is all it is for, and it is the only number most rolls ask for.

When it is not obvious which stat a roll uses, ask what the character is actually doing. Picking a lock under a guard's nose is Body (hands). Working out how the lock was made is Mind. Talking the guard into opening it is Soul. The MM calls the stat along with the difficulty, before you roll.

---

## Setting Your Stats

**Table II.2–1: Stats at Creation**

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Your Facet's stat | +2 |
| One other stat, your choice | +1 |
| The last stat | +0 |

A stat never goes above **+3**. At levels 4 and 8 you add +1 to a stat of your choice (Chapter II.4). And no roll, however many knacks and talents you pile on, ever adds more than **+4** in total.

That ceiling is deliberate. Your character grows through hit points, talents, treasure and the people they know. A level 10 character is far more capable than a level 1 character, but a lock is still a lock, and it still wants a roll.

---

## Two Characters, One Stat Line

Mordai and Zulnut have the same stats: Body +2, Soul +1, Mind +0. They play nothing alike. Mordai wears plate, carries a longsword and takes hits for his friends. Zulnut wears nothing heavier than a shirt, avoids hits on principle, and would rather climb the wall than fight the man guarding the gate.

The difference is carried by everything else on the sheet: their **knacks** (what they trained in and where they come from), their **kit**, and their **talents**. Stats say how good you are in broad strokes. The rest of the sheet says at what.

> **Through the Mirror — why only three**
>
> Earlier drafts of this game had nine attributes. Nobody could remember which one a roll wanted, and every character ended up with the same two high numbers in different clothes.
>
> Three stats means the MM can call a roll without looking anything up and a player can read their sheet in a glance. The texture those nine numbers used to carry now lives in knacks and talents, which are words rather than numbers, and words are easier to play.

## In Play: The Vanishing of Millhaven

The party has arrived in Thornwall to look into a string of disappearances in the nearby village of Millhaven: three days, seven people, no bodies. They have decided the municipal archive is the place to start.

**MM:** "The archive is vast. Floor-to-ceiling shelves, a front desk buried under forty years of paper, and an archivist who looks like she has outlasted four city governors and plans to outlast four more. Where do you start?"

**Zahna:** "I cross-reference the census records with the tax assessments near Millhaven, then check the incident logs for anything closed without resolution."

(The MM sighs, the sigh of someone who built a mystery and is watching it get taken apart like a sum.)

**MM:** "Mind. Neither of your knacks is the city's paperwork, so no bonus. Standard."

→ Zahna rolls **2d6 + Mind (+2)** at Standard and gets a **10**. Full success.

**MM:** "Three prior disappearances, fifteen years ago. Same season, same area. There was an investigation. It was closed. Very quietly."

*Zahna notes this on a scroll and keeps reading. He does not share it with anyone.*

**Mordai:** "I tell the archivist that people are missing in Millhaven and we need to know what happened there fifteen years ago."

**MM:** "She tells you there is a formal request process and points at a stack of forms."

**Mordai:** "There isn't time for forms."

**MM:** "She tells you there is always time for forms. Soul, at Hard. She has run this archive longer than you have been alive and has opinions about people who think urgency excuses paperwork."

→ Mordai rolls **2d6 + Soul (+1)** at Hard (−1) and gets an **8**. Partial success.

**MM:** "You get what you came for, and more than she meant to give. She shoves back from her desk, keyring jangling on the edge as she stands, and informs you at volume that the Millhaven business was handled by the Governor's office, that it was resolved, and that the archive closes in one hour. She is pointing at the door. The two clerks in the back have stopped pretending to work."

*The archivist is no longer behind her desk. The keyring is.*

**Zulnut:** "The brass key. Lower archives."

(The MM had meant that keyring as scenery. Zulnut's player has just called a shot on a detail from the narration, mid-chaos, with perfect timing.)

**Another player:** "Spark?"

**MM:** "Absolutely. And Easy, because Mordai has given you the perfect window. Body."

→ Zulnut spends the Spark and rolls **3d6 + Body (+2)**, keeping the best two, at Easy (+1) and gets a **14**. Full success.

**MM:** "She is telling Mordai that she has personally filed complaints against three city watchmen and is not intimidated by whatever he thinks he is. She does not notice you. You have the key."

*Outside, Zahna explains what he found. Zulnut produces a keyring (several keys, none labeled) and regards it with mild interest.*

**Zahna:** "Do you know which one—"

**Zulnut:** "No."

**Zahna:** "When did you even—"

**Zulnut:** "Mordai was very loud."

(The MM is frantically drawing a basement map.)

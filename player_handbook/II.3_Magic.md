# Character Creation: Magic

Zahna has never once looked up a spell. He has a territory (the signs people write and the meanings they carry) and inside that territory he can attempt anything he can describe, at whatever ambition he is willing to pay for.

That is the whole of magic here. A domain says where you can reach. An intent says what you are reaching for. A scope says how far. Then you roll the same two dice as everyone else, and a big working leaves you tired in a way you can feel in your pack straps.

**If your class doesn't cast, skip this chapter.** Nothing else in character creation depends on it.

---

## Who Casts

You cast if you hold a casting talent. There are two, one for each tradition:

**Table II.3–1: The Traditions**

| Tradition | Talent | Facet | Stat | Style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thaumaturgy | *Thaumaturgy* | Mind | Mind | Scholarly: magic studied, written, worked out |
| Invocation | *Invocation* | Soul | Soul | Intuitive: magic felt, called, answered |

The Thaumaturge and the Invoker presets start with their talent. Any other Mind or Soul character may take it as a level-up pick, or as one of their two starting talents in a custom class (Chapter II.4). A casting talent gives you one domain from your tradition's list and two signature workings.

Casting talents never cross Facets. A Mind character cannot learn Invocation from a teacher, and a Soul character cannot learn Thaumaturgy.

## Domain, Intent, Scope

### Domain

Your **domain** is your magical territory: the thing your magic is *about*. Fire, Shadow, Inscription, Divination. There are 21, each described in the Appendix: Magic Domain Catalog, with what it covers and where its reach ends.

A domain is agreed at character creation and used the same way from then on. A Fire caster cannot command the weather because fire is hot and storms are loud. That conversation happens once, with the catalog open, and then it is settled.

### Intent

Your **intent** is what you want to happen, said in one sentence. *I want the lock to melt.* *I want the guard to hear footsteps behind him.* There is no list of spells. Anything your domain covers is fair game, if you can say it plainly.

### Scope

Your **scope** is how far you reach. It sets the difficulty, the cost, and the harm a working can do.

**Table II.3–2: Scope**

| Scope | Difficulty | Fatigue | From level | Harm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minor | Standard | 0 | 1 | none; in a fight it is a stunt |
| Significant | Standard | 1 | 1 | 1d8 to one target |
| Major | Hard | 2 | 3 | 2d8 to a group |

**Minor** is small, local and brief: a candle lit, a whisper carried, a door that sticks for a moment. It never deals damage and never decides anything on its own.

**Significant** is meaningful power or precision: a door fused, a person bound, one foe struck.

**Major** changes the scene: a building, a crowd, a group of foes, an effect that lasts. You cannot attempt Major workings before level 3.

**The full-form rule.** If a working does something meaningful, whether that is real power or fine precision, it is not Minor, however small it looks. Hurting someone is never Minor. A chain of small workings that add up to a big result is priced as the big result. The MM makes this call, and makes it before the roll.

> **Example — one intent at three scopes**
>
> *Minor:* Zahna traces a glyph on a lock so it sticks for a moment when the guard tries it.
>
> *Significant:* Zahna seals the door shut with a glyph that holds until he says otherwise.
>
> *Major (level 3 or higher):* Zahna seals every door on the floor at once, and they stay sealed until morning.

## Casting

Say your domain, intent and scope. The MM confirms the scope and sets the difficulty. Then roll **2d6 + Mind** (Thaumaturgy) or **2d6 + Soul** (Invocation), +1 if a knack fits.

**10+:** it works as you intended.

**7–9:** it works, with a cost. You choose one of two costs offered from the magic complications table (in MM6; the app rolls two and shows them). The working still happens.

**6−:** something goes wrong. The MM rolls on the magic mishaps table (also in MM6), and the Graceful Fail applies as it does to any 6− (Chapter III.1). A mishap is not always bad for you. It is always interesting.

Pay the Fatigue whatever the result. The effort was spent.

### Harm

A harmful working deals damage like a weapon: 1d8 to one target at Significant, or 2d8 to a group at Major (roll once; every foe in the group takes it). Add your level's damage bonus (Chapter II.4), and the target's armor subtracts, as with any hit (Chapter III.3).

### Sparks and Help

Sparks, Help and Borrowed Trouble work on a casting roll exactly as on any roll: each adds a d6 and you keep the best two.

## Fatigue

Magic is paid for in **Fatigue**. Each point of Fatigue fills one of your inventory slots, just as a coil of rope does. A night's rest in safety clears all of it.

**No free slot, no full working.** If every slot you have is full, you cannot cast at Significant or Major. You can still cast Minor workings, which cost nothing.

**Heavy armor** adds 1 Fatigue to every Significant or Major working. A caster may wear plate. It will cost them.

This is the whole limit. A caster who casts a lot is also a caster carrying less, and one bad hit from real trouble, because Wounds take slots too.

> **Through the Mirror — why magic takes up room in your pack**
>
> Magic in this game has always been open-ended: no spell list, just a territory and your imagination. That freedom needs a price, or the caster solves every problem in the first five minutes.
>
> We tried counting prepared intents and it worked, but it was one more thing to track. Fatigue in slots is a price everyone can see on the sheet. It also connects to everything else: a caster who fills their pack with gear has less room to cast, and a caster who has cast all day has less room for Wounds. The whole party can see the Thaumaturge getting tired, which is exactly what we want them to see.

## Signature Workings

Every caster names **two signature workings** at creation, and one more at level 5 and again at level 9. A signature working is one specific thing you do, written as a short phrase: *a sealing glyph that holds a door shut until its trigger word; call the crows; put out every light in the room.*

When what you cast is a signature working, it is **one step Easier** at whatever scope you cast it. It is also what your character is known for. The other casters in the city know Zahna as the one who seals things.

Choose signatures that come up. A signature you use every session is worth more than a clever one you use once.

---

## More Than One Domain

A caster holds one domain. The *Wider Domain* talent (Chapter II.4b) gives a second from the same tradition, one step Harder until the talent is improved. The improved form can instead trade that second domain for a **prismatic** domain (from level 5), which is always one step Harder.

The six prismatic domains are the widest territories in the catalog: The Undying, Fate and The Living World for Invocation; The Arcane, The Constructed Mind and Chronomancy for Thaumaturgy. No character starts with one.

---

## Magic Outside the Traditions

**Lineage gifts.** A setting may offer lineages born with a spark of magic (Chapter II.5). A gift gives you one domain at Minor scope only, free, cast with Soul. It never grows past Minor unless you also take a casting talent.

**Relics.** A relic's power belongs to the relic (Chapter IV.2). Anyone can use one. Carrying a relic that throws fire does not make you a caster.

**The Body.** The Facet of the Body has no tradition and no domains. What the Body trains is formidable, and it is not magic.

## In Play: The Thornwall Lower Archive

The party, holding a key that may or may not open the lower archives, has come back after dark. The archivist has gone home. The archive has not.

**MM:** "The lower archive isn't on the building's plan. No windows, no vents you can find, and the air at the bottom of the stairs smells like it has been here since the last century. One door: iron-banded oak, a lock the size of your fist. And light seeping from under it, which there shouldn't be, because this archive has been closed for fifteen years."

**Zulnut:** "We have a key. I try it."

(The MM had not expected this to be the approach.)

**MM:** "The key fits. The lock does not turn. It's been sealed from the inside."

**Zahna:** "I read the seal. What kind of working is it?"

**MM:** "Mind, and *Arcane theory* fits. Standard."

→ Zahna rolls **2d6 + Mind (+2) + knack (+1)** at Standard and gets a **10**. Full success.

**MM:** "It's not a ward. It's Inscription: a glyph on the inside of the lock. Someone wrote a locking instruction into the lock itself, and it's still active, which means whatever it was told to keep shut, it is still keeping shut."

**Zahna:** "This is either a trap or an extremely dedicated filing system."

**Mordai:** "I will break the door."

**Zahna:** "Please don't. I could unwrite it. Significant, one Fatigue, and I—"

**Mordai:** "The door is not alive."

**Zahna:** "I am aware of that."

**MM:** "Mordai, Body, at Hard. It's reinforced."

→ Mordai rolls **2d6 + Body (+2)** at Hard (−1) and gets an **8**. Partial success.

**MM:** "The door opens. You don't so much beat the lock as beat the hinges. The oak holds and the iron doesn't, and the door swings in with a sound like a small, dignified explosion. The light inside does not go out. Something in the room makes a noise that suggests it has been alone for a very long time and is not pleased that has changed."

**Zulnut:** "I was going to wait outside."

(The MM smiles the particular smile of someone who has been waiting for this exact moment.)

**Zahna:** *(to Mordai, quietly)* "It would have cost me one slot."

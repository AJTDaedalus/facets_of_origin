# Rules: Core Resolution

Every uncertain moment in the game is settled by one roll. Fighting, casting, climbing, lying to a magistrate: the same two dice, the same three bands. This chapter is that roll and the handful of ways to change it.

---

## The Roll

When your character attempts something risky and the outcome is uncertain, roll **2d6 + a stat**. The MM names the stat and the difficulty before you roll. If one of your knacks fits, add **+1**.

**Table III.1–1: Outcome Tiers**

| Total | Result | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 10+ | Full success | You achieve your goal cleanly. |
| 7–9 | Success with a cost | You succeed, but with a complication or cost. The story moves forward at a price. |
| 6− | Things go wrong | Things go wrong, but the story always moves forward. |

**Knacks never stack.** However many of your knacks fit, the bonus is +1.

**The +4 ceiling.** Your stat, a knack, and any bonus a talent gives never add up to more than +4 on one roll. Difficulty is not a bonus and does not count toward it.

---

## Difficulty

The MM sets the difficulty from the circumstances, never from how good your character is. Your stat already says that.

**Table III.1–2: Difficulty**

| Difficulty | Modifier | When |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | +1 | Clear advantage, weak or inattentive opposition, favorable conditions. |
| Standard | +0 | The default: uncertain outcome, no special advantage or impediment. |
| Hard | −1 | Skilled opposition, poor conditions, acting against the grain. |
| Very Hard | −2 | Extraordinary opposition, severe circumstances, nearly impossible odds. |

Standard is the default. When the MM isn't sure, it is Standard. Effects that make something "one step Easier" or "one step Harder" move it one row on this table; Very Hard is as hard as it gets, and Easy is as easy.

> **MM Note — say it out loud**
>
> **Default:** name the stat and the difficulty, with a reason, before anyone touches the dice. "Body, Hard, because the stones are wet."
>
> **The dial:** you can keep the difficulty hidden and reveal it after.
>
> **The cost:** a hidden difficulty feels like a trap when the roll fails, and players stop taking risks. A difficulty said out loud is a piece of information they can plan around, or spend a Spark on.

---

## Extra Dice

Three things add a die to a roll. Each adds a **d6**, and you keep the **best two** dice. They all stack.

**A Spark.** Spend one before you roll.

**Help.** An ally who spends their action helping you adds a die. They share whatever cost the roll brings: if you are caught, they were there too. One Help per roll.

**Borrowed Trouble.** Accept a complication offered before the roll, and add a die. One per roll.

Spend them *before* the roll. A character who spends a Spark, takes Help and accepts Borrowed Trouble rolls 5d6 and keeps the best two. That is the most one roll can be made into.

---

## Naturals

Look at the two dice you kept.

**Table III.1–3: Natural Results**

| Kept dice | Result |
|---|---|
| 6 and 6 | A full success whatever the modifiers, and something more besides. You name it. |
| 1 and 1 | If the roll failed, the Graceful Fail is confirmed without asking. |

A natural 12 always succeeds, even at Very Hard. A natural 2 does not force a failure; a character with enough in their favor can still make the roll, and then nothing special happens.

---

## Sparks

A **Spark** is a narrative resource you earn by doing something remarkable and spend for an edge when the stakes are highest. You start every session with **3**. Sparks do not carry over.

### Earning Sparks

**MM award.** The MM hands one over for exceptional roleplay, creative problem-solving, or playing your character's traits and weaknesses at a real cost. No ceremony, no waiting for the scene to end.

**The peer call.** Any player may call out "Spark?" for another player's moment. The MM confirms. The recognition comes from the table, which is the point.

**Act break nomination.** After a major scene transition, each player may nominate one other player for something they did in the scene just past. The MM confirms, and almost always should.

**The Graceful Fail.** On any 6−, narrate how you make the failure worse or richer for the story, and claim a Spark; the MM confirms. The narration has to *add* something: a complication, a truth about your character, a detail the table can use. Restating the failure is just failing.

### Spending Sparks

Before you roll, spend as many Sparks as you like. Each adds a d6; keep the best two. Sparks work on every roll you make, including attacks, workings, avoid rolls and Hold On.

---

## Borrowed Trouble

Sometimes you need the die and you don't have the Spark. You can always buy one.

**Before a roll, the MM or any player may offer you a complication.** Accept it, and add a d6, keeping the best two. The price is not the *risk* of the complication. It is the complication itself: **it happens whether you succeed or fail.** You are selling a piece of your own near future for a better chance now.

Offering costs nothing and declining costs nothing. A declined offer simply didn't happen. A good offer is specific, easy to picture, and genuinely bad. "You get seen" is an offer; "something bad happens later" is not.

> **Example — Borrowed Trouble**
>
> **Zulnut:** I go up the outside of the building.
>
> **MM:** Body, at Hard: wet stone, and you're carrying more than you should. *(then, after a moment)* Borrowed Trouble: you make it up either way, but you leave the satchel on the ledge to do it, and it's still up there when you come down.
>
> **Zulnut:** *(who has thought about this for slightly too long)* Yes. Obviously yes.
>
> *Zulnut rolls 3d6 + Body (+2) at Hard (−1) and keeps the best two. The satchel is on the ledge whatever the dice do.*

> **MM Note — what Borrowed Trouble is for**
>
> It is the pressure valve. When a player wants something badly and the odds are poor, the answer does not have to be "roll and hope" or "no". It can be "yes, and here's what it costs."
>
> Two failure modes. Don't offer trouble that takes away the player's next choice; it should give them a new problem, not a dead end. And don't offer it on every roll, or it becomes a tax. Once or twice a session, at moments already tense, is the right rhythm.

## Avoid

When something happens *to* your character (poison in the cup, a floor giving way, a voice in your head telling you to kneel), you **avoid** it. Roll **2d6 + the stat that fits what is acting on you**: Body for what acts on the flesh, Mind for what acts on the senses and attention, Soul for what acts on the will.

**Table III.1–4: Avoiding Harm**

| Total | Result |
|---|---|
| 10+ | You avoid it entirely. |
| 7–9 | You avoid the worst of it; something lingers. |
| 6− | It takes hold. |

Avoid is for things that come to you. If your character chose to engage with the danger, it's an ordinary roll instead. The difference matters: an avoid roll is a reaction, and the MM calls for one only when there is something specific to avoid.

---

## Contested Rolls and Group Rolls

**Against someone the MM plays**, outside a fight, only you roll. How sharp the guard is sets the difficulty of your roll to slip past him. The MM's characters roll only to attack in combat (Chapter III.3).

**Against another player's character**, both roll. The higher total wins. On a tie, both get a success with a cost, and the situation deepens.

**When the whole party does the same thing** (sneaking past the same guards, climbing the same cliff), everyone rolls. If most of you get 7 or better, the group succeeds. The MM still notes who stumbled and who shone; a character who rolled 10+ is the one who spotted the loose board and warned the others.

When one character is clearly the one doing the work and the others are helping, use Help instead: the lead rolls, and each helper adds a die.

---

## When Not to Roll

Call for a roll only when the outcome is uncertain, the stakes matter, and both success and failure would move the story. If failure would just mean nothing happens, don't roll: say yes and move on. A Specialty (Chapter II.6) covers a lot of this ground by itself.

> **MM Note — the "don't roll, just do it" principle**
>
> This game has fewer rolls per session than most, on purpose. Every roll should be a moment when the table leans in. If you are rolling for routine things, the dice are stealing weight from the moments that deserve it. Lean toward yes.

---

## Reading the Result

**On a 10+**, the character gets what they wanted, cleanly. Don't tack on a hidden cost.

**On a 7–9**, they get it, and something else happens too: it takes longer, it costs something, it leaves them exposed, it attracts attention, or the win is smaller than hoped. The MM names the cost. In the app, a 7–9 shows a short list of costs for the MM to choose from (MM6).

**On a 6−**, things go wrong, and the story moves. The MM makes a move: introduces a new danger, takes something away, reveals an unwelcome truth, or puts someone in a spot. A 6− is never "nothing happens". The situation the characters face afterward is not the one they rolled against.

## In Play: The Front Desk

The archive scene from Chapter II.2 starts here, half an hour earlier, at a desk with a sign on it.

**MM:** "The Thornwall Municipal Archive closes to the public at four. It's ten past. The front desk has a brass bell, a stack of request slips, and an archivist who has already put her coat on."

**Mordai:** "I explain that this is important."

**MM:** "How?"

**Mordai:** "Loudly."

**MM:** "That's Soul. She's leaving, she has heard it all before, and you are large and shouting in a library. Hard. Saying it now so nobody's surprised later."

→ Mordai rolls **2d6 + Soul (+1)** at Hard (−1) and gets a **6**. Failure.

**MM:** "She takes her coat *off*. Not to help you. To write your description down on a slip. You are now a person the archive knows about."

**Zahna:** "Can I point out that I'm not with him?"

**MM:** "You can try."

**Zahna:** "I'm not with him, I only need the Millhaven parish returns, and I know the shelf."

**MM:** "Mind. You're asking a professional a professional question in her own language, so *Guild Apprentice* fits, and you've just set yourself apart from the shouting. Easy."

→ Zahna rolls **2d6 + Mind (+2) + knack (+1)** at Easy (+1) and gets an **11**. Full success.

**MM:** "She fetches the box herself. It takes her four minutes, and for four minutes the desk is unattended."

**Zulnut:** *(delighted)* "I would like to be near the desk."

**MM:** (I know. I know you would.) "That's Chapter II.2's problem. Roll it there."

Notice what the 6 did. It didn't stop the party getting into the archive. It cost Mordai something that will be waiting for him later.

## Standing Rulings

### Acting on Unnarrated Details

A player may act only on details the MM has described. A player may always **ask**: "Is there a window?" "How high is the ceiling?" Asking costs nothing and is encouraged. What a player may not do is declare an action that assumes the answer. The MM's default answer leans toward yes, and once a detail is narrated, anyone may use it.

### Trying Again

A failed or partial roll may be tried again **only if the fiction has changed**: a new approach, new information, or time that cost something. If nothing has changed, the first result stands. A new approach gets a fresh difficulty.

> **MM Note — "What's different this time?"**
>
> That question is the whole ruling. Ask it back. If the player has an answer, they have a roll. If they don't, the first result stands and the scene moves on.

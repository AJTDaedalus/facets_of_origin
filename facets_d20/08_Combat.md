# Combat

Combat in Facets d20 is SRD 5.2.1 combat with less bookkeeping. You still roll a d20, add your bonus and compare it to Armor Class, and you still move, take an action and maybe a bonus action. The changes are around that: each side rolls initiative once, monsters don't roll damage, and most foes give up before they die. A standard fight is over in three or four rounds.

---

## The Round

A round has two halves: one side acts, then the other.

**Table 8–1: The Round**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Who goes first | Each side rolls one d20 (below). |
| 2. First side acts | Every creature on that side takes its turn, in any order the side likes. |
| 3. Second side acts | Every creature on the other side does the same. |
| 4. Next round | Back to step 2. The order of the sides never changes. |

### Who Goes First

If one side is **surprised**, it goes second. No roll.

Otherwise each side rolls **one d20** and adds its best initiative modifier: for the party, the best Dexterity modifier among you (plus anything that adds to initiative); for the foes, the MM uses the best among them. The higher total goes first. On a tie, the players do. If anyone in the party has *Alert* and is conscious, the party's roll has advantage.

That is the only initiative roll of the fight.

### Your Turn

Your turn is the SRD turn: move up to your speed, take one action, take a bonus action if something gives you one, and interact with one object for free. You can split your movement around your action.

On your side's half, the players decide the order. If the rogue wants the fighter to knock the ogre prone first, the fighter goes first. Each character takes a whole turn before the next starts. Every creature still has its own turn, so effects that last "until the start of your next turn" work as written.

> **MM Note: the committee problem**
>
> **Default:** let the party plan its half of the round out loud. That's the fun of side initiative.
>
> **The dial:** if the table starts deliberating for minutes, say "thirty seconds," then ask whoever looks most certain to go.
>
> **The cost:** push too hard and you get the old problem back, where everyone waits in silence for their name.

### Actions

The actions are the SRD 5.2.1 list: **Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence, Magic, Ready, Search, Study** and **Utilize**. Help gives advantage, as the SRD says. Influence in a fight uses the attitude track (Chapter 06); a foe who has been hurt is often much readier to listen. Ready is how you react to something on the other side's half: "if it comes through the door, I hit it."

## Reactions and Opportunity Attacks

You get **one reaction** per round, and it comes back at the start of your turn.

**Leaving an enemy's reach provokes one opportunity attack from it**, if it has its reaction. It happens only when you move out on your own; being shoved, teleporting or taking the Disengage action doesn't provoke.

---

## Attacks and Damage

Attack rolls, advantage and disadvantage, critical hits and cover work as the SRD says.

**You roll your damage. Monsters don't.** A monster deals the **fixed damage** printed on its stat block, which is the SRD average. On a critical hit the MM rolls it: double the damage dice, roll them, add the modifier.

### One Rider a Turn

A **rider** is extra damage dice that a talent adds to a hit. The talents that have one say so: *Precision* and *Sworn Strike*. **Only one rider applies on your turn**, however many you could trigger. Flat bonuses aren't riders (*Rage*'s +2, Veteran's +2, *Anatomist*'s Intelligence), and neither is *Cleave*'s damage to a second foe or a spell such as *Hunter's Mark*.

### One Controlled Creature

**You control at most one summoned or conjured creature, companion or undead at a time.** It acts right after your turn, and it deals fixed damage, like a monster. *Clockwork Guardian* is a controlled creature; *Wild Shape* isn't, because the beast is you.

---

## Sparks in a Fight

A Spark is spent after a d20 test and before the MM says what happens: add 1d6 to it (Chapter 06). You can spend one on your own roll or on an ally's roll you help with, and helping this way costs no action.

---

## Bloodied and Morale

A creature at or below half its hit points is **Bloodied**. This applies to everyone, you included. The MM tells you when a foe becomes Bloodied; you can see the limp, the dropped shield, the look toward the door.

Most foes don't want to die. A standard foe checks its **morale** once, when it is first Bloodied: a DC 10 Wisdom save. If it fails, it **breaks**: it flees, surrenders or tries to bargain, whichever suits it. Minions check when their leader falls. Bosses never check, and neither do mindless or bound creatures; the MM knows which is which.

So a fight usually ends before the last foe falls, and it often ends with somebody you can question. A foe who surrenders is a prisoner and a source of answers. What you do with one is the kind of choice the game is built to ask you, and the MM will remember it.

---

## Kinds of Foe

**Minions** have 1 hit point. Any damage kills one, from a hit or an area. A minion that succeeds on a save against damage takes none. Minions deal fixed damage and come in groups.

**Standard** foes are SRD stat blocks used as written, with fixed damage.

**Bosses** take a turn at the **top of every round**, before either side, and another turn in their side's half. When an effect would stun, paralyze, incapacitate, banish or polymorph a boss or put it to sleep, the boss loses its next turn instead and the effect ends; it can lose at most one turn a round this way. A boss changes when it becomes Bloodied. Bosses have no legendary or lair actions. The rest of their rules are the MM's (Chapter 09).

---

## Dropping and Dying

The SRD rule, unchanged. At 0 hit points you fall **unconscious** and make **death saving throws** at the start of each of your turns: DC 10, no modifier. Three successes and you're stable; three failures and you die. A natural 20 brings you back with 1 hit point; a natural 1 counts as two failures. Taking damage at 0 is a failure, or two from a critical hit. Any healing wakes you.

When you reduce a foe to 0 with a melee attack, you can knock it out instead of killing it.

**Conditions** are the SRD 5.2.1 list, unchanged.

> **Through the Mirror: why fights are short here**
>
> We cut four slow things. Side initiative ends waiting for your name. Fixed monster damage saves the MM a roll on every attack. Minions let a big fight stay quick. Morale lets a fight end once it has been decided. We left alone everything a player does on their own turn. The encounter tables in Chapter 09 were tuned by simulation so that a standard fight lasts three or four rounds.

## In Play: The Alley

*The party is walking back from the archive after dark. The MM has already described the alley: narrow, wet cobbles, one lamp at the far end, and a stack of crates against the left wall.*

**MM:** Four figures step out from behind the crates. Three have clubs. The fourth has a sword and the air of someone who has done this before. Your purses, he says, and nobody has to bleed.

**Mordai:** I'd like him to try.

**MM:** Nobody's surprised. One roll a side.

→ The party's best initiative modifier is Zulnut's Dexterity (+3): **d20 + 3**, a **17**. The MM rolls **d20 + 2** for the thugs, an **11**. *The party goes first.*

→ Behind the screen: the three with clubs are minions (AC 12, 1 hit point, a fixed 4 damage). The leader has AC 13 and 22 hit points and deals a fixed 6.

**Zulnut:** The nearest one with a club. I hit him and keep walking.

→ Zulnut rolls **d20 + 5** against AC 12: a **15**. Hit. Any damage kills a minion.

*One minion folds up against the crates. Zulnut steps over him and ends up under the lamp, as if he'd been there all evening.*

**Mordai:** The leader.

→ Mordai rolls **d20 + 6** against AC 13: a **17**. Hit. His longsword deals **1d8 + 3**: 8 + 3 = **11**. The leader is at 11 of 22, so he is Bloodied, and he checks morale: a DC 10 Wisdom save, and he rolls a **14**.

**MM:** He staggers, and he doesn't run. He wants this purse now.

**Zahna:** *Fire Bolt* at the leader.

→ Zahna rolls **d20 + 5**: a **9**. Miss.

**Zahna:** The lighting in this alley is inadequate.

**MM:** Their half. Both minions go for Zahna, who is complaining loudly about the lighting.

→ The MM rolls two attacks: an **8** misses, a **16** hits. Zahna takes a fixed **4**. The leader swings at Mordai: a **12** against AC 19.

*The blade rings off Mordai's shield.*

**MM:** Round two. Your side.

→ Mordai rolls a natural **20**: a critical hit. He rolls **2d8 + 3** and gets **13**. *The leader goes down in a heap of expensive cloak.*

**MM:** Their leader's fallen, so the minions check morale.

→ DC 10 Wisdom saves: a **6** and a **3**. Both break.

**MM:** One drops his club and runs. The other raises both hands and says he has a mother.

**Zulnut:** I'd like a cigarette, and I'd like it lit from that lamp.

**MM:** (Two rounds. Nobody dead, one prisoner, and the fight took less time than choosing dinner.)

# Character Creation: Lineage

Zulnut is human. So are Zahna and Mordai, and so is nearly everyone they have ever met. In Shattered Origin that is not a limitation; it is the setting saying something. Lineage is the one word on the sheet that answers *who were you born as*, and at most tables it is answered in one syllable and never thought about again.

It matters anyway, because a setting that says otherwise needs somewhere to say it. Your lineage is who you were born as. Your background is what you did about it (Chapter II.6). They are separate questions on purpose, and a lineage that reads like a profession has stopped being a lineage.

---

> **Reading the Entries — lineages**
>
> Every lineage prints four fields, in this order. A field is never printed empty; if a lineage has no gift, the entry says *None*.
>
> **Name:** what these people are called, with common variants in italics after it.
>
> **Description:** two or three sentences: where they are from, how they live, what the world expects of them.
>
> **Gift knack:** *None*, or a third knack that every gifted member of the lineage carries. It works like any knack (Chapter II.4): +1 when it fits, never stacking with your others.
>
> **Gift domain:** *None*, or a line saying how the lineage's magic **shows itself**. The player chooses which domain it is (Chapter II.3). Where only some of a people are gifted, the entry says so as fiction, and you decide which your character is.

---

## Human

**Human** *(folk, people)*

**Description:** The default of the core rules and of Shattered Origin. Humans are what the rest of the book assumes: no gift, and nothing on the sheet that a Facet, a class and a background do not put there. Every example character in this book is human.

**Gift knack:** None.

**Gift domain:** None.

> **Through the Mirror — why the core lineage is empty**
>
> A lineage with nothing in it is not a placeholder. It is the baseline every other lineage is measured against.
>
> Shattered Origin defaults to human on purpose. A new table has one fewer choice to make on the night it is learning the dice, and every lineage a setting adds later is a *counted* difference from a known shape. If we had given Human a bonus to make it worth choosing, every later lineage would have to be worth choosing against that bonus, and the arithmetic would start to matter more than the fiction.

---

## Gifted Lineages

A setting Facet may add lineages whose people are born with something extra. A gifted lineage gives exactly two things, and a setting may give one without the other:

**A gift knack.** A third knack, describing what these people know or can do from birth. *Stone-sense. The old songs of the river folk.* It follows every knack rule: +1 when it fits, and knacks never stack.

**A gift domain.** Magic in the blood, at **Minor scope only**. The player chooses one domain from the catalog (not a prismatic one); the lineage decides only how the gift looks, sounds and feels. A gift is always cast with **Soul**, whatever the domain, because blood is not study. Minor workings cost no Fatigue, so a gift is free to use and never grows past Minor.

A gift stays a gift. A gifted character who also takes a casting talent casts the talent's domain as that talent says, and the gift at Minor with Soul, as before.

## MM: Creating a Custom Lineage

**1. Name it and say who they are.** Two or three sentences. A lineage is a *people*, not a job. If the description says what they do for money, you have written a background.

**2. Decide whether it is gifted.** If not, you are done.

**3. Write the gift.** A gift knack, a gift domain, or both. For a domain, write one line on how the gift **shows itself** in these people and stop there. Do not write a new domain; your players choose which existing one it is, and two members of the same people can carry gifts as different as Fire and Divination and still be unmistakably kin.

**4. State the rate as fiction.** "Nearly all." "One in five." "Vanishingly rare." The rate tells you how the ungifted are treated, which is usually the more interesting half of the question. It is never a roll.

**5. Count it.** Every custom lineage lives in a setting Facet, and that Facet's counted-novelty line names it: *this Facet adds seven lineages; the core 2d6 is unchanged.*

> **MM Note — a lineage is not a stat bonus**
>
> **Default:** a lineage grants a gift knack, a Minor-only gift domain, both, or nothing.
>
> **The dial:** you can hand a lineage a number: +1 to a stat, extra HP, an extra slot.
>
> **The cost:** everything. The moment one lineage has a number, every other lineage in your setting has to be priced against it, and players stop choosing who their character is and start choosing what the sheet is worth. If a lineage feels thin, it needs a better knack or three more sentences of description, not a bonus.

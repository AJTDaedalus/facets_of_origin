## How to Read This Chapter

**The blocks are built for four 4th-level characters.** Challenge ratings and XP
are the SRD's. Every fight card in chapter IX sums its foes' XP, multiplies the sum for
their number, and sets the result against the party's thresholds: for four 4th-level
characters, **Easy 500 · Medium 1,000 · Hard 1,500 · Deadly 2,000** (chapter IX,
"Running the Snakes", has the method and the other party sizes). The
three Uninvited are the deliberate exception. They are CR 9 to 11, and they carry a
trait called **Leashed** that means none of that matters, because tonight they
cannot be killed. The **Attendant** who came with them is the other exception, in the
other direction: a boss that can be beaten, and its block and card S14 say how.
**Master Vell** is the last
exception. His numbers are real, set so high that no party of 4th or 5th level drops
him before the boat clears, and he is worth no XP.

**Every block has four lines after its numbers**: **Wants** (what ends the fight without a body), **Tells** (what a watchful
character sees before it happens), **Breaks** (when it stops, and what it does
instead), and **Nastier** (one step harder, for a table that wants it). Read the
Breaks line before you run the block. Most of these people would rather be anywhere
else, and most of them break long before the end.

**The palace's two mercies**, which several blocks refer to:

- **Knocked out, not killed.** A creature reduced to 0 Hit Points by an attack
  that detains (the honor guard, the sect guard, the Church Wardens; a Bought Blade on a
  contract to detain leaves a creature at 1 Hit Point with the Grappled condition
  instead, see its *To the Terms*) is **Unconscious and Stable** instead of dying. Before
  midnight it wakes in the gatehouse cell at the start of the next Movement, and
  somebody comes for it (see chapter IV, "The Palace on Alert"). After midnight, see
  chapter V, "Down, Not Out". Any attacker at this ball may also knock out rather than
  kill, as the SRD's Knocking Out a Creature, with any attack roll; never with an area
  effect (see chapter IX, "Running the Snakes").
- **Down, Not Out.** The Uninvited set most people aside instead of killing them. A
  creature one of them drops is thrown clear, and a companion or the crowd gets it up,
  unless it has made itself a threat to that one. The Attendant sets aside whoever it
  drops while it is Idle; Focused, it can kill. The rule is printed once, in chapter V
  ("Down, Not Out"). Every block below that uses it points there, and adds only what is
  its own.

**Standard numbers in the text.** DCs follow the ladder in chapter I: **Easy 10 ·
Standard 13–15 · Hard 18–20 · Very Hard 25.** "Half its Hit Points" means at or below
half its Hit Point maximum. A creature's armor is named on its Armor Class line; its
weapons are named in its Actions. "Until the scene ends" means until the fight ends or
the Movement changes, whichever comes first. At a 2024 table, read the box below first.

> **DM Note — at a 2024 table**
>

> - **The block lines.** Armor Class, Hit Points and Challenge are AC, HP and CR. The

## The Uninvited, Before You Read Their Blocks

The three gray masks are the storm, and a 5e table has spent years learning that
every storm has a spell that ends it. This one does not. Their blocks are full and
honest (Armor Class, Hit Points, attacks that would end most characters at this
table in one turn), so that a player who reads the numbers understands what they are
standing in front of. Then every door a clever table tries opens a little, and no
further.

The reason is in the fiction: something holds each of the three on
a leash that runs east. That leash holds their lives, minds and
shapes, and it pulls them home when their work is done or at the last bell, whichever
comes first. Say that at the table when a player reaches for the spell, and then say
what the spell bought. Most good ideas buy something, and what they buy is **Delay**
(see chapter V, "Buying Time"). Each point costs one of the three a turn of movement
toward their errand, and every point is spent on somebody's life.

**One rule covers most of the list.** Anything that would block, hide from or
wall off one of the Uninvited works for one round at most, and that round is **1
Delay**. Deep Boranis ward-crystal is the only thing that holds them longer (chapter
V, ⟨They trap one of the Uninvited⟩).

**What spells buy.** A spell that lands buys **1 Delay**, or **2** if it plays
to that one's nature, once per spell on each of them. A spell with an attack roll or a saving throw uses that roll, and *Centuries of Practice* can refuse the save; a spell with neither is a trick check with the caster's spellcasting ability, DC 13. Their condition
immunities stay. Whatever the spell would do to them ends at once (*Held by Something
Else*), and the Delay is all it buys. The obvious ones:

- **Damage, a lot of it.** Every round the party deals 30 or more damage to one of
  them (20 with three characters, 40 with five; see chapter V, "Adjusting the Attack"), that one gains
  1 Delay, never more than 1 a round. At 0 HP they step into the world's shadow and
  are back at full HP on their next turn, and whoever dealt the last blow makes a save
  or is thrown (*Leashed*).
- ***Power Word* anything, *Disintegrate*, a long fall, drowning.** They are not dead.
  The leash holds their lives (*Leashed*). A spell that lands still buys its Delay.
- ***Banishment*, *Plane Shift*, any teleport they didn't choose.** The leash pulls
  them back to where they stood, at once: 1 Delay.
- ***Hold Person*, *Sleep*, *Hideous Laughter*, *Command*, *Charm Person*,
  *Suggestion*, *Calm Emotions*, fear, *Polymorph*, any shape-change.** It ends at
  once, Prone and all: 1 Delay.
- **A grapple, *Web*, a net, a pin.** The Wept and the Hollow step into the world's
  shadow and out of the grip: 1 Delay if it landed. The Radiant can't while he is
  *Witnessed*: 1 Delay, and he spends his action tearing free.
- **A door, a wall, *Arcane Lock*, a barricade, *Tiny Hut*.** Ordinary barriers
  don't hold them. One put in the way at the right moment is a trick like any other:
  1 Delay, and on their next turn they open it, as they open anything set against
  them (a *Tiny Hut* buys this once tonight).
- ***Blindness/Deafness*.** It works until they save at the end of a turn. While they
  have the Blinded condition they can't Shadow-Step, because they must see where they
  arrive. 1 Delay.
- ***Sanctuary* on Raunu.** It is a ward rather than a compulsion, so it holds once: 1 Delay, and
  then they walk through it and the spell ends.
- ***Slow*, *Ray of Frost*, anything that cuts their Speed.** 1 Delay if it landed,
  and a whole turn at half Speed, or at 20 feet or less, is half a turn's progress
  (see chapter V, "Buying Time"). Nothing slows the Radiant below 20 feet.
- ***Darkness*, a *Dark-Burst*, doused lanterns.** Magical Darkness blinds even their
  Darkvision, so they can't Shadow-Step inside it. On the Radiant it also takes away
  his witnesses, so he moves faster. It still plays to his nature (2 Delay), and a
  table that means to deny him his congregation has earned a try at his Stop as well.
- **Steering a palace ward at them.** Each ward-point steers once tonight. Against
  one of the Uninvited a steered ward holds 1 round: 1 Delay (chapter V, principle 2).
- ***Counterspell*, *Dispel Magic*.** They cast no spells, and nothing on them is a
  spell. The leash is older than the word.
- **Turn them, frighten them, make them flee.** They are not undead and they are not
  afraid, and the Radiant above all cannot be turned (*Cannot Be Turned*). Fear ends
  at once: 1 Delay.

Tricks without a spell are chapter V's (see chapter V, "Buying Time"). Treat a good
idea that fails the way you treat a hard blow: say what the moment cost them, and what
it bought.

**What changes the night**, and it is the complete list: **Delay**, bought with the
room, the crowd, the wards and good ideas; each one's **Stop** and **End**, the person
still inside, reachable by someone who paid attention; **deep ward-crystal**, the one
thing that can hold one of them, and the Radiant's only End (the Root of the House is
the deepest of it, and they don't know to avoid it: see chapter V, ⟨They trap one of the
Uninvited⟩); and the **leash**, which takes all three east the moment their work is
finished or lost, and at the last bell of Oraga if nothing else has. Chapter V runs
the attack; these blocks are what it runs on. Their challenge ratings describe how
hard they hit. Nothing tonight stops them.

**Stop and End.** Each Uninvited has **Fracture tells**, human moments salted through Movements
III–V. Reaching the person inside with the right words lands a **Stop**, good for the
scene, and every tell a character has seen makes it easier. Two or more tells and the
one method that reaches that person land an **End**, good for the night. The full rule,
and the table of tells, is in chapter V (see chapter V, "The Fractures"). Each block's
last trait says what lands.

**And one more came with them.** The **Attendant** has been at the ball all night,
a cloak over one arm, waiting for a master nobody ever sees. At midnight it stops
pretending. It is not Leashed, it can be beaten, and it is the thing an Uninvited
turns on the party when the party gets in the way. Its block is the first in the
list below.

## The Blocks, A to Z

### The Attendant
*A quiet guest with no master.*

*Medium Construct, Unaligned*

**Armor Class** 17 (Natural Armor)
**Hit Points** 144 (17d8 + 68)
**Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 (+4) | 16 (+3) | 18 (+4) | 22 (+6) | 12 (+1) | 6 (−2) |

**Saving Throws** Con +7, Int +9
**Skills** Arcana +9, Perception +4
**Damage Immunities** Poison, Psychic
**Condition Immunities** Charmed, Exhaustion, Frightened, Poisoned
**Senses** Darkvision 120 ft.; Passive Perception 14
**Languages** Common; speaks only when spoken to
**Challenge** 6 (2,300 XP)

**Traits**

***Idle and Focused.*** The Attendant is **Idle** until the party becomes a real
interruption: they have earned Delay against one of the three, struck one, or stood
between one and their errand. From then on, at the start of each of its turns, it is
**Focused** if any one of the three in the scene has no Delay — one of them glances at
it, then at the party, and that is enough. A distraction breaks its focus (*Can Be
Distracted*), but only until its next turn. Delay on every one of the three in the
scene keeps it Idle. Say it out loud when it happens, and say its state every round
after that: "locked on you," or "drifting."

***Literal Orders.*** Its order is to keep the three from being interrupted. It acts
only against a creature that has attacked, hindered or interfered with one of the
Uninvited, or attacked the Attendant, since the start of its previous round. Everyone
else is furniture. A creature that stops interfering stops being its business.

***Rusty (Idle Only).*** It has spent centuries doing nothing. While Idle, its action
is one Joined Hands attack, with Disadvantage, instead of its Multiattack. It can use
Put Aside instead only if it is at or below half its Hit Points or its focus has been broken twice tonight.

***Can Be Distracted.*** A creature the Attendant can see or hear can take an action
to distract it: describe the trick, then make an ability check with whatever skill
fits it: **DC 13 while it is Idle, DC 18 while it is Focused**. It falls for **one
trick a round**: once anyone has tried, nobody else can until the next round. The Help
action applies. Playing the trick out in character adds +2, and using one of its
three habits the party has seen adds +2 (+4 at most). A trick already tried on it
while it was Focused gets no bonus and has Disadvantage; the same trick never works a
third time. A trick tried while it is Idle is not spent.
Its habits: it stares at worked crystal and light; it waits for a master who is not
there; it follows music that changes. A success against it while
it is Focused **breaks its focus** until the start of its next turn; on a success by 5 or
more it loses that turn as well. A success while it is Idle makes the distractor
**furniture** to it: it ignores that creature, as if it had never interfered, until that
creature next attacks it or interferes with one of the three. It goes on acting as before.
That success doesn't count toward the four, but a natural 20 while it is Idle costs it
its next turn. On a failure the trick is spent, and a Focused
Attendant attacks the distractor next. The **fourth** time its focus is broken
tonight, it wanders off: out of the fight, standing at a window, watching the fires.
That is a win. An argument that what the party is doing is not, strictly, interrupting
the three is one more trick, under these same rules. *(The full rule, the natural 20, and
how to hint at all of this: card S14.)*

***Clears the Way.*** At the start of a turn it hasn't lost, the Attendant can spend
that turn's action to remove 1 Delay from one Uninvited in the same scene (see chapter V,
"Buying Time"). Idle, it does so whenever nobody has attacked it since its last turn.
Focused, it attacks on its first turn and clears the way on its second; if none of the
three in the scene has Delay to take, the second turn attacks instead. A lost turn clears
nothing. It moves the obstacle: the barricade, the guest in the way, the door somebody
was made to go round.

***Removes Interruptions.*** A creature the Attendant reduces to 0 Hit Points while it
is Idle is set aside, Unconscious and Stable (see chapter V, "Down, Not Out"). While it is
Focused, the creature uses the ordinary rules for dying. A Focused Attendant
attacks a creature at 0 Hit Points only if that creature interfered since the
Attendant's last turn. It doesn't hunt.

***Where the Three Go.*** It stays in the scene of the first Uninvited the party
interrupts (card S14), and it follows its three anywhere, the Root included: nothing
there touches it.

***Not Here.*** At 0 Hit Points the Attendant doesn't fall. It loses interest in being
here, steps into the world's shadow, and is gone for the night. There is no body.

***Two Turns (Focused Only).*** While Focused, the Attendant takes two turns each
round: one on its Initiative and one at Initiative count 10 lower (last in the round,
if that count is below 1). On the second turn its action is to clear the way (*Clears
the Way*) or its full Multiattack (with three characters, one Joined Hands attack
instead; against a lone character, no attack at all; card S14). Focus is checked at the start of each of the two turns, so a focus broken
on its first turn lasts only until its second; if it is Idle when the second turn comes,
it takes that turn Idle (*Rusty*, or *Clears the Way*). It still has only one Reaction a
round, and it rolls Put Aside's recharge only at the start of its first turn each round.

**Actions**

***Multiattack.*** The Attendant makes two Joined Hands attacks. It can replace one
of them with Put Aside, if Put Aside is available.

***Joined Hands.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 9 (1d10 + 4)
Bludgeoning damage, and a Medium or smaller target is pushed 5 feet away.

***Put Aside (Recharge 5–6 While Focused; Recharge 6 While Idle).*** Each creature of
its choice within 20 feet of it must make a DC 15 Strength saving throw. On a failed
save, a creature takes 9 (2d8) Force damage, is pushed 15 feet straight away from it and
is knocked Prone. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and is neither
pushed nor knocked Prone.

**Before Midnight**

A quiet guest dressed as a great house's attendant, a cloak over one arm, waiting for a
master who never appears. It was sent to watch ahead of midnight and it is bad at
it. Its magic is real; it rarely cares to use it. It will not fight before the Unmasking: confronted or attacked, it walks into a
shadow and is gone for the rest of that Movement, and steel drawn on it is still steel drawn at the
ball (see chapter IV, "The Palace on Alert"). Asked whom it serves, it names nobody, and
looks round, briefly, for the master it is supposed to have.

> **Wants.** Nothing. It has an order, to keep the three from being interrupted, and
> if asked what the order is, it repeats it word for word.
> **Tells.** *Before midnight:* stands apart, staring, as if someone is expected; stops
> in front of a crystal wall and watches the light for a full minute;
> turns to follow the band when the tune changes; loses the people it is watching
> whenever something bright goes past. *At midnight:* sets the cloak down
> on the nearest table. In a fight, it goes still when one of the three
> looks at it, and then it comes for whoever that one was looking at.
> **Breaks.** Not from fear or pain. Its focus broken four times, or 0 Hit Points,
> and it is gone. An argument that what the party is doing is not, strictly, interrupting the
> three is a distraction like any other (*Can Be Distracted*): it breaks a focus, or makes
> the arguer furniture until they next interfere, and never ends the fight by itself.
> **Nastier.** It is already Focused when the party first draws its attention at
> midnight.
> *Made, not born, and in the service of the Uninvited's master; it came through with
> them tonight. What it is, for the DM alone: chapter VII. Card: S14. Chapter V.*
> *Its CR 6 is for the Focused Attendant: 2,300 XP, a little past Deadly for four
> 4th-level characters, on purpose (card S14). Award the full XP however the party gets
> it out of the way. With three characters it has 120 Hit Points (card S14).*

### Boranis Honor Guard
*Medium Humanoid (Human), Lawful Neutral*

**Armor Class** 18 (Chain Mail, Shield)
**Hit Points** 52 (8d8 + 16)
**Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 (+3) | 12 (+1) | 14 (+2) | 10 (+0) | 13 (+1) | 11 (+0) |

**Saving Throws** Str +5, Con +4
**Skills** Athletics +5, Perception +3
**Senses** Passive Perception 13
**Languages** Common
**Challenge** 2 (450 XP)

**Traits**

***Detain and Expel.*** The guard's attacks detain: a creature it reduces to 0
Hit Points is Unconscious and Stable, and wakes in the gatehouse cell without its
mask or its invitation.

***Ward-Points.*** The guard knows every ward-point in the palace by heart. Within
30 feet of one, it can't be surprised and has Advantage on Wisdom (Perception)
checks.

**Actions**

***Multiattack.*** The guard makes two House Blade attacks, or one House Blade
attack and uses Seize.

***House Blade.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 7 (1d8 + 3) Slashing
damage.

***Seize.*** One Medium or smaller creature within 5 feet of the guard must succeed on a
DC 13 Strength saving throw or be Grappled (escape DC 13). The guard grapples with its
shield arm, keeping its blade in hand.

***Warder (1/Day Each).*** The guard carries two charged house crystals, and
releases one in place of one House Blade attack:
- *Seal.* One door within 30 feet swings shut and grows closed. Until the scene
  ends it is locked; forcing it takes a DC 20 Strength (Athletics) check or a key
  from Corval.
- *Flare.* Bright Light fills a 30-foot radius around the crystal until the end of
  the guard's next turn. Nothing in it can benefit from the Invisible condition or
  take the Hide action, and every face in it is seen and remembered.

**Reactions**

***Call the House.*** When the guard drops to half its Hit Points or fewer, and no guard
has called the house this scene, it moves up to half its Speed toward the nearest
ward-point without provoking Opportunity Attacks and calls out. Four more Boranis Honor
Guards arrive at the start of the second round after this one, and this guard holds its
ground until they come.

> **Wants.** The room held and the offender out of it: detained and expelled, never
> killed.
> **Tells.** Glances at the nearest ward-point before moving; touches the crystal at

## Items of the Night

### Crystal Charges

Orthaen crystal holds finished workings, and a **charge** is one of them: a
consumable magic item, one stored working, released at a touch by anyone, gifted or
not, trained or not. Releasing one does not make its bearer a caster.

- **Releasing a charge** takes an action (Use an Object), and no
  check. The charge is spent. Charges need no attunement.
- **The six every Orthaen knows** are common: minor, local, brief. A gifted Orthaen
  with the Orthaen Gift can grow one in a day of downtime from 25 GP of raw crystal,
  one at a time, and no more than one a week: a gift is not a mint (see chapter III).
  Price: 50 GP, which is a season's wages in the wrong district.
- **The house and the trade grow larger ones.** The four below the common list are
  uncommon, and nobody at the ball grew them with a gift alone. Price: 200 GP, when
  they can be bought at all.
- **Chancy releases.** When the fiction makes a release uncertain (fumbled in the
  dark, jostled in a crowd), the bearer makes a DC 13 Charisma check. On a failure,
  the action is spent and the charge is not.
- **Near the Uninvited** (within 30 feet of any of the three), every release needs
  that DC 13 Charisma check, and a failure means the light goes out of the crystal and
  the charge is spent. A charge that is already running (a *Steady Light* carried in,
  a door already sealed) keeps running. Only the release is at risk. Foreshadow this
  the moment a player's clever crystal plan meets it.

**Table X–3: Crystal Charges**

*All are consumable Wondrous Items; none requires attunement.*

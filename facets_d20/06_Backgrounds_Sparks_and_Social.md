# Backgrounds, Sparks and Social Play

Your Facet says what you can do. This chapter is about who you are: where you came from, what you want, and how you get along with the people you meet. None of it is decoration. A background gives you real proficiencies, your Specialty gives you advantage, your Drives earn you Sparks, and a conversation can win what a sword can't.

---

## Backgrounds

Your **background** is the life you led before the story started. It uses the SRD 5.2.1 shape, with one change: the SRD's origin feat is replaced by an **origin talent**, and a Specialty is added.

A background gives you:

- **Ability scores.** Three abilities are listed. Increase one by 2 and another by 1, or all three by 1. No score goes above 20.
- **Two skill proficiencies.** If your Facet already gave you one of these, pick any other skill instead.
- **One tool proficiency.**
- **One origin talent.** Any tier 1 talent marked *origin* in any of the three Facet chapters (03, 04 and 05). It doesn't use up one of your talent picks, and it doesn't have to come from your own Facet.
- **A Specialty.** See below.
- **Equipment.** The background's tool and 25 gp.

Any character can take any background. The eight below are examples, not a list you must choose from; writing your own is encouraged, and the steps come after them.

> **Reading the Entries — backgrounds**
>
> Each background has a **name** and a short **history**, then its **abilities**, **skills** and **tool**, a suggested **origin talent**, and an example **Specialty**. The talents that can be origin talents are *Alert*, *Cunning Action*, *Expertise* and *Tough* (Body); *Expertise*, *Field Medic*, *Gadgeteer*, *Magic Initiate* and *Skilled* (Mind); *Magic Initiate*, *Silver Tongue* and *Wild Kin* (Soul). The suggestion and the Specialty are starting points. Change them to fit your character.

### City Watch Veteran

You walked a beat for years: street patrols, crowd control, and the kind of violence that happens at close range in narrow alleys. You learned to read a situation before it turned.

**Abilities:** Strength, Constitution, Wisdom
**Skills:** Athletics, Insight
**Tool:** Gaming set (dice)
**Origin talent:** *Alert* (Chapter 03)
**Specialty:** Knows the layout, the regulars and the unofficial rules of one district you patrolled.

### Wilderness Scout

You went ahead of other people into country nobody had mapped yet, and came back to tell them what was there. You sleep lightly, and you notice broken twigs.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Constitution, Wisdom
**Skills:** Survival, Stealth
**Tool:** Cartographer's tools
**Origin talent:** *Wild Kin* (Chapter 05)
**Specialty:** Can follow a person or animal's trail across ground they crossed within the last day.

### Dockworker

You loaded and unloaded ships for a living. You know rope and weight and tide tables, and you know which crates are lighter than their paperwork says.

**Abilities:** Strength, Dexterity, Constitution
**Skills:** Athletics, Investigation
**Tool:** Vehicles (water)
**Origin talent:** *Tough* (Chapter 03)
**Specialty:** Knows how cargo manifests work, how they get falsified, and what a suspicious shipment looks like on paper and on the dock.

### Guild Apprentice

You were bound to a guild or learned institution and spent years copying, fetching and being corrected. You learned its methods before you understood them, and you understand them now.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom
**Skills:** Arcana, Investigation
**Tool:** Calligrapher's supplies
**Origin talent:** *Gadgeteer* (Chapter 04)
**Specialty:** Knows the vocabulary, record-keeping and people of the guild that trained you, well enough to read its documents and recognize its work.

### Physician's Assistant

You held the basin, boiled the linen and watched a physician work. You have seen most of the ways a body goes wrong, and you are hard to shock.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom
**Skills:** Medicine, Insight
**Tool:** Herbalism kit
**Origin talent:** *Field Medic* (Chapter 04)
**Specialty:** Recognizes the common diseases, poisons and injuries, and can say how each will progress.

### Temple Acolyte

You served in a house of worship: lighting lamps, learning rites by heart, listening to people who came in carrying something heavy. Whether you still believe is your business.

**Abilities:** Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma
**Skills:** Religion, Insight
**Tool:** Calligrapher's supplies
**Origin talent:** *Magic Initiate* (Chapter 04 or 05)
**Specialty:** Knows the rites, symbols and internal politics of one religious tradition in detail.

### Street Performer

You worked crowds for coins: songs, tricks, tumbling, whatever kept them from walking on. You learned to read an audience in a heartbeat, because the ones who couldn't went hungry.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma
**Skills:** Performance, Acrobatics
**Tool:** Musical instrument (one of your choice)
**Origin talent:** *Silver Tongue* (Chapter 05)
**Specialty:** Can judge a crowd's mood, and the best way to approach it, within a few moments of watching.

### Traveling Merchant

You bought in one town and sold in the next. You have haggled in markets where you didn't speak the language, and walked away from deals that smelled wrong.

**Abilities:** Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma
**Skills:** Persuasion, Insight
**Tool:** Vehicles (land)
**Origin talent:** *Skilled* (Chapter 04)
**Specialty:** Can tell the fair price of common goods, and whether an offer means opportunity, desperation or a trap.

### Writing Your Own Background

1. **Name it.** What you call yourself, or what others call you.
2. **Write the history.** Two or three sentences. The MM will read it, so write for them.
3. **Pick three abilities** the history would have exercised.
4. **Pick two skills and a tool** from the SRD 5.2.1 lists.
5. **Pick an origin talent.** Any origin talent from any Facet.
6. **Write the Specialty.** One narrow thing (see below).

Zulnut's background is his own. He calls it **Wandering Disciple**: a teacher who moved on, and the habits that stayed. Dexterity, Wisdom and Constitution; Acrobatics and Sleight of Hand; a gaming set. His Specialty is noticing the exact moment a room's attention shifts off him.

## Specialties

A **Specialty** is one narrow area of expertise, specific enough that the MM knows exactly when it applies. You have one, from your background.

**When your Specialty applies to a check, you have advantage on it.** And inside your Specialty, routine and informational things just happen: you know the answer, you read the document correctly, you recognize the symptom. The MM only calls for a roll when there is real risk or opposition, and then you roll with advantage.

"Good with people" is not a Specialty. "Can read a crowd's mood within a few moments" is. If you can't picture the moment it would come up, it is too broad. If you can't picture it coming up twice, it is too narrow.

A Specialty is not a skill. It can apply to a History check, an Insight check or a Persuasion check, whatever fits the moment. It's the reason a former watchman gets advantage on the Intimidation check in his old district and nowhere else.

> **Example — a Specialty doing its job**
>
> **MM:** You're back in the district you walked for eleven years. The tavern on the corner has a new sign over the door.
>
> **Mordai:** Who owns it now? And where does the old owner drink?
>
> **MM:** That's your Specialty, so no roll. You know both. The old owner drinks here still, at the back table, because the new one owes him money and he likes to watch it being earned.
>
> **Mordai:** Then I sit at the back table.
>
> **MM:** (Of course you do.)

## Drives

Every character has two **Drives**, each one short sentence:

- **A want.** Something your character is reaching for. *I want to see my name on a guild charter. I want the people who burned my village to answer for it.*
- **A line.** Something your character will not do. *I will not leave a fight while someone weaker is still in it. I will not lie to a priest.*

Drives are for the MM as much as for you. They tell the MM what to put in your path. A good Drive can be tested: the MM can build a scene where it costs you something.

You can rewrite one Drive at the end of any session, if your character has changed. That's allowed, and it's usually a good sign.

## Sparks

**Sparks** replace Heroic Inspiration. They are the currency of playing your character well.

**Table 6–1: Sparks**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Start** | You begin every session with 1 Spark. Unspent Sparks don't carry over. |
| **Maximum** | 3 at a time. |
| **Earn one** | When one of your Drives costs you something. When you roll a natural 1 and narrate the complication with flair. When the MM calls a moment great. |
| **Spend one** | Before you roll: gain advantage. After you roll: reroll the d20 and take the new result. On an ally's roll: describe how you help, and add 1d6 to it. |

A few details:

- **One Spark per roll.** You can't spend two on the same roll. Two players can each spend one on the same roll, as long as they each describe how.
- **Some talents grant Sparks too.** They say so, and the cap of 3 still holds.
- **Helping with a Spark costs no action.** You spend it after your ally rolls and before the MM says what happens. Your character has to be in a position to help, and the description has to make sense.
- **A Drive has to cost you.** Mordai refusing to leave a fight earns a Spark when staying gets him hurt, or lets the villain get away. Refusing to leave a fight he was winning anyway earns nothing.
- **The natural 1 is yours to narrate.** You still fail. But you tell the table how, and if your version makes things interesting (the rope snaps, the lie comes out in the wrong accent, the horse takes it personally), the MM hands you a Spark.

> **Through the Mirror — why Sparks reset**
>
> An earlier playtest of this game let Sparks pile up, and the players did what players do: they saved them for a crisis that never came. Starting every session at 1 and capping at 3 means a Spark in your hand is a Spark you should be looking to spend.

## Social Play

Talking your way through a scene uses the same d20 as everything else. It's a whole third of the game, and the rules for it fit on one page.

### Attitude

Every character the MM plays has an **attitude** toward the party. It sits somewhere on this track:

**Table 6–2: The Attitude Track**

| Attitude | What they will do for you |
|---|---|
| **Hostile** | Work against you, openly or not. Won't help. |
| **Wary** | Talk, but give nothing that costs them anything. |
| **Neutral** | Help in small ways, when it's no risk to them. |
| **Friendly** | Help at some cost or risk to themselves. |
| **Ally** | Take real risks for you. |

Anything inside what their attitude allows, you get by asking. No roll. The roll is for moving the attitude.

### Making Your Case

When you want someone to move along the track, say what you're doing and why it should work. The MM calls for a check that fits: usually Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation or Performance, sometimes Insight or even History if the argument depends on it. In combat this is the Influence action.

The MM sets the DC by how hard this person is to move: **10** for someone with no real reason to refuse, **15** for someone with reasons, **20** for someone with good ones.

**Table 6–3: Social Results**

| Result | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Beat the DC by 10 or more | Moves **two** steps toward Ally. |
| Meet or beat the DC | Moves **one** step toward Ally. |
| Miss by 1 to 4 | No change. That argument won't work again this scene. |
| Miss by 5 or more | Moves **one** step toward Hostile. |

**Advantage.** You have advantage when you have **leverage** (something they want, something they fear, a debt they owe) or when your **Specialty** fits. Having both is still just advantage.

**What doesn't move.** Nobody moves past what makes sense for them. A guard captain who would be hanged for opening the gate won't open it at Friendly; he might at Ally, or he might tell you when the shift changes. The MM will tell you when you're asking for something no roll can get.

There are no social hit points and no way to talk someone into anything with enough dice. A person is moved by what you offer them, and the check is how well you offered it.

## Exploration and Travel

Exploration uses the SRD 5.2.1 rules as written: travel pace, marching order, light and vision, hazards, and foraging. Two changes of emphasis:

**Travel is a few scenes, not a march.** The MM frames a journey as the moments that matter: the river that needs crossing, the night the watch hears something, the arrival. Somebody navigates (a Survival check, once for each leg of the journey), and somebody keeps watch.

**Resting is the SRD rule.** A short rest is an hour; a long rest is eight. You can spend Hit Point Dice on a short rest and recover everything on a long one, as the SRD says. Spells and talents that recharge on a rest say which one.

# BRIEF — Oraga Night as the Starter Module, Lineages, and Val'loh Magic

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Tier:** Brain (Fable)
**Owner direction (verbatim, this session):** *"Val'loh has magic, so has domains. If you think it makes sense for them to be custom domains, that's fine, but they should have domains. The prior models have made some judgements I am not married to. The Tribes are Human, but that's all that I'm married to here. Lineages make sense to me, and I think were a suggestion of mine, and implementing them sounds like a good idea. As for Oraga, I'm not a big fan of Night one, I like the idea of it starting on the actual night of the ball, at the earliest mingling outside. You also have freedom to rewrite things as needed to add tension for combat. … feel free to propose rewriting things to make it a good starter module."*

**Reads:** `docs/RESEARCH_fun_audit_2026-09.md` §8 (why the module is a poor system test), `style/analysis/adventures.md` (the 3.5-era module patterns, concepts only), `references/oraga_night/WORKING_NOTES.md` and `TUXXE_NAVAA_CANON.md` (canon and rulings), `/root/wanvil/docs/systems.md` §Magic and §Mage Tribe Magic (Svara canon), the whole of `adventures/oraga_night/`, `enemies/bought_*.fof`.

## 1. Problem

Oraga Night is the game's first module and its best piece of adventure writing, and it does not show the game. It swaps the magic system for a mini-Facet, makes every fight opt-in or unwinnable, hands out no Technique and no advancement, and opens with a prelude night the owner does not like. A new table that starts here learns the core roll, Sparks, and Specialties, and learns nothing about exchanges, domains, or growth. It also carries a design decision no one is married to (Val'loh has no domains) that contradicts what the setting canon actually says.

Underneath the module sit two core-rules gaps the module exposed: the game has no Lineage step, so the tribes had nowhere to live and were bolted onto Backgrounds; and the game has no stated way to express an innate, inherited magic, so the tribal gifts were written as "flair" instead of as what they are in canon, which is magic.

This BRIEF sets direction for three connected pieces of work: **A.** Lineage as a core creation step. **B.** Val'loh magic expressed in the core system. **C.** Oraga Night rebuilt as the starter module. A and B are prerequisites for C and are small; C is the large piece.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
1. A new table can run Oraga Night as its first session and come out having used every flagship mechanic once: an exchange with postures and reactions, a Named enemy with a stance and a Technique, a Boss whose phase they see, a domain cast at Minor scope, a Spark spent and one earned, and a Technique unlocked by the end of the night or the short campaign.
2. The module opens on the night of the ball, outside the gates, and needs no prelude.
3. Combat is opt-in in tone (nobody is forced to draw) and *inviting* in structure: three visible, escalating fights that a combat-hungry table will walk into, each with an objective and a clock, none to the death.
4. The tribes of Val'loh are human Lineages built in core terms; their gifts are domains; nothing in the module is an exception to the core rules.
5. The module reads like a published module: the format legend, keyed areas, scene cards, a visible villain clock, and paired branches that `style/analysis/adventures.md` describes.

**Non-goals**
- Changing the canonical outcome (Raunu dies, Veier and the child escape, no one is charged) or the operational-truth spoiler boundary. Both rulings from 2026-07-12 stand.
- Making the Uninvited beatable. "Force buys time, Fractures change outcomes" is the module's spine and stays.
- Rebuilding the core magic system. Val'loh is served by *adding* domains and one setting rule, not by touching II.3.
- Writing the Shattered Origin lineage list. Lineage as a *step* is core; the human default is core; every non-default lineage belongs to a setting module. Val'loh's tribes are the first such module.
- Reading the proprietary 3.5 modules directly. The distilled patterns in `style/analysis/adventures.md` are the copyright-safe route and are sufficient.

## 3. Canon this BRIEF stands on

From `/root/wanvil/docs/systems.md` (Canonical section):
- Two systems of magic. **Traditional magic** is spellforms drawn thread by thread; useful workings take hours to weeks, and constructing one under stress "causes severe mental strain, risking focus loss and ruinous spell collapse."
- **Mage tribe gifts** are "innate, inherited abilities specific to each tribe — distinct from and independent of traditional magic. A gifted member of a mage tribe can use both."
- **Orthain** grow crystals that hold spellforms; **Thenya** intervene when a bonded loved one faces death; **Phern** heightened perception and danger sense; **Akathi** weapon bond; **Kshalo** dream-walking; **Vethala/Fthala** animal communion; **Scora** knowledge retention and pattern recognition; **Dekhi** physical resilience; **Krenn** telepathy; **Tyndi** stone and metal resonance. Gift rates as given there.

From the owner this session: the tribes are human. From 2026-07-12: weapons culture, the three Uninvited and their bound emotions, Navaa's abilities and sword, PCs are Orthaen or rarely Phern.

**What the module currently asserts that is *not* canon and is now retired:** "There are no magic domains in Val'loh," "nobody casts in a crisis, ever," and "gifts are flair, not magic." Those were a prior model's reading of the slow-spellform rule. The rule is real; the conclusion was wrong. Gifts are the fast magic of Val'loh, and in this game fast magic is a domain.

## 4. Workstream A — Lineage as a core creation step

**Decision.** Add **Lineage** to the six steps of character creation as a new step between the Primary Facet and the Background (step 3 of seven; attributes and derivation shift down). The core handbook ships exactly one lineage, **Human**, with no gift and no mechanical content beyond a line of prose, and a page of MM guidance for building custom lineages. Setting modules supply the rest.

**What a Lineage is.** Who you were born as, orthogonal to what you did (the Background). Every lineage entry prints the same four fields:

- **Name** and the common in-world variants.
- **Description:** two or three sentences.
- **Gift:** either *None* or a **domain**. A gifted lineage names one domain (or a short list to choose from) that the character holds from creation. It is a domain in every respect: intuitive tradition, Spirit + Attune, Minor scope until formalized, full scope after. It **replaces the Background's secondary skill**, exactly as a magic-granting Background's domain origin does, and a character holds one domain at creation from either source, never both.
- **Heritage:** one narrow fact about the lineage that works like a Specialty (Standard → Easy when it bears directly; information without a roll when tangential) and draws from the same one-step allowance. An ungifted member of a gifted lineage keeps the Heritage; it is what "being Orthaen" means when the crystals never came.

**Why a domain and not a new mechanic.** The audit's central finding is that this game's fun is carried by a few strong mechanics and dies when a subsystem is bolted on beside them. Domain + Intent + Scope is the strongest thing in the book for creative play, the engine already stores domains, formalization already has a Technique, the app already rolls them, and the Bestiary already resists them (crystals guttering near the Uninvited is a domain interaction, not a special case). A gift-as-domain is a *new domain entry*, not a new rule.

**The one rule Planner must settle: how a lineage gift formalizes.** Domains formalize at Facet level 1 through the Soul or Mind Tier 1 Technique. A Body-Facet Orthaen with the crystal gift would need to cross-train to reach full scope, which for a Dassa-shaped character may be never. Two options; Brain recommends the first:
1. **A lineage gift formalizes at the character's first Facet level, in any Facet, without spending the Technique pick.** Blood is not study. The Technique economy is untouched because no pick is consumed; the formalization is simply the gift arriving. Cost: one sentence in II.4 and a flag on the domain entry (`formalizes_on: first_facet_level`).
2. Lineage gifts follow the standard rule and Body characters stay at Minor scope until they cross-train. Simplest, and it makes "one in five Orthaen are ungifted" mechanically identical to "gifted but Body," which the fiction does not support.

**Engine and data.** `facet.yaml` gains a `lineages` collection (merged by id like backgrounds); `LineageDefinition` mirrors `BackgroundDefinition` with `gift_domains: [ids]`, `heritage: str`; character files gain `lineage:`; creation validation enforces one creation domain. The Quick Start and II.1 gain one line. Tests per the TDD rule.

## 5. Workstream B — Val'loh magic in core terms

**Decision.** Val'loh's setting module (`facets/valloh/`) adds domains, a lineage list, and **one** setting rule. Nothing else about magic changes.

**B1. The gifts are custom Focused domains.** One per tribe, intuitive tradition, entered in the same format as the appendix catalog (territory, beyond-the-focus, three example intents per scope). Proposed territories, each one line, each **needing the owner's read** because the example intents are new invention:

| Lineage | Domain (working name) | Territory in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Orthaen | **Crystal** | Growing, shaping, charging, and reading soul-crystal; holding a spellform in a lattice |
| Thenya | **The Bond** | Protective intervention for someone the caster loves, triggered by feeling; the rarest gift, and the one that fires without being asked |
| Phern | **Warning** | Sensing hidden danger, direction, and intent before it lands |
| Akathi | **Blade-bond** | One bonded weapon: finding it, calling it, striking with it as a part of the self |
| Dekhi | **Stone-flesh** | Enduring cold, poison, hunger, and blows that would drop another |
| Kshalo | **Dream** | Entering and shaping the dreams of the sleeping |
| Fthala | **Wildspeech** | Speaking with, bonding with, and moving among animals and wild places |
| Scora | **The Weave** | Perfect recall, pattern across memory, one true question of the past |
| Krenn | **Mindshare** | Telepathy among kin; reading and, at scope, steering other minds |
| Tyndi | **Resonance of Stone** | Metals and stones that carry and release magical power |

Krenn and Tyndi are MM-side in 3164 (one Krenn is at the ball, and he is not a player option). Scope calibration follows the core table: a Phern sensing that this corridor is wrong is Minor; a Kshalo walking a named stranger's dream is Significant; an Orthaen sealing a corridor with a lattice grown in seconds is Major and, for a Focused domain, Hard.

**B2. Traditional spellforms are the scholarly tradition, with one setting rule.** A Val'loh character who studies spellcraft casts with Knowledge + Lore from the Mind list of domains as normal. The setting rule: **outside a crystal, a Significant working takes a scene and a Major working takes days; a spellform held in an Orthaen crystal releases at Minor speed at the scope it was grown to.** This is canon's time constraint stated as a difficulty of *tempo* rather than a ban, and it is why crystals are wealth. It is one paragraph and one flag on the module.

**B3. Crystals are consumables.** A charged Orthaen crystal is a one-use item: a stored working, declared when grown, released at a touch, resolved as an ordinary roll only when the fiction makes it chancy. This is exactly the audit's R8 (rotating one-use items, no permanent bonuses) and the module already runs on it. A gifted Orthaen begins with a few; charging more is downtime. Val'loh becomes the first module with loot, and the loot is the setting's magic.

**B4. Nothing is "flair."** The Chapter VIII line "gifts are mostly flair" is retired. A gift is a domain; the difference between a Phern's danger-sense and a Soul mage's Shadow is territory, not weight.

## 6. Workstream C — Oraga Night rebuilt as the starter module

### 6.1 Shape

**Cut the prelude wing.** The module opens on the last night of Oraga, on the approach to the palace, in the line. The hooks (invited, entourage, wrong name, hired, errand, the wrong place deliberately) become the module's five to six *named, converging hooks* in the introduction, each ending "begin at the Gatehouse Court, Movement I." Everything Night One and Two did that mattered is folded in: the discarded-invitation trade is a hook, the extra swords every house hired are a fact stated in the introduction, Vell's preparations are seen only at the ball, the mask-maker becomes a single line for the aftermath (her casting-blanks still prove the gray masks came from nowhere).

**Keep the seven Movements**, but Movement I is now the module's first *scene* and not a transition: the line, the gossip, the first omen, the first opt-in trouble.

**Keep the aftermath wing** as the campaign extension; it is where a Technique arrives (6.5).

### 6.2 The combat arc

Three fights, escalating on the MM2 three-encounter template (Skirmish → Standard → Hard), all visible, all opt-in, all with an objective and a clock, none lethal by default. Each is a one-page scene card at the end of its chapter with a pointer line both ways (the Stage-3 encounter format from the module analysis). Each teaches one thing.

| Movement | Fight | Recipe | Objective and clock | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **II** | **The seating feud** boils over in the galleries. Kinsmen, drunk and masked | Skirmish: 4–6 Mooks, Tier 1 bruising; bare steel summons guards | Pull the two principals apart before the four-segment clock fills; full = guards flood the galleries for a Movement and one agenda door closes | Postures, Tier 1 Conditions clearing per exchange, Absorb as a choice, no steel |
| **V** | **Knives in the dark.** Tavva's crew staging in the service corridors during the Dead Dance | Standard: Tavva (Named, Vanisher, stance triggers) + 2–3 gallery knives; both sides hushed | Stop the staging before they reach the gallery; a shared noise clock: any 6−, or any bare steel, advances it, full = guards, and *both* sides lose | A Named enemy with a stated stance and a Technique; Press; Sparks; morale |
| **VII** | **The gate.** See 6.3 | Hard: 1 Named + 4 Mooks, a Boss if the table is strong | Open the way out for two hundred guests before the fire clock fills; or let the sect guard in | Intercept, a Boss phase the party sees, negotiation as a way to end a fight |

Two smaller fights remain as sidebars, unchanged: the honor guard's detain-and-expel at the east wing (Movement V) and the gallery looters (Movement VII), which becomes a *branch* of the gate scene rather than its own set piece.

**Why the seating feud is the opener.** Nobody dies, nobody draws, and a table that has never played sees the exchange structure with nothing at stake but dignity. The style analysis calls this the tutorial encounter; MM2 already says Skirmishes are tutorials.

### 6.3 The gate at midnight *(needs the owner's ruling: new canon)*

The module already says the sect guards took "one long hour" to fight their way into the palace, and it already says fires were set in two districts and a courier post went dark as diversions "by the same answer as everything else tonight." Three Uninvited on a leash did not set fires across a city. Someone was paid to.

Proposal: **the Bought** (Bestiary `B2_Folk`: blades, a sergeant, a captain, already carrying contract-boundary conduct, a "Hold the Terms" Technique, a "Second Clause" that changes the objective mid-fight, and a negotiation surface) were contracted by a factor they never met, paid half up front, to set the fires at the quarter-bells and then **hold the gatehouse from the first bell to the last**: nobody out, nobody in. Their contract boundary is the gate. Their Second Clause, which only the captain has read, is *"if a woman in Thenya wool comes out the front, hold her."* They do not know who they work for or why. They are not monsters. They are the night's one beatable, talkable, negotiable antagonist, and they are why the honor guard faces inward.

This gives Movement VII a fight with every lever the system has: a Named sergeant naming terms aloud, a Boss captain who never goes Aggressive and who *starts negotiating mid-exchange at Resolve 3* (that is the phase change the party will see), Mooks who disengage in order when two of four are down, a fire clock, an objective (the way out), and three endings: fight through, void the contract (proof the employer has broken terms; a bought-out fee; the named target already gone through the river gate), or the sect guard arriving on the clock's last segment. It also seeds the aftermath: a captured sergeant with a contract case is the inquest's best evidence and the only mortal thread that leads east.

If the owner would rather not add a mortal faction, the fallback is the gallery looters as the Hard fight with the ward-fire clock, which is weaker (one Named, fights to leave) but needs no new canon.

### 6.4 The villain clock and the palace on alert

Two apparatus pieces from the module analysis that the current draft lacks:

- **A one-page night clock** with the Uninvited's own timeline (arrive with the Movement III crush; wait; strike mid-sentence; the Wept to the dais, the Radiant to the east wing, the Hollow to the doors; the leash at the last bell) and **indented conditional lines that are the mission menu**: *Delayed one beat if the Wept's Fracture is found before midnight. Delayed one hallway per witness on the Radiant. The Hollow leaves early if…* The night-tracker already holds the program; this adds the "what the players can move" column and prints it where the MM looks.
- **An alert-state paragraph for the palace**, written once above the keyed rooms: what the honor guard does when steel is bared, when the east wing is forced, when the lights die; what the Bought do at each bell. Room entries then say "if alerted" and stop.

### 6.5 What the characters carry out

- **Session Sparks flow as written**, and the module's printed rewards stay.
- **Advancement happens.** The one-shot ends with the standard 4 skill points and a reflection scene at the epilogue. The aftermath wing, run as written for two sessions, lands Facet level 1 for a starting character under D16a's 2–4 session floor: the module says so, and coaches the MM to place the reflection scene at the inquest.
- **Pregens** are rebuilt: five, one per agenda archetype as now, with **two at Endurance Pool 4–5** (the house-blade and one more), **one gifted caster whose domain is used at Minor scope in the module's own examples** (Serane's Crystal, Pello's Warning), and each pregen's sheet listing the Technique they would take at Facet level 1 so a table sees the road ahead.
- **Crystals as consumables** are the module's loot: a few per gifted Orthaen at the start, one or two findable in the palace, all of them guttering near the Uninvited.

### 6.6 What stays exactly as it is

The agendas, the omens, the undercurrents, the cast, the Fractures and the tell-table, the read-aloud discipline, the safety text, the ⟨If History Breaks⟩ sidebars, the Crossing, the epilogue question. These are the module's quality and the reason it is the starter module rather than a replacement.

### 6.7 Module apparatus to add (from the style guide's checklist)

Format legend in the Overture; a synopsis paragraph per Movement that ends with the world-state; the Q&A dialogue format for Corval, Vorlain, and Raunu's summons (italic question, quoted in-voice answer, "if friendly" tier); troubleshooting sidebars for the three forecast MM errors the invalid playtests still managed to surface (defaulting to Hard, reading a 7–9 as a penalty, reverting to turn order) plus "the table will not stop hitting the Wept" with two in-fiction answers; one signed designer's note per part; an abridged-run box ("four hours: cut Undercurrent A and the east-wing scene").

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **Keep the mini-Facet and just add domains to it.** Rejected: it leaves the tribes as Backgrounds, which collide with professions, and leaves "gifts are flair" standing.
- **Make gifts Techniques instead of domains.** Rejected: a Technique is earned, a gift is born; and it would consume a Technique pick from a three-pick career.
- **Make the Uninvited beatable to get a Boss fight.** Rejected by the 2026-07-12 ruling and by the design: the Bought supply the Boss without touching the spine.
- **Keep the prelude as optional.** Rejected by the owner; its content survives as hooks.
- **Invent a new antagonist faction for Movement VII.** Rejected in favour of the Bestiary's Bought, who exist, are setting-agnostic by design, and already carry the conduct and negotiation surface the scene needs.

## 8. Downstream robustness

- Lineage adds one merged collection and one character field; every existing character file is valid with `lineage: human` defaulted. No rules text in III.x changes.
- Gift domains are catalog entries; the engine's domain code paths are unchanged. The only new engine behaviour is the formalization rule in §4 if option 1 is chosen.
- The three fights are Recipe-Table shapes and can be simulated before the text is written; the gate scene should be, because a Boss captain who negotiates at Resolve 3 needs its Resolve set so the phase lands in exchange 2 of 3, not the last.
- Everything new is opt-in at the table, so the one-shot's running time does not grow; the abridged box protects the four-hour promise.

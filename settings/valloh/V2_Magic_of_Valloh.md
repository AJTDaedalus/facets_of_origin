# V2. The Magic of Val'loh

Val'loh has the same magic as the rest of the game. Same 2d6, same Domain plus Intent plus Scope, same difficulty by scope, same Sparks, same readied intents for Significant and Major workings. There is no Val'loh-only slot, count, or tempo rule. If you know how to cast in Facets of Origin, you know how to cast here.

What differs is only **where a domain comes from**, and there are two answers.

---

## Gifts

A Gift is a domain carried in the blood (Chapter II.5), and **you choose which domain it is.** Any Soul or Mind domain in the core catalog that is not Prismatic. Your lineage does not pick it; your lineage says how it *shows itself*.

So an Orthaen's gift always comes through grown crystal — but one Orthaen's crystal burns (Fire), another's holds a working for later (Inscription), and a third's grows walls across a corridor (Warding). Two Orthaen at the same table can be as different as a Soul mage and a Mind mage. A Phern's gift is always a prickle before danger; what that prickle is *made of* is up to the player.

Three things are the same for every gift, whatever you choose:

**It is cast intuitively** — Spirit, plus your Attune rank — even when the domain you chose is one scholars reach through Knowledge and Lore. Blood is not study.

**It is Minor scope until it formalizes**, which happens at your first Facet level in any Facet and costs no Technique pick.

**It is the same domain everyone else uses.** A Soul mage in Shattered Origin can learn the domain your gift is. The difference between you is where it came from and how it looks, never what it can do.

## Spellcraft

The other answer is study, and it works as Chapter II.3 already writes it: the scholarly tradition, Knowledge and Lore, from the Mind list of domains, at the standard cross-Facet cost for anyone whose Facet is not Mind.

The fiction of Val'loh says spellcraft is a patient art. A spellform is drawn thread by thread; the useful ones take a scholar hours, and the great ones take seasons; the histories are full of workings that collapsed because someone tried to finish one in a hurry. Every word of that is true in the world.

**None of it is a rule.** A spellform caster in play rolls what any caster rolls, as often as the fiction allows, at the difficulty their scope sets. The patience is where the fiction lives — in what a character has spent their life on, in why the Church guards its scholars, in why nobody in a burning hall is drawing threads — and the difficulty ladder is where the game lives.

A gifted member of a tribe may learn spellcraft as anyone may. Nothing about the blood interferes with the study, and several of the more irritating figures in Val'loh's history have had both.

---

## Crystal

Orthaen crystal holds finished workings. This is the fact the whole economy of Val'loh rests on, and it is why the walls of Rekuzan are wealth and not decoration.

In play, a **charge** is a one-use item. One stored working, at one scope, released at a touch by anyone — gifted or not, trained or not. There is no roll.

That last part is the point. A charged crystal is magic that the ungifted can carry and spend, which is the only route to a working most people in Val'loh will ever have, and it is why a small pink splinter is worth a season's wages in the wrong district.

**Growing one** is an ordinary working in the grower's own gift, done in downtime, under the normal rules of Chapter II.3 — the working is whatever the charge will hold, and the crystal is how an Orthaen's gift shows itself. Nothing extra is tracked: the character makes the roll their scope calls for, and if it lands they have a charge. Growing a Significant or Major charge spends a readied intent like any working of that size.

**Releasing one is not a working**, so it spends nothing — no readied intent, no roll. That is what makes a charge worth a season's wages: it is magic grown on a quiet day and spent on a bad one, carried across the rest that would otherwise have reset it.

**Spending one** takes no roll at all. The charge is in the inventory or it is not. When the fiction makes a *release* chancy — fumbled in the dark, jostled in a crowd, held near something that eats magic — that is a Luck roll at Standard, which is a thing the table already knows how to do.

The six charges every gifted Orthaen has heard of are **steady light**, **a sealed door**, **a veil of quiet**, **a chime at a threshold**, **warmth**, and **a held image**. All Minor. All in the equipment list. A setting can grow the list; the rules do not change when it does.

> **MM Note — crystal and the things that eat it**
>
> **Default:** a charge releases when it is touched, and it works.
>
> **The dial:** you can rule that a specific thing in your scene *suppresses* charges — they gutter, they will not take, the light dies as it leaves the stone.
>
> **The cost:** almost nothing, and it is the best tool this setting hands you. It is a legible, physical signal that something is badly wrong, and it costs a player no resource to receive. Use it where you would otherwise have narrated dread. Do not use it so often that players stop growing charges.

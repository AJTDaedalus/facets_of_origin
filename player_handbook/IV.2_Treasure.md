# Compendium: Treasure

Treasure is one of the reasons to go into the dark, and one of the five questions the table asks at the end of every session (Chapter II.4). It comes in four kinds. The MM rolls most of it on tables in MM6; this chapter says what each kind is, and what it can do for you.

---

## Coin

Coin buys gear, rooms, horses, favors and the occasional very good evening. It fills a slot per 100 (Chapter IV.1), which is why hoards get left behind, and why a party that finds one starts making plans.

---

## Trinkets

A **trinket** is a small strange thing with no rule attached: a glass eye that is warm to the touch, a key to nothing anyone has found, a letter in a hand nobody can read. Trinkets fill no slot unless you gather a sackful.

A trinket's value is in the question it asks. The MM may pay one off later. Players often do it first.

---

## Curios

A **curio** is a one-use wonder. A draught that closes wounds, a flask of smoke, a chalk that draws a door on a wall for as long as a breath. Each curio's entry says exactly what it does. Use it and it is gone.

You may carry **three** curios at a time. (The *Tinker* talent carries one more.) A curio fills a slot like any item. Draughts brewed with the *Alchemist* talent are curios.

---

## Relics

A **relic** is a lasting magical object with a **power** and a **quirk**. The power is why you want it. The quirk is the price of owning it: the sword that hums near water, the ring that makes its wearer honest at the worst moments, the lantern that shows the dead.

Anyone can use a relic. Its power belongs to the object, not to the person holding it. A relic that throws fire does not make its bearer a caster, and it costs no Fatigue unless its entry says so. A Body character who carries one is still a Body character, with no domain and no tradition.

Relics are rare. A campaign might see one every few sessions, and each is worth a story about where it came from.

> **Through the Mirror — why treasure comes early**
>
> A lot of fantasy games hold the good stuff back until the characters have earned it. We think that gets it backwards. Finding something strange in the first session is one of the best reasons to come back for the second.
>
> So the tables in MM6 hand out trinkets and curios from the start, and the session-end questions count treasure brought home as progress. Curios are one-use so that finding them never stops being exciting; relics carry quirks so that owning them never stops being interesting.

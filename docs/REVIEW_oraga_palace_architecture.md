# Review: Palace Boranis Visual Brief, §7 Extrapolations

*Independent second review of `docs/REFERENCE_oraga_palace_visual_brief.md` §7 (line 1972 onward),
2026-10-25. Checked against the printed 5e module (`conversions/dnd5e/oraga_night/`), the
Facets edition and Val'loh setting chapters (lore), DECISIONS TP28 and TP47–TP56, the
owner's reference image `references/house_raunu.jpeg`, and owner canon (used only to judge
theme; it isn't quoted here). Citations are `file:line` in the 5e module unless another
path is given. Module files 01, 04, 05, 08, 09 and 10 were being edited by another worker during this review; line numbers were re-checked against the working tree at the end of the review and may drift.*

## Summary

1. **Verdicts:** 2 APPROVE (7, 12), 13 APPROVE WITH CHANGE, 0 STRIKE. Most of the architecture fits. The owner's image demands the gothic silhouette, and the module supports a grown palace built "course over course".
2. **Hard contradictions with print:** the Root isn't "beneath the wine cellars" (item 10); B8 at +30 is a *third* floor in the module's American usage (item 5); the dark-wing stair can't start at gallery level when TP56's clean path runs through the *ground-floor* state rooms (item 5); the upper terrace has no grass, though S9 duels on the grass (item 14); and the river "running north" makes the boat's trip *up* the tributary go the wrong way (item 15).
3. **Geometry errors in §1.4:** the bell tower sits on the east-wing corridor, the SE Court tower sits inside the servants' hall, the S10 garden covers the wing's rooms, the east gallery's alcoves dig into the cleared corridor, and the garden wing is too small to hold B7 and TP56's drawing rooms.
4. **Theme risks:** the palette reads violet-charcoal first and rose second, which pushes the Court toward a horror castle when the module says "inside of a shell". Some masks aren't spirit-faces, and Sella's optional plain gray mask would blur the night's key omen. The Attendant's "dated" clothes add a tell the module never prints. The brief also has the palace still glowing after midnight, which contradicts the drain.
5. **What fits well:** the single-material reading of grown crystal, the plank-boarded doorway for the "one plank thick" wall, the octagonal chapel, the Audience Hall's light-dark-light walk, the 8-ft fall that explains S10's 20-ft wall, the shut-and-shrouded quiet rooms, the half-timbered street (the setting prints Rekuzan's in-between building as wood and stone), and the no-lettering rule.

## Ground truth used throughout

- **The palace is all one grown pink crystal.** "The seat of House Boranis is grown, not built. Its pink crystal was raised over generations by gifted ancestors, each of whom gave a working lifetime to a wing, a hall, a single perfect stair" (04:29–31). It is "pink crystal grown course over course" (04:149). The Court is "a vault of rose-lit crystal", warm "like stone that has been in sun all day" (04:237–240). In the Dead Dance the walls are "the color of the inside of a shell" (04:1426). Setting: "The sect palaces are crystal too. Everything else in the city is dull by comparison" (`settings/valloh/V3_Rekuzan_and_the_Tribes.md:31`). Owner canon agrees on the material and treats the architecture as generational gift-work.
- **Tone:** "Glamour Over a Blade … Play the first five Movements warm" (01:435–441); "a night of wonder and courage as much as fear" (01:454).
- **The drain:** "the rose draining away from the crystal" (05:130). From the river the palace is "burning rose-colored" (05:733), which is fire, not glow.
- **Spelling and usage are American** (`STYLE_5e.md:32`), so "second floor" means the storey above the ground floor.
- **The image** shows a dark violet gothic facade with every pane glowing magenta, two big towers flanking a central front, flying-buttress arcs, and a tall pointed window headed by a rosette (not a free-standing rose). A steep stair about 15–20 ft wide climbs straight to a tall arched entrance, with lantern posts at its foot and along it. Carriages wait at street level beside the stair foot, with no ramps. Half-timbered jettied houses with amber shopfronts line the street, the cobbles are wet, it is raining, and the guests wear 19th-century formal black, white and gowns.

## The 15 items

### 1. Footprint and shape — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** A U opening north, with the Court as the bar and the gardens cupped between the wings, fits everything printed. The Court is about 140 × 80 (05:525–529), the terraces are 120 ft wide (05:530–533), the east wing runs along the gardens at gallery level (05:534–538), and TP56 printed the garden wing, mews and stable yard north-west (04:469–482). A footprint of about 370 × 290 ft suits a chieftain's seat with "whole wings dark" (04:40). The blocks inside the footprint collide, though; see §B, item 1. The fix is to the table, not the footprint.

**Replacement wording (append to item 1):**
> …(§1.3). The §1.4 block table must be corrected so that no two blocks on the same level overlap (see the review's geometry list); the footprint itself is unchanged.

### 2. The street front — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** A landing and a stair below the gatehouse matches the image and doesn't conflict with S3. Three Blades stand "in a loose line across Gate Street, 10 feet out from the gate" (09:804), and the gate-walk is a "15-foot drop to the street" (09:800). Both work if the landing is the head of Gate Street, as the brief says. Three things don't fit:
- **The stair's size.** The image shows a steep stair about 15–20 ft wide. The brief's own §0.2 says "steep", but B0 gives a 50-ft-wide flight of shallow steps.
- **The carriage ramps.** They aren't in the image, where carriages set down at the stair foot. The module doesn't need them either. "The line of carriages reaches down to the second gate" (04:151) works with carriages queuing to the stair foot, and TP56 already sends carriages round by the West Lane to the stable yard (04:480–482). The ramps add about 140 ft of masonry-like bulk for nothing.
- **The gate itself.** In the image the stair climbs to one tall arch. In the brief that arch is the gatehouse, with a 45-ft court hidden behind it. The brief should say so, so that an artist doesn't draw the stair running straight into the Court's main doors.

The "single perfect stair" of 04:30–31 is a printed hook the stair can honor visually: one hand, one style. That isn't new canon.

**Replacement wording:**
> 2. **The street front:** a gate landing (80 × 30 ft, the head of Gate Street) and a steep grand stair about 20 ft wide rising 25 ft, heavy balustrades, crystal lanterns on posts at its foot and up both sides, matching the owner's image; carriages set down at the stair foot and go round by the West Lane to the stable yard (no carriage ramps). At the stair head, the gatehouse arch is the tall arched entrance of the owner's image, flanked by two gate towers; the Gatehouse Court lies hidden behind it and the Court's south front rises above it.

### 3. The south gable, rose window, towers, flèche, bell tower, pinnacles — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The twin towers, pinnacles and buttress arcs are in the owner's image, so a gothic silhouette is owner-directed and should stay. The module supports grown courses (04:149) and a "vault" (04:237, 05:525–526). The problems are these:
- **The rose window.** The image's great window is a tall pointed lancet headed by a rosette, not a free-standing 30-ft rose filling the gable. A wheel window is also the most church-specific shape in the cathedral kit, and the module's religion is eight gods in niches (04:284–286).
- **The flèche.** It is in neither the image nor the module. It is cathedral jargon for a timber-and-lead crossing spire, which this palace has no reason to have, and the printed bell tower (04:476) already gives the north end its vertical.
- **The bell tower's coordinates.** As drawn in §1.4 (x 94→112, y 134→152), it stands on the east-wing corridor and Veier's rooms (§B, item 1).
- **Masonry vocabulary.** The brief never says that buttresses, tracery and pinnacles are *grown*, not cut. "Carved" appears in B2 and B6, and "stonework" in places. A crystal-grown culture's gothic should show growth lines and facets, with no mortar joints, no cut-stone sculpture and no gargoyles.

**Replacement wording:**
> 3. **The south front** over the main doors, as in the owner's image: a great tall pointed window of grown tracery headed by a rosette, flanked by twin 120-ft Court towers; the bell tower at the Court's north-east corner (TP56), placed on the east gallery's north end so it clears the east wing; pinnacles and buttress arcs throughout. All of it is grown, not cut: facets and growth lines, no mortar joints, no carved figures or gargoyles. Each wing may show a slightly different crystal habit, since each was one ancestor's working life (04:29–31). No flèche.

### 4. The Court's interior — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The interior works with the printed numbers: 140 × 80, a 50-ft vault, galleries with 12-ft rails, a 10-ft main door, and the dais 120 ft away (05:525–529). It leaves the floor clear for the crowd rule (one guest per 5-ft square, 05:553). Ground-level aisles under the galleries give the S1 rail fall "into the crowd". A high gallery in the wall thickness gives Raunu's printed Movement III glimpse a place to happen. The musicians' gallery over the main doors gives "Dancers under a gallery of musicians" (04:237). Three changes are needed:
- **The east gallery's alcoves.** B3 puts "six deep alcoves (about 8 × 10 ft)" in each gallery's outer wall. The east gallery's outer wall is x = 100, and directly behind it lie the east stair (y 30–54) and the cleared corridor (y 54–114). Alcoves 8 ft deep there would cut into the corridor that card S4 needs clear and 10 ft wide (05:534–537). They would also open a second way from the banquet gallery to the wing doors that skips the east stair (TP28 seals only "the double doors at the head of the east stair").
- **"Clerestory"** is acceptable as a word, but the walls themselves are the light source. Windows should read as clearer crystal in a glowing wall, not as holes in a dark one.
- **"Carved crystal balcony"** for the musicians' gallery should be *grown* (04:29).

The hearths ("fire in the great hearths", 04:33) and the south-corner stairs are reasonable, unprinted guesses.

**Replacement wording:**
> 4. **The Court's interior:** clear dance floor; piers on its edge lines; ground-level side aisles beneath the banquet galleries; a high gallery at about +30 in the wall thickness; tall windows of clearer, brighter crystal above it (the walls are the light; windows are where the crystal is thinnest); a grown crystal musicians' gallery over the main doors; grand stairs to the galleries in the south corners; two hearths in the aisles. The east banquet gallery has no alcoves and no doors in its outer wall south of y 114, where the east stair and cleared corridor run behind it; its alcoves sit in its north end or within its 20-ft depth.

### 5. The West Range — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The Audience Hall at 110 × 50 with a 35-ft ceiling suits "bigger than it needs to be and almost entirely empty … the length of the room is between you and them" (04:256–262, 04:1133). Lighting it only at the door and the dais is a good reading of the dust-in-the-light read-aloud. The octagonal chapel is a natural shape for "Eight niches, one for each god" (04:284). These are the best parts of the brief. The problems:
- **B8 at +30.** The module says "second floor" five times (04:326, 04:464, 05:542, 08:237, 09:1254) and is written in American English (`STYLE_5e.md:32`), where the second floor is the storey just above the ground. The brief puts the gallery storey at +12 and the "second floor" at +30, which is a third floor. It then has to explain away S10's "lit window on the second floor" (09:1494) as "the window's storey seen from this low garden". Under the module's own convention, gallery level simply *is* the second floor in both places. TP56 approved the rooms "as placed", but §7's preface keeps heights open.
- **The dark-wing stair.** §1.5 starts it at the south end of the west banquet gallery, at gallery level. But TP56's printed detail puts "one clean path walked straight through toward the dark-wing stair" in the *ground-floor* shut state rooms (04:462). A path through ground-floor rooms can't lead to a stair that starts upstairs.
- **B8's furniture.** The brief gives it "shelves of crystal" for the instruments, which an image model will draw as bookshelves against the no-books rule (02:19–21), and "a cold fireplace, swept clean, with nothing in it". TP38 cut the hearth of scorched cards. An empty fireplace drawn in that room reads as the cut clue's ghost.

**Replacement wording:**
> 5. **The West Range:** the B4 antechamber (30 × 30) and Audience Hall (110 × 50, ceiling 35) west of the Court; the octagonal chapel (40 ft) off the antechamber; shut state rooms on the ground floor, with the dark-wing stair rising from among them (TP56's clean path leads to it); the dark wing on the second floor, the storey above the ground floor (about +15, roughly level with the banquet galleries but with no door to them), with Raunu's study in a south-west corner tower (30 × 25). The study has no shelves and no fireplace: the instruments stand on a long grown bench and on ledges grown out of the wall.

### 6. The garden wing, trophy gallery, lower gallery, mews, ward-ring — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The rooms are placed as TP56 approved them, and they satisfy B13's "reachable from B7 and from the service run" (04:420–421) with the narrow stair from B7 and the service door at the lower gallery's south end. Two problems:
- **The ward-ring.** The brief draws the grandmother's soul-crystal inside a ring set in the floor and calls it "the gallery ring of Raunu's own work". That phrase comes from the trap rules (05:1326–1327), which name such a ring as one of three places an Uninvited can be caught, without saying where. B7's wards are "old crystalwork" (04:313) and "generations-old" (02:371). Making the soul-crystal's ward Raunu's own trap ring does three things the [VX] rule forbids: it fixes a story location the module leaves open, it mixes old work with Raunu's, and it puts a trap under the object Agenda 5 must lift.
- **Space.** The garden wing is 35 × 64 ft (§1.4: x −55→−20, y 150→214). A 30 × 60 trophy gallery fills almost all of its upper floor, but TP56 printed "the garden wing's drawing rooms, gallery level, beside the trophy gallery" (04:469–471). They don't fit.

**Replacement wording:**
> 6. **The garden wing** mirroring the east wing on the terraces' west side: the trophy gallery (30 × 40) at gallery level off the gallery corridor, with the two drawing rooms beyond it at the wing's north end; the lower gallery and B13's door beneath; the falcon mews behind it. The soul-crystal stands on its own old ward (generations-old crystalwork on its pedestal), not inside a ring of Raunu's. Where "a gallery ring of Raunu's own work" is, if anywhere, is the owner's to decide.

### 7. The East Range — APPROVE

**Reason.** TP56 printed the servants' hall, dormitories and laundry in the East Range beside the kitchens (04:447–455). Kitchens behind the gatehouse with a kitchen-yard gate fit "slip in through the kitchens" (04:208) and S7's "the second course going up" (kitchens below the gallery level). The amber, fire-lit kitchen as the one room lit more by fire than crystal is a good touch. One fix belongs to §1.4, not to this item: the SE Court tower is drawn inside the servants' hall (§B, item 1).

### 8. The service-passage ring — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The route meets every printed constraint: passages that "thread the whole palace, including the east wing and the garden stair" (04:398), 5 ft wide with "one lamp in three", and the S11 door on the west side. Placing the "one plank thick" wall (05:422) at an old doorway boarded with a single plank is the best idea in the brief. It explains why a grown-crystal wall is a plank there. The servants' postern on the West Lane gives the Phern line its printed exit ("take Corro out through the gallery-side service door, and whoever follows them gets out too", 05:1050). Two small changes:
- **After midnight**, the Bought hold more than the gate. Sixteen Blades: "four at the gatehouse, twelve on the perimeter" (`facts.yaml:105, 118`). A map that shows the postern, the kitchen-yard gate and the river gate should flag that the perimeter exists, and should leave to the owner what it does at each door.
- **"Plain plastered crystal"** mixes materials. Use "plain crystal gone dull with handling".

**Replacement wording (append):**
> 8. … and a servants' postern on the West Lane. All passages are plain crystal gone dull with handling, not plaster. After midnight the Bought's twelve-Blade perimeter (facts.yaml) lies outside the lanes and gates; what it does at the postern and the kitchen gate is the owner's to decide, and no art should show it.

### 9. The ground falls north and east — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** This is the right kind of extrapolation. It adds no story and resolves two printed facts at once: "the upper terrace lies below that rail" (05:540) and "the wall is 20 feet of grown crystal to the window" (09:1525), with a 12-ft gallery floor (05:534–535). The arithmetic is slightly off, though. With the garden 8 ft below the Court, the wing's floor is 20 ft above the garden, so the *window* (sill about 3 ft above the floor) is about 23 ft up. The terraces can stay at −8, which gives the garden stair its 20-ft fall and the rail crowd its view down at S9. The S10 garden is a separate garden "around the corner" and can sit a little higher. The brief's B9/S10 note should also drop the "seen from this low garden" gloss on "second floor" (see item 5).

**Replacement wording:**
> 9. **The ground falls north and east:** the upper terrace about 8 ft below the Court (so the garden stair falls 20 ft from the wing's corridor), and the garden below the east wing about 5 ft below the Court, so the wing's lit window (sill about 3 ft above the +12 floor) is 20 ft above that garden, as card S10 prints. The wing's gallery level is the "second floor" S10 names, in the module's American usage.

### 10. The cellars and the Root — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The printed route is kitchens → wine cellar → lower cellar stair → seam → Root (04:661, 08:241), and the brief keeps it. But the Root is printed as lying "**Beneath the wine cellars**, behind a seal of living crystal" (04:410). The brief puts the wine cellar under the kitchens in the East Range and the Root under the Court's south half, about 60–100 ft away laterally, so the Root is not beneath the wine cellars. The reasoning (the Court's walls are "the palace's oldest work", 04:243) is attractive, and it can be kept by running the wine cellars west under the Gatehouse Court to the Court's south end. The root-node column is a strong, theme-true image ("centuries of house rising from this one root", 04:410–413), but it is the brief's single biggest visual invention in the room the module calls beautiful, so it should be flagged. "It keeps its glow when every wall above goes dark" bears on the printed light rules ("nobody sees the Radiant without a light", 05:551–552) and on why guests shelter there (TP47). It is a story-adjacent claim, so the owner should decide it.

**Replacement wording:**
> 10. **The cellars and the Root:** the wine cellars (about 60 × 40, −15) run from under the kitchens west beneath the Gatehouse Court to the Court's south end; the lower cellar stair goes down at their west end to the lower cellar (−30) and its living-crystal wall; the Root (a 50-ft domed rotunda, −45) lies beneath the wine cellars' west end, under the Court's south half, grown from a central root-node (owner to confirm the root-node's look). Whether the Root keeps its glow after the drain is the owner's to decide; until then, draw it lit by Raunu's candles and lamps.

### 11. Materials and color — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** Treating the palace as one material that reads differently at night is right, and it is how the owner's image squares with the printed "pink crystal". But the brief makes it *two* crystals: "dense smoky crystal" for the mass and "clearer crystal" for the glow. Print knows only pink crystal (04:29–33; `V3:31`), and owner canon agrees. The palette also leads with violet-charcoal, while every printed light cue is rose, shell, warm, "in sun all day" (04:237–240, 04:1426), and the tone is "Play the first five Movements warm" (01:437). A palace drawn mostly charcoal reads as a vampire's castle, which this house is not until midnight. After the drain, the brief's mood table still has "the palace glowing and burning rose" in the gardens (§0.2), which contradicts 05:130. The rose at 05:733 is fire seen through dead crystal. "Ward-light as hard white-rose" is fine: it is the house's stored crystal firing.

**Replacement wording:**
> 11. **Materials and color:** one material, pink grown crystal. Where it is thick, unlit or seen against the night sky it reads deep violet to charcoal (the owner's image); wherever it is thinner or lit from within it glows rose to magenta up close and from the street. Before midnight rose leads and the dark mass supports it; up close the light is warm and shell-like. After the drain the crystal is colorless, milky, gray-violet dead glass everywhere; any rose after midnight is fire seen through or reflected in it, never the walls' own glow. Ward-light is a hard white-rose.

### 12. Boranis livery and the honor guard's surcoats — APPROVE

**Reason.** The module gives no house colors, and violet-charcoal with rose-crystal buttons ties the household to its walls without lettering. The honor guard's printed kit is "Chain Mail, Shield" (10:474) with two house crystals. Surcoats over that add nothing to the story. Livery has to be recognizable at a glance, because "a festival hire's livery and a confident walk" is a printed trespass route (04:306) and Tavva's crew wear it (07:689). The rose piping does that work, so keep it. **Owner to decide:** whether household staff and honor guards wear Oraga masks. The module says "Rekuzan wears masks" (02:37) but never says so of staff, and the brief draws all of them bare-faced.

### 13. Costume, age and hair (§4) — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The house style fits the image's 19th-century formal black. The printed sash-and-thigh knives are kept, and the Thenya are in plain border wool (09:1494, 07:783), which is good. Problems:
- **Doublets and breeches.** These pull toward the Renaissance and away from the image's long coats and gowns. Drop "fitted doublets".
- **Masks must be spirit-faces.** The module defines them as "the dead of the year, the spirits of the harvest, the luck and grief of the season" (02:37–39). Draunel's "gilt sun-faced mask" and Callun's "narrow silver half-mask" are Venetian party masks, not spirit-faces.
- **No other mask may be plain gray.** The omen is "three guests in spirit masks: gray, unornamented" (04:1187). Sella's optional "plain gray mourning mask" and an unspecified "plain" mask for Essin or Vell would blur it. Strike the gray option, and say that every other plain mask is not gray.
- **The Attendant's dress.** "Good but dated" adds a tell the module never prints. It passes as "a great house's attendant, by its clothes" (07:633), and only the three's dress is "centuries out of fashion".
- **The Church's crystal.** Kovaun's "heavy pectoral of plain crystal" and crystal-headed staff tie the Church's regalia to Orthaen crystal, a relationship the module doesn't set up. Use plain metal.
- **Invented emblems.** Corro's "Phern colors (warm ochre and green)" invents tribal colors. Sella's "wooden token" invents an emblem of Elanna. Tavva's "ring of keys" hands a thief a plot object, while the only printed key is Corval's to the service door (TP39).
- **Ages.** Maiven is printed as "Veier ten years younger" (07:496). With Maiven "about twenty", Veier should be about thirty, not late twenties.
- **Vorlain's face.** Vorlain has "his brother's face" (§4's own Printed line). His [VX] line should say so, so the two are drawn alike.

**Replacement wording (edits to §4; item 13 then reads "All costume, age and hair proposals in §4, as amended"):**
> - House style: "Long coats and fitted doublets for men" → "Long tailored coats for men".
> - Vorlain [VX]: add "Raunu's face, a few years younger and never still."
> - Veier [VX]: "late twenties" → "about thirty".
> - Draunel [VX]: "a gilt sun-faced mask" → "a gilt harvest-spirit face".
> - Callun [VX]: "mask a narrow silver half-mask" → "mask a narrow silver spirit-face of the year's dead".
> - Sella [VX]: "unmasked or a plain gray mourning mask (owner to choose)" → "unmasked, or a dark grief-spirit face (owner to choose); never gray". Delete "a simple wooden token at her neck".
> - Kovaun [VX]: "a heavy pectoral of plain crystal (no inscriptions); the staff a tall plain rod with a crystal head" → "a heavy plain metal pectoral (no inscriptions); the staff a tall plain rod".
> - Corro [VX]: "in Phern colors (warm ochre and green)" → "in warm, bright colors".
> - Tavva [VX]: delete "a ring of keys".
> - The Attendant [VX]: "an old-fashioned upper-servant's dark coat and breeches, good but dated" → "a great house's upper-servant's dark coat, correct and current, nothing to remark on".
> - Add to the §4 preamble: "No mask but the three may be plain gray. Every other mask is a spirit-face: the year's dead, a harvest spirit, the season's luck or grief."

### 14. Garden planting — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** Autumn planting (late roses, asters, ripe apples) fits a harvest festival at "the turn of the season" (02:32). Lanterns stopping short of the river gate fit the printed escape. But S9's duel is on the upper terrace (`facts.yaml:183`), and its terrain is "gravel paths", "anyone on the grass", and Draunel "will not set foot on the grass" (09:1425–1428). The brief's upper terrace is four clipped-box beds around a gravel cross, with no lawn to duel on and box hedges in the way. The grass walk is on the second terrace. The flower beds that matter are in the lower garden (Table V–5 trick 3, 05:439) and the S10 garden (09:1527), and the brief has both.

**Replacement wording:**
> 14. **Garden planting:** upper terrace an open lawn (the duelling ground, S9) framed by gravel paths and low flower borders against the balustrades, lanterns on poles; second terrace a parterre of low clipped beds around a gravel cross; third terrace an orchard walk of pleached apples; lower garden looser, a central gravel path to the river gate between deep flower beds; garden walls of crystal.

### 15. Neighborhood — APPROVE WITH CHANGE

**Reason.** The half-timbered townhouses with amber shopfronts are in the owner's image and match the setting: the city "swallowed the gaps between [the districts] with wood-and-stone homes, taverns and industry … Everything else in the city is dull by comparison" (`V3:31`). This is the best-supported item on the list. The contrast between a dull timber street and a glowing palace is the printed point. The West and East Lanes and the stable yard are TP56-printed (04:480). Three corrections:
- **The river.** The boat goes "north **up** the tributary to the foothills" (02:216). Going up means upstream, so the tributary flows *south* from the foothills. The brief's "running north" (§1.1, site plan) has it flowing toward the foothills. The site plan also runs the river east–west along the garden foot while calling it the north-running tributary.
- **Where the street lies.** The setting puts wood-and-stone building *between* the districts. Whether Gate Street lies inside the Boranis district's crystal walls, and so whether its houses should be timber at all, is the owner's to decide. The image says timber, and that should probably win.
- **Mist off the river.** "Mist" is this setting's most loaded word (the eastern mists, 02:25–28). A river mist in every garden plate invites a reading the module never makes. Drop it, or keep it to one thin line on the water in the boat scene.

**Replacement wording:**
> 15. **Neighborhood:** half-timbered townhouses and amber shopfronts on Gate Street (the owner's image; owner to confirm the street lies outside or inside the Boranis district walls), the West and East Lanes, the stable yard. At the garden's foot the river runs past the river walk; Vell's boat goes upstream, north, up the tributary toward the foothills (the tributary flows south). No river mist, except perhaps a thin line on the water in the boat scene (owner to decide).

## Problems outside §7

1. **§1.4 block overlaps** (same level, checked by coordinates):
   - The bell tower (x 94→112, y 134→152) covers the east-wing corridor (10 × 18 ft) and the edge of Veier's rooms. Move it onto the east gallery's north end, for example x 82→100, y 122→140.
   - The SE Court tower (x 94→114, y −14→6) sits inside the servants' hall (14 × 16 ft) and the kitchens.
   - The S10 garden is given as x 110→220, which covers the wing's rooms (x 110→135) and the service stair hall. It should start at x 135.
   - The service run (x ~100→125) passes through the east stair (x 100→110, y 30→54).
   - The SW tower clips the chapel and the state rooms by about 4 ft. That is minor, if the tower is meant to be engaged.
2. **The east gallery's alcoves vs. the cleared corridor:** see item 4.

7. **Gatehouse Court capacity.** The [MAP] 60 × 45-ft court holds about 108 guests at one per 5-ft square (05:553), not 200. That is fine, and it matches "the crowd's back is to the heat" (the overflow is in the Court). But Scene 13 shouldn't show 200 people inside the gatehouse court.

# Combat

Combat in Facets d20 is SRD 5.2.1 combat with the slow parts taken out. You still roll a d20, add your bonus and compare it to Armor Class. You still move, take an action and maybe a bonus action. What changes is the bookkeeping around all that: there is no initiative count, most monsters don't roll damage, and most foes give up before they die.

The goal is a fight that ends in three or four rounds. If yours are running to seven, something below is being skipped, and the MM's guide (Chapter 09) will help find it.

---

## The Round

A round has two halves: one side acts, then the other.

**Table 8–1: The Round**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Who goes first | Surprise decides it, or one Dexterity contest between the sides. |
| 2. First side acts | Every creature on that side takes its turn, in any order the side likes. |
| 3. Second side acts | Every creature on the other side does the same. |
| 4. Next round | Back to step 2. The order of the sides never changes. |

### Who Goes First

If one side is **surprised**, the other side goes first. No roll.

Otherwise, run one contest. Every player rolls a Dexterity check (your initiative, with anything that adds to it or gives you advantage on it), and the party uses its **best** roll. The MM rolls one Dexterity check for the enemies, using the best Dexterity modifier among them. Higher goes first. On a tie, the players do.

That is the only initiative roll of the fight. A couple of signatures (*Master Plan* and *Seen It Coming*) let a side skip the contest and simply go first; they say so.

### Your Turn

Your turn is the SRD turn: move up to your Speed, take one action, take a bonus action if something gives you one, and interact with one object for free. You can split your movement around your action.

On your side's half of the round, the players decide the order. Talk it over. If the rogue wants the fighter to knock the ogre prone first, the fighter goes first. Each character takes a whole turn before the next one starts, so nobody is waiting on a half-finished move.

Every creature still has its own turn, so effects that last "until the start of your next turn" or "until the end of its next turn" work exactly as written.

> **MM Note — the committee problem**
>
> **Default:** let the party plan its half of the round out loud. That planning is the fun of side initiative.
>
> **The dial:** if the table starts to deliberate for minutes, say "thirty seconds," and then ask whoever is looking most certain to go.
>
> **The cost:** push too hard and you get the old problem back, where everyone waits in silence for their name.

### Actions

The actions are the SRD 5.2.1 list, unchanged: **Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence, Magic, Ready, Search, Study** and **Utilize**. Influence in a fight uses the attitude track (Chapter 06); a foe who has been hurt is often much more willing to listen.

Ready is how you react to something the other side does on its half of the round: "if it comes through the door, I hit it."

## Reactions and Opportunity Attacks

You get **one reaction** per round, and it comes back at the start of your turn.

**Leaving an enemy's reach provokes one opportunity attack from it**, if it has its reaction. That is the whole rule. It only happens when you move out on your own; being shoved, teleporting, or taking the Disengage action does not provoke.

---

## Attacks and Damage

Attack rolls, advantage and disadvantage, critical hits and cover all work as the SRD says. Cover in brief: half cover is +2 to AC and Dexterity saves, three-quarters cover is +5, and total cover means you can't be targeted directly.

**You roll your damage.** Monsters do not. A monster deals the **fixed damage** printed on its stat block, which is the SRD average. On a critical hit the MM rolls it: double the damage dice, roll them, and add the modifier. So an ordinary hit is just a number off the page, and a critical hit is a roll the whole table watches.

---

## Bloodied

A creature at or below half its hit points is **Bloodied**. This applies to everyone, you included, and some talents care about it.

The MM tells you when a foe becomes Bloodied. You can see it: the limp, the dropped shield, the look toward the door.

### Morale

Most foes don't want to die. A foe that isn't a boss checks its **morale** when it first becomes Bloodied, and again when its leader falls. It makes a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw; if it fails, it **breaks**. A broken foe flees, surrenders or tries to bargain, whichever suits it. A few things never break: the mindless, and the bound. The MM knows which.

The MM runs morale. What you need to know is that it changes what a fight is for. A fight usually ends before the last foe falls, and it often ends with somebody you can question. A foe who surrenders is a prisoner and a source. What you do with one is the kind of choice the game is built to ask you, and the MM will remember it.

---

## Kinds of Foe

**Minions** have 1 hit point. Any hit kills one. A minion that succeeds on a saving throw against damage takes none, and one that fails is dead. Minions deal fixed damage like any monster. They come in groups and fall in groups, and the MM will usually make it obvious that they are the cannon fodder.

**Standard** foes are everything in between: SRD stat blocks, used as written, with fixed damage.

**Bosses** take **two turns** on every one of their side's halves of the round. When a boss becomes Bloodied, it changes: it gets worse, gets desperate, calls something up, or tears the room apart. Bosses do not check morale. They have no legendary or lair actions; the second turn and the change at Bloodied do that job.

---

## Dropping and Dying

This is the SRD rule, unchanged. At 0 hit points you fall **unconscious** and start making **death saving throws** at the start of each of your turns: DC 10, no modifier. Three successes and you're stable; three failures and you die. A natural 20 brings you back with 1 hit point; a natural 1 counts as two failures. Taking damage while at 0 is a failure, or two from a critical hit. Any healing wakes you up.

When you reduce a foe to 0 with a melee attack, you can knock it out instead of killing it.

## Conditions

The conditions are the SRD 5.2.1 list, unchanged: Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Exhaustion, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned and Unconscious.

> **Through the Mirror — why fights are short here**
>
> Long fights are rarely long because they are interesting. They are long because the ogre has 30 hit points left after everyone has run out of good ideas, and because every turn waits on a count and a damage roll.
>
> So we cut in four places. Side initiative ends the waiting for your name. Fixed monster damage saves the MM a roll every attack. Minions let a big fight stay cheap. Morale ends the fight when it has been decided, instead of when the arithmetic runs out. None of it touches the part you came for: your turn, your roll, your idea.

## In Play: The Alley

*The party is walking back from the archive after dark. The MM has already described the alley: narrow, wet cobbles, one lamp at the far end, and a stack of crates against the left wall.*

**MM:** "Four figures step out from behind the crates. Three have clubs. The fourth has a proper sword and the air of someone who has done this before. 'Your purses,' he says, 'and nobody has to bleed.'"

**Mordai:** "I'd like him to try."

**MM:** "Nobody's surprised, so roll for sides. Everyone make a Dexterity check."

→ Zulnut rolls **d20 + Dex (+3)** and gets a **19**. The MM rolls **d20 + 2** for the thugs and gets an **11**. *The party goes first.*

*(Behind the screen: the three club-wielders are minions, AC 12, 1 hit point, attack +3, fixed damage 4. The leader is AC 13, 22 hit points, attack +4, fixed damage 6. The MM has already decided the minions are paid by the hour.)*

**Zulnut:** "I'll go first. The nearest one with a club. I hit him and keep walking."

→ Zulnut rolls **d20 + 5** against AC 12 and gets a **15**. Hit.

*One minion folds up against the crates. Zulnut steps over him and ends up leaning on the lamp post as if he'd been there all evening.*

**Mordai:** "The leader. With the sword."

→ Mordai rolls **d20 + 5** against AC 13 and gets a **17**. Hit. His longsword deals **1d8 + 3**: 8 + 3 = **11**.

**MM:** "He's at 11 out of 22. That's Bloodied." (The MM rolls his morale save: **d20 + 0**, a **14**.) "He staggers, and he doesn't run. He wants this purse now."

**Zahna:** "I'll study the leader. His stance, his sword, whether he's had training."

**MM:** "Study action. Intelligence, and your Specialty doesn't cover street fighting. DC 12."

→ Zahna rolls **d20 + Int (+3)** and gets a **16**.

**MM:** "Watch training, years ago. He holds his guard the way Mordai does."

**Zahna:** "Mordai. He's one of yours."

**Mordai:** "He is not."

**MM:** "The enemies' half. The two minions go for Zahna, who has been announcing things loudly."

*The MM rolls two attacks: an 8 misses, a 16 hits. Zahna takes a fixed 4 damage and says something about the scholarly method being undervalued. The leader swings at Mordai, with disadvantage from the Sap mastery of Mordai's longsword: a 19 and a 7, so a 7. It rings off his shield.*

**MM:** "Round two. Your side."

→ Mordai rolls **d20 + 5** and gets a **20**, a critical hit. He rolls **2d8 + 3** and gets **13**. *The leader goes down in a heap of expensive cloak.*

**MM:** "Their leader's fallen. The two minions check morale." (A **6** and a **3**. Both fail.) "One drops his club and runs. The other raises both hands and says he has a mother."

**Zulnut:** "I'd like a cigarette, and I'd like it lit from that lamp."

**MM:** (Two rounds. Nobody dead, one prisoner, and the fight took less time than choosing dinner.)

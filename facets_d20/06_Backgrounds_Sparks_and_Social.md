# Backgrounds, Knacks, Sparks and Social Play

Your Facet says what you can do in a fight. This chapter covers the rest of who you are: where you came from, the small things you're good at, what you want, and how you get along with the people you meet. A background gives you real proficiencies and a knack, your Specialty gives you advantage, and your Drives earn you Sparks.

---

## Backgrounds

Your **background** is the life you led before the story started. It gives you:

- **Abilities:** +1 to each of three abilities. No score goes above 20.
- **Skills:** proficiency in two skills. You take these before your Facet's two picks, so if the background already gives you a skill on your Facet's list, pick a different one from that list (Chapter 02, step 6).
- **A tool:** proficiency with one tool.
- **A knack:** the one the background names (*Knacks*, below).
- **A Specialty:** you write it (*Specialties*, below). Each background suggests one.

Any character can take any background. The eight below are ready to use; after them is how to write your own.

> **Reading a background entry**
>
> A name, two or three sentences of history, then the three abilities that each rise by 1, the two skills, the tool, the knack and an example Specialty. The example is a starting point: rewrite it to fit your character.

### City Watch Veteran
*Background*

You walked a beat for years: street patrols, crowd control, and the kind of trouble that happens at close range in narrow alleys. You learned to read a situation before it turned.

**Abilities:** Strength, Constitution, Wisdom · **Skills:** Athletics, Insight · **Tool:** gaming set · **Knack:** *Streetwise*
**Specialty:** knows the layout, the regulars and the unofficial rules of one district you patrolled.

### Wilderness Scout
*Background*

You went ahead of other people into country nobody had mapped, and came back to tell them what was there. You sleep lightly, and you notice broken twigs.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Constitution, Wisdom · **Skills:** Survival, Stealth · **Tool:** cartographer's tools · **Knack:** *Pathfinder*
**Specialty:** can follow a person's or animal's trail across ground they crossed within the last day.

### Dockworker
*Background*

You loaded and unloaded ships for a living. You know rope and weight and tide tables, and you know which crates are lighter than their paperwork says.

**Abilities:** Strength, Dexterity, Constitution · **Skills:** Athletics, Investigation · **Tool:** vehicles (water) · **Knack:** *Skilled*
**Specialty:** knows how cargo manifests work, how they get falsified, and what a suspicious shipment looks like on paper and on the dock.

### Guild Apprentice
*Background*

You were bound to a guild or a learned institution and spent years copying, fetching and being corrected. You learned its methods before you understood them, and you understand them now.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom · **Skills:** Arcana, Investigation · **Tool:** calligrapher's supplies · **Knack:** *Gadgeteer*
**Specialty:** knows the vocabulary, record-keeping and people of the guild that trained you, well enough to read its documents and recognize its work.

### Physician's Assistant
*Background*

You held the basin, boiled the linen and watched a physician work. You have seen most of the ways a body goes wrong, and you are hard to shock.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom · **Skills:** Medicine, Insight · **Tool:** herbalism kit · **Knack:** *Field Medic*
**Specialty:** recognizes the common diseases, poisons and injuries, and can say how each will progress.

### Temple Acolyte
*Background*

You served in a house of worship: lighting lamps, learning rites by heart, listening to people who came in carrying something heavy. Whether you still believe is your business.

**Abilities:** Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma · **Skills:** Religion, Insight · **Tool:** calligrapher's supplies · **Knack:** *Encyclopedic*
**Specialty:** knows the rites, symbols and internal politics of one religious tradition in detail.

### Street Performer
*Background*

You worked crowds for coins: songs, tricks, tumbling, whatever kept them from walking on. You learned to read an audience quickly, because the performers who couldn't went hungry.

**Abilities:** Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma · **Skills:** Performance, Acrobatics · **Tool:** a musical instrument · **Knack:** *Performer*
**Specialty:** can judge a crowd's mood, and the best way to approach it, within a few moments of watching.

### Traveling Merchant
*Background*

You bought in one town and sold in the next. You have haggled in markets where you didn't speak the language, and walked away from deals that smelled wrong.

**Abilities:** Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma · **Skills:** Persuasion, Insight · **Tool:** vehicles (land) · **Knack:** *Silver Tongue*
**Specialty:** can tell the fair price of common goods, and whether an offer means opportunity, desperation or a trap.

### Writing Your Own

1. **Name it,** and write two or three sentences of history. The MM will read it, so write for them.
2. **Pick three abilities** the history would have exercised. Each rises by 1.
3. **Pick two skills** from the SRD 5.2.1 list.
4. **Pick a knack** from the list below.
5. **Write the Specialty.**

A written background has no tool.

## Specialties

A **Specialty** is one narrow area of expertise, specific enough that the MM knows exactly when it applies. You have one.

**When your Specialty applies to a check, you have advantage on it.** Inside your Specialty, routine and informational things just happen: you know the answer, you read the document correctly, you recognize the symptom. The MM calls for a roll only when there is real risk or opposition, and then you roll with advantage.

"Good with people" is too broad to be a Specialty; "can read a crowd's mood within a few moments" is about right. If you can't picture the moment it would come up, it is too broad. If you can't picture it coming up twice, it is too narrow. A Specialty isn't tied to one skill: the same one can help a History check, an Insight check or a Persuasion check, whichever the moment calls for.

> **Example: a Specialty doing its job**
>
> **MM:** You're back in the district you walked for years. The tavern on the corner has a new sign over the door.
>
> **Mordai:** Who owns it now? And where does the old owner drink?
>
> **MM:** That's your Specialty, so no roll. You know both. The old owner still drinks here, at the back table.
>
> **Mordai:** Then I sit at the back table.
>
> **MM:** (Of course he does.)

## Knacks

A **knack** is a small, useful thing your character can do outside a fight. You get one from your background, one at 2nd, 6th and 10th level, and you can take one instead of an ability increase at 4th or 8th. A knack never changes attack rolls, damage, AC, hit points, saving throws, initiative or spell slots.

### Expertise
*Knack*

Choose two skills you are proficient in. Your proficiency bonus is doubled for checks with them.

### Skilled
*Knack*

Gain proficiency in any three skills or tools, in any combination.

### Silver Tongue
*Knack*

Once per NPC per scene, a failed Persuasion or Deception check doesn't move their attitude down. And beating the DC by 5 moves their attitude two steps, instead of needing to beat it by 10.

### Wild Kin
*Knack*

You can speak with beasts, and they start no worse than Neutral toward you. You have advantage on Survival checks to track or forage.

### Field Medic
*Knack*

You can stabilize a creature as a bonus action. During a short rest, each Hit Die a creature you tend spends restores extra hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

### Gadgeteer
*Knack*

You gain proficiency with tinker's tools. With them and a short rest you can make one simple non-combat device: a grapnel, a tripwire alarm, a lamp that won't gutter, a lock you can pick in a breath. It works until your next long rest.

### Encyclopedic
*Knack*

Once per long rest, state a fact about a creature, a place or a piece of history. It is true (the MM may add a complication).

### Well-Travelled
*Knack*

You know two more languages, and in any settlement you can find someone to translate any other language for you within the hour.

### Streetwise
*Knack*

In any settlement you can find someone who knows someone, given an hour and a few coins.

### Performer
*Knack*

You can earn a modest living by performing, and a crowd that watches you perform starts one step friendlier toward you.

### Pathfinder
*Knack*

Your group can't get lost except by magic, can travel stealthily at a normal pace, and finds twice as much food when you forage.

### Artisan
*Knack*

Gain proficiency with two kinds of artisan's tools. You craft nonmagical items in half the time.

### Many Faces
*Knack*

Gain proficiency with the disguise kit. You can put on a disguise in a minute without one, and your disguise holds up to a casual look without a roll; only close scrutiny calls for a check.

## Drives

Every character has two **Drives**, each one short sentence:

- **A want:** something your character is reaching for. *I want to see my name on a guild charter.*
- **A line:** something your character won't do. *I won't lie to a priest.*

Drives are for the MM as much as for you. They tell the MM what to put in your path. A good Drive can be tested: the MM can build a scene where holding to it costs you.

**Compels.** Once per scene, when one of your Drives would make things harder for you, say so. If the MM agrees, you take the complication and gain a Spark. You call the compel and the MM agrees or doesn't; the complication has to be one that really costs you.

You can rewrite one Drive at the end of any session. A Drive fulfilled, or broken for good, is a fine moment for the MM to call a new level.

> **Example: a compel**
>
> **MM:** The two thugs you haven't dropped are backing off down the alley. The one with the knife is still standing over the shopkeeper.
>
> **Mordai:** I'm not leaving while he's there. That's my Drive. The others can chase the two.
>
> **MM:** Agreed. They get away, and you've got a knife fight on your hands. Take a Spark.

## Sparks

**Sparks** replace Heroic Inspiration.

**Table 6–1: Sparks**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Start** | 1 Spark at the start of every session. Unspent Sparks don't carry over. |
| **Most you can hold** | 3. |
| **Earn one** | A compel (above). The MM's call, for a great moment. Some features and talents: *Kindle*, *Prophecy*, *Turn the Odds*. |
| **Spend one** | After the MM says a d20 test by you, or by an ally whose help you describe, missed or failed, and before anything comes of it: add **1d6** to the roll. |

The MM tells you whether a roll hits or succeeds; you don't need to know the AC or the DC. If it missed or failed, you can then spend a Spark, before the MM says what happens next. The MM may still say "that's nowhere close" when a Spark obviously can't help. A natural 1 on an attack roll still misses. You can spend one Spark on a roll; two players can each spend one on the same roll, as long as each describes how they help. Helping this way costs no action, but your character has to be in a position to help. A Spark never gives advantage or a reroll.

> **Through the Mirror: why Sparks reset**
>
> Earlier drafts let Sparks pile up, and in simulated play they sat unspent, saved for a crisis that never came. Starting each session at 1 and capping at 3 keeps them moving.

## Social Play

Talking your way through a scene uses the same d20 as everything else, and its rules fit on one page.

### Attitude

Every character the MM plays has an **attitude** toward the party, somewhere on this track. A stranger starts **Neutral**; someone the party has crossed, or who has reason to distrust people like them, starts **Wary**. The MM can start anyone elsewhere for a reason.

**Table 6–2: The Attitude Track**

| Attitude | What they will do for you |
|---|---|
| **Hostile** | Work against you, openly or not. Won't help. |
| **Wary** | Talk, but give nothing that costs them anything. |
| **Neutral** | Help in small ways, when it's no risk to them. |
| **Friendly** | Help at some cost or risk to themselves. |
| **Ally** | Take real risks for you. |

Anything their attitude already allows, you get by asking. The roll is for moving the attitude.

### Making Your Case

Say what you're doing and why it should work. The MM calls for the check that fits: usually Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation or Performance, sometimes Insight or History if the argument depends on it. In a fight this is the Influence action, and a foe you move to Neutral or better stops fighting, as if it had broken (Chapter 08).

The DC depends on how hard this person is to move: **10** for someone with no real reason to refuse, **15** for someone with reasons, **20** for someone with good ones.

**Table 6–3: Social Results**

| Result | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Beat the DC by 10 or more | Moves **two** steps toward Ally |
| Meet or beat the DC | Moves **one** step toward Ally |
| Miss by 1 to 4 | No change, and that argument won't work again this scene |
| Miss by 5 or more | Moves **one** step toward Hostile |

**One roll per character per person per scene.** Each of you gets one try at moving a given person in a scene. Another character can make their own case; nobody gets a second roll until something changes.

**Intimidation** moves someone toward Ally only while the threat holds. They do what you want because they're afraid, not because they like you. Once the threat is gone, they fall one step toward Hostile from wherever it left them.

**Advantage.** You have advantage when you have **leverage** (something they want, something they fear, a debt they owe) or when your **Specialty** fits. Both at once is still just advantage.

**Limits.** Nobody moves past what makes sense for them. A guard captain who would hang for opening the gate won't open it at Friendly; at Ally he might, or he might tell you when the shift changes. The MM will tell you when you're asking for something no roll can get. There are no social hit points, and no amount of dice talks a person into anything.

## Exploration, Travel and Rest

Exploration uses the SRD 5.2.1 rules: travel pace, marching order, light and vision, hazards and foraging. The MM frames a journey as the moments that matter, such as the river that needs crossing, the night the watch hears something, and the arrival. Somebody navigates (a Survival check for each leg) and somebody keeps watch.

Resting is the SRD rule. A short rest is an hour; a long rest is eight. On a short rest you can spend Hit Dice, rolling each as the SRD says and adding your Constitution modifier (hit points on level-up are fixed; Hit Dice still roll). You have one Hit Die per level, of your current size. You recover everything on a long one. Features and talents that return on a rest say which one.

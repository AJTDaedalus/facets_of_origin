# Val'loh

*Walls of pink crystal, grown over generations, luminous at dusk.*

*Masks worn for the dead, so the living can say what they like for one night.*

*And a sea in the east that has stopped breathing in.*

---

Val'loh is glamour over a blade. The great houses have been at each other for generations and the Church blesses it, so the politics are conducted at parties, in public, in beautiful rooms, by people who are all visibly armed and all quite serious about not drawing. Everyone knows the rules. Everyone is watching to see who breaks them first.

That register is not decoration; it is where the Sparks are. A Val'loh table that talks its way through a room is paid for it exactly as a table that fights would be — and the fights, when they come, are between people who would all rather have been talking.

---

## What This Is

A setting Facet for Facets of Origin: one continent of the same world as Shattered Origin, far enough away that neither has heard of the other.

Ten peoples, all human, some of them born carrying magic in the blood. A capital city grown out of crystal by the hands of the people who live in it. A god-ordained law that reserves the written word to the Church, so that everything anyone knows is held in somebody's memory. A harvest festival where the whole city wears the faces of its dead. And a Blackwatch on the eastern coast that has spent this year watching the mists go out, and is not sleeping well.

It is written to be run with *Oraga Night*, which is a single night at a single ball, and to keep going afterward.

---

## Ten Things You Need to Know

**1. The tribes are human**, all ten of them, and four in five Orthaen are born able to grow crystal out of stone.

**2. A gift is a domain.** Not flair, not a knack — magic, in exactly the sense the core rules mean it. It is fast, it is yours, and it is Minor scope until you have grown into it.

**3. Spellcraft is the other road.** The scholarly tradition, learned over years, cast like any other domain. The fiction says it is patient work. The rules say nothing special about it at all.

**4. Crystal is wealth**, because a charged crystal is magic that anyone can carry and spend — including the four people in five who were born without a gift, and the visitors who were born without a tribe.

**5. There are no books.** The Church writes; everyone else remembers, and the Scora remember for everyone. Two exceptions, both narrow: a chief's formal invitation, and private slate scratch-work that gets wiped.

**6. Rekuzan is nine walled districts that grew together.** The gaps filled in with wood and stone centuries ago; the old crystal walls are still standing, and they glow at dusk.

**7. Everyone carries a knife to a ball.** Wearing one is dress. *Drawing* one is the crime, and the room enforces it before the guards arrive.

**8. The mists in the east have fallen to record lows this year**, and the people whose entire job is watching them are frightened. Inland this is a dinner-table curiosity.

**9. The strong are chosen by Fraden**, the Church says, so the tribes' wars are holy. Two things unite the tribes: Mazaa in the west and the mists in the east. Nothing else has ever managed it.

**10. This Facet changes nothing about how you roll or how you cast.** See below.

## What It Adds, Counted

> The core 2d6, Sparks, Conditions, exchanges, advancement, magic, and the three Facets are unchanged. Val'loh adds exactly: **ten Lineages** (two playable in Oraga Night), **ten Gift domains**, and **crystal charges** as one-use items. It removes nothing and it changes no rule.

Nothing in this Facet touches how you roll, how difficulty is set, how Sparks work, how Conditions land, how exchanges run, how skills and Facets advance, or how magic is cast. There is no Val'loh combat rule and no Val'loh casting rule. A table that has played one session of Facets of Origin can play this one without learning anything new about the game — only about the place.

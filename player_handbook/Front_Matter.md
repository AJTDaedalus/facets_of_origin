# Facets of Origin — Player Handbook

*A digital-first tabletop roleplaying game, built to keep the rules out of the story's way.*

---

## How to Read This Book

**If you have never played a tabletop roleplaying game before**, don't start here. Start with the **Quick Start** — it hands you a finished character, the one rule the whole game runs on, and an example scene, and has you playing in about ten minutes. This book will still be here afterward, and it will make far more sense once you have rolled a few dice.

**If you are a player**, you need less of this book than you might think:

- **Chapter I** — what the game is and how it thinks
- **Chapters II.1 through II.6** — creating your character: attributes, magic, Facets and advancement, Background, and skills
- **Chapter III.1** — the core resolution system: one roll, three outcomes
- **Chapter III.2, Chapter III.3, and Chapter IV.1** — hazards and recovery, combat, and equipment; skim them now, look things up later

**If you are the Mirror Master**, read the whole handbook, then the Mirror Master's Manual: MM1 (encounters and enemies), MM2 (session design), MM3 (campaign design), MM4 (running the table), and MM5 (the mid-session quick reference). The Bestiary is the third book and the one you consult rather than read — eighteen creatures, already statted, already carrying their own conduct.

**The Facet modules** listed at the end of the Table of Contents — Downtime, Crafting, Economy, Feats, Technology — are optional. No table needs them to play. Your MM will tell you if your campaign uses any.

**None of the three books is a novel.** Read the Quick Start, then your chapters, then stop. All three are built to be consulted mid-session: the Glossary defines terms, the Index says where each term is ruled on, and the List of Tables names every table by designation. Tables are numbered per chapter — Table III.3–2 is the second table in Chapter III.3 — and the text always cites them that way.

## The Boxes

Six kinds of box appear in these books. Each has one job and one voice, and they never blend — a box never mixes a rule with a flavor beat, and a rule that the game always uses is never in a box at all. If it is boxed, it is either optional, worked, or meta.

**Through the Mirror** — the designers, speaking as *we*, about why a rule is shaped the way it is: what it is protecting, and what breaks if you house-rule it away. Read these when you want to change something. Roughly one per major system.

**MM Note** — technique for whoever is running the game. Always states a default, the dial you may turn, and the cost of turning it. Players may read them; nothing in one is a secret.

**Example** — a worked instance, usually with the recurring cast (Zahna, Mordai, Zulnut, and their MM). Arithmetic is carried through to the total. Where an example is a scene of play, the speaker tags are `MM:` and the character's name.

**Variant** — an optional rule. Nothing in a Variant box is baseline; the game is complete without every one of them.

**Reading the Entries** — a legend for the catalog that follows it: the fields, their order, and what it means when one is missing. Every catalog in these books has one, and every entry in that catalog has the same shape.

**What Characters Can Know** — three rows keyed to the three outcome tiers, each written to be read aloud: what anyone notices on a 6−, what a knowledgeable character recalls on a 7–9, and what changes the plan on a 10+. These appear in the Bestiary, beside the creature they are about.

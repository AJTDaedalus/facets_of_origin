**Date:** 2026-09-28

**Read:** `facets_d20/` README, 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08, 10. **Not read:** 09, `data/`, `docs/`, `software/`, the Appendix. Everything below is what the player books alone teach.

## Task 1: README and Chapter 01

**Time:** about 8 minutes for both.

**What's different from 5e, in my own words:**

- **No classes.** You pick one of three Facets. It works like a stripped-down class chassis: hit die, saves, armor, two skills, one or two built-in features. Everything else comes from **five talents** taken at odd levels, from a menu of 10–13. Up to two of the five can come from another Facet's menu.
- Talents are tagged **Steel** or **Spell**. How many of each you have ("depth"), and which you have more of ("main track"), unlocks the things 5e hands out by class level: martial weapons, armor, Extra Attack, full versus half slots, extra domains.
- Even levels give a **knack** (non-combat only) or, at 4th and 8th, an ASI. The game stops at 10th level and spells stop at 5th.
- **Hit points are fixed** and never rolled.
- **Magic:** no spells known and no preparation. A caster uses a 15-spell Common list plus one or more themed **domain** lists and casts anything on them it has a slot for.
- **Backgrounds** work like 2024 backgrounds, but give a knack instead of a feat, plus a **Specialty** that grants advantage. Everyone is human.
- **Sparks** replace Inspiration: +1d6 after a roll, earned through Drives.
- **Social scenes** use an attitude track.
- **Combat:** side initiative, monsters deal average damage, morale, minions, bosses with an extra turn, and one damage rider per turn.

**Could I start building now?** Almost. Chapter 01 gives me the shape but not enough to finish a sheet. I also needed Tables 2–1, 2–2 and 2–3 and one Facet chapter. The README's claim ("read 01 and 02 and you can make a character tonight") is accurate. On my first pass, item 13 in 01 ("Body counts as two Steel talents when finding its main track") meant nothing to me. It only made sense after Table 2–3.

## Task 2: Four 1st-level builds

### (a) Preset: Priest (Soul)

The log:

1. Chose the Priest because I want a caster for the fight in Task 4. (1)
2. Name: Brother Tamsin. (2)
3. Drives: *want* "to found a free infirmary"; *line* "I won't refuse healing to anyone who asks." (3, 4)
4. Specialty: "the rites and politics of one temple," taken from the Temple Acolyte example. (5)
5. Language: from the SRD. (6)

**7 decisions**, as advertised. I checked the card's numbers myself and they are correct: HP 16+2 = 18; AC 14 (scale mail) + 0 + 2 = 16; mace +3; DC 13.

**Where I had to hunt:**
- **The card doesn't list my spells.** I had to read Chapter 07 to find the Common list and The Tide. My 1st-level list is *Fire Bolt, Light, Message, Resistance, Spare the Dying* (cantrips) and *Burning Hands, Magic Missile, Thunderwave, Detect Magic, Mage Armor, Cure Wounds, False Life, Healing Word, Inflict Wounds*. The card also doesn't list saves, skill totals or tool. "Quick way" is true for decisions, not for writing the sheet.
- **Channel, Kindle and Second Wind** are only named on the card, so I had to open Chapter 05 to find out what my 1st-level character does.
- **Surprise: my Priest knows *Fire Bolt*.** That's fine, and 07 explains it (every caster gets a damage cantrip), but it will surprise every 5e player.

**Where I had to hunt, guess, or found a conflict:**
1. **The skill overlap rule runs both ways.** Chapter 02 Step 6 says: if the background already gave you a Facet skill, "pick another" (implicitly from the Facet list). 2. **Mending Hands versus Channel.** The Soul menu's "What it's for" column helped. I picked Mending Hands for a bonus-action heal that uses no slots. Then I had to decide whether Mending Hands is a "spell" for *Rage* or *Wild Shape*, and whether the SRD 5.2.1 rule about casting a slot spell with a bonus action blocks it. I guessed "not a spell". The book never says.
3. **Casting with mace + shield + holy symbol.** I used SRD 5.2.1's "holy symbol on a shield" rule. 07's *Focus* paragraph doesn't cover hands.
4. **Kit rule:** "any preset card of your Facet, as long as you can wear its armor at 1st level." That is clear. I checked that the Oathsworn's chain mail needs Steel main (heavy armor via *Martial Training*), so I can't take it.

1. **Does "counts two extra Steel talents" count toward depth?** If it did, every Body character would have Steel 2, and Extra Attack for free. 01 says "when finding its main track", which settles it, but the Quick Reference (10, *Steel and Spell*) just says "Body counts +2 Steel" with no qualifier. 4. **The casting-ability tension** (Body wants Str/Con; the tradition wants Int or Wis/Cha) is real. Chapter 02 Step 5 hints at it, and the book is honest that a Body character "can't become a full caster". Fine.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | +2 | knack (1) | *Expertise* (Investigation, Arcana) | same | *Studied Recovery*, which does nothing yet because I don't cast. HP 17+5+1 = 23 |

| 6 | +3 | knack (1) | *Well-Travelled* | same | Nothing in combat. HP 47 |
| 7 | +3 | talent + domain (2) | *Wider Study* [Sp], Divination | 2 · 2 → **tie, Steel** | No rank change. Two domains. Half row 7: 4/3. HP 53 |
| 8 | +3 | ASI (1, plus targets) | +1 Dex, +1 Int → Dex 18, Int 17 | same | HP 59 |
| 9 | +4 | talent (1) | *Weapon Expert* [St] | 3 · 2 → Steel | **Veteran** (+1 HP/level, +2 weapon damage); *Precision* 3d6; *Anatomist* crit 18–20; *Weapon Expert* 5th/9th lines. HP 65 + 9 = 74 |
| 10 | +4 | knack (1) | *Skilled* | same | HP 80 + 1 (Veteran) = 81 |

**Choices: 12 over nine level-ups**, one per level (two at 3rd and 7th, counting the domain), plus ability targets at 4th and 8th.

**10th-level sheet:** HP 81 · AC 13+2+2+1 = 18 · rapier +9, twice, 1d8 + 4 + 2 (+3 against my studied target) · Precision 3d6 rider · crits on 18–20 against the studied target · Half slots 4/3/2 · DC 15 · two domains.

**Surprises, and whether the book warned me:**

1. **The 9th-level trap (warned).** At 9th I seriously looked at *Turn the Odds* [Sp]. That makes Spell 3 v Steel 2, so **Spell becomes main**. I'd gain the Full table (4/3/3/3/1 at once) and Deep Magic (a prismatic domain, Chronomancy for *Haste*). I'd **lose** *Martial Training* (medium armor, shields, d8 → d6) and **Extra Attack**. I'd **keep** Martial Weapons and, oddly, *Precision* 2d6, because upgrade lines need depth but not main track. The book does warn about this: 02 *Ranks can lapse* uses almost exactly this example. Good. But it doesn't say:
   - **Hit points when the die shrinks.** 02 says to recalculate "if your hit die gets bigger". Nothing covers smaller. I assume you recalculate down: from 65 (d8) to 55 (d6).
   - **Armor you're no longer proficient in.** SRD 5.2.1 says you can't cast spells in armor you aren't trained with. So the character who switches to Spell main *to cast better*, while still in a chain shirt and shield, **cannot cast at all** until they change. Nobody at my table would catch that.
   - **Hit Dice pool size** after the die changes.
2. **The trap nobody warns about: a *Steel* pick costing a *casting* rank.** Take a Spell-first hybrid: *Evoker* (1st), *Wider Study* (3rd, Spell 2, so Full table), *Precision* (5th, Spell main 2 v 1), then *Anatomist* at 7th. That makes 2 v 2, the tie goes to Steel, and the character **drops from Full (4/3/3/1) to Half (4/3) slots at 7th**. You lose your 3rd- and 4th-level slots for taking a sword talent. The *Ranks can lapse* paragraph covers this in principle, but its example runs the other way, and "Steel wins a tie" reads as harmless until it bites. A pure Spell-first build never sees it, only a hybrid does, and the hybrid is the build the Facets idea is meant to invite.
3. **Upgrade lines are gated differently from ranks.** Ranks need depth *and* main track. Talent 5th and 9th lines need depth only. A character who is Spell main with Steel 2 still gets *Precision* 2d6 but not Extra Attack. That is consistent across 02 and 10, but it is a third gate to track, and I had to verify it twice.
4. **Level 2, 6 and 10 feel empty in play.** A knack never touches combat, so three of my ten levels changed nothing I'd use in a fight. The book is upfront about this. Every 5e player will notice it.
5. **Recalculation load.** At every level I recomputed HP, and at 3, 5, 7 and 9 I also re-derived main track, ranks, slot table and upgrade lines. Leveling is "one choice" but not one *step*. A printed per-level checklist ("after every talent pick: recount depth, find main track, check Table 2–3, check 5th/9th lines, re-read slot table") would save real time.

## Task 4: Three rounds, Priest (a) and three bandits, using only 08 and 10

Setup: my Priest (HP 18, AC 16), with an ally playing the Fighter preset (HP 20, AC 19, +6, 1d8+3), against three SRD 5.2.1 **Bandits** (AC 12, HP 11, scimitar +3 for a fixed 4, light crossbow +3 for a fixed 5). The MM runs them as standard foes. As a player I'm never told whether they're minions, which is fine.

**Initiative.** The party's best Dex modifier is the Fighter's +1. We roll 9 + 1 = 10. The bandits roll 13 + 1 = 14. **Bandits go first**, and that order holds for the whole fight. *(Easy. 08 Who Goes First.)*

**Round 1, the bandits' half.** A and B close on the Fighter: 15 misses, 20 hits for a fixed 4 (Fighter 16/20). C shoots me: 17 hits for a fixed 5 (Priest 13/18).
**Our half.** We choose the order: me first.
- Bonus action: **Kindle** on the Fighter. He now has 2 Sparks.
- Action: **Magic Missile** at C. Three darts, 9 damage. C is at 2/11, so **Bloodied**, and makes a morale save (DC 10 Wis): 7, so he **breaks**. The MM has him throw down the crossbow and surrender.
- Fighter attacks A: 15 hits for 8. A is at 3/11 and Bloodied. Morale: 12, holds.
- *Hunted:* whether I could Kindle **myself** ("a creature within 60 feet that can hear you"). I guessed yes and didn't do it. When exactly C's surrender takes effect: immediately, it seems. **Not found:** whether a foe who breaks and *flees* on our half provokes an opportunity attack, or leaves on its own turn.

**Round 2, the bandits' half.** A hits the Fighter for 4 (12/20). B misses.
**Our half.** The Fighter swings at A and rolls 4 + 6 = **10**.
- **Stall:** "Does that hit?" The Spark rules (06, 08, 10) all say to spend **after the roll and before the MM says what happens**. But nothing in 08 or 10 says whether the players know the AC. In 5e the AC is usually hidden, so I either spend blind or the MM has to say "you'd need 2 more", which amounts to announcing the result. We ruled that the MM says "close" and I spend: "I shout 'left side, his guard's down'." The Spark die rolls 3, total 13, a hit for 6. A drops to 0. It was a melee hit, so the Fighter chooses to knock him out (08 *Dropping and Dying*).
- Me: bonus action **Healing Word** on the Fighter: 2d4 + 3 = 8, **+3 from Channel** (2 + slot level). *(Found in 05, not in 08 or 10.)* Fighter 20/20. Action: *Fire Bolt* at B: 11, a miss.
- *Guessed:* whether Kindle and Healing Word conflict. Yes: both are bonus actions (SRD), so it's one per turn. This is the reason not to open with Kindle every round.

**Round 3, the bandits' half.** B, alone, swings at the Fighter and misses. *(The MM could have had him check morale here, but only "when first Bloodied" triggers it, and B is untouched.)*
**Our half.** Me first: **Channel** (Magic action), harm option on B. His Con save is 6, a fail, so 1d8 + 3 = 8 radiant. B is at 3/11, Bloodied, morale 4, and he **breaks**: he drops the scimitar and begs. Fight over. Two prisoners, one unconscious. I spent 1 Spark on an ally, used 1 Kindle, 1 slot and 1 Channel.

**Rules I couldn't find in 08 or 10 during the fight:**
1. Whether target numbers (AC/DC) are known before spending a Spark. *(Biggest stall.)*
2. When a broken foe leaves, and whether fleeing provokes opportunity attacks.
3. Whether Kindle can target yourself.
4. Whether talent abilities like Channel, Mending Hands and Kindle are "spells". This matters for Counterspell, *Rage* and *Wild Shape* ("can't cast spells"), and the Druid preset's "a heal in your pocket either way" implies Channel works in beast form.
5. Where a creature that joins mid-fight goes (with its side, presumably).
6. Whether Bloodied at 11 HP is 5 or 5.5 ("at or below half" makes it ≤5, which is fine once you think about it).
7. The quick reference has no line for the **Help** action or for **Sparks on an ally needing your character to be "in a position to help"** (06 has it, 10 doesn't).

Everything else, including side order, fixed damage, morale, knock-out and crits, I found in under ten seconds. Combat is the best-taught chapter.

## Verdict

**Is it simpler than 5e?**

- **Simpler, clearly:**
  - **Character creation:** 7 or 13 decisions against the 25+ a 2024 5e build takes with species, class, subclass, origin feat, spells known and prepared.
  - **Spell management:** no preparation and no spells known. It's the best idea in the book, and Chapter 07 explains it in one paragraph.
  - **Hit points:** fixed.
  - **Monster-side combat:** fixed damage, morale, minions.
  - **Side initiative:** my fight had no waiting and was over in three rounds.
- **Heavier:**
  - **The Steel/Spell engine.** 5e multiclassing is famously fiddly, but a single-class 5e character never thinks about it. *Every* Facets character, even a pure one, has to understand depth, main track, the tie rule, rank gates and upgrade gates to level up correctly. A hybrid has to re-derive them at every talent pick, and a pick can take things away. In 5e, leveling up never costs you anything you had.
  - **Recalculation after hit-die changes** (*Martial Training*, *Hardy*, lapses).
- **Just different:**
  - Sparks replacing Inspiration: fine, but see stall #1.
  - The social track: simple and good.
  - Knacks: fine, but they make the even levels feel empty.
  - One rider per turn: a clean cap.

**Is the Facets idea ("build freely instead of picking a class") delivered?** Partly.
- **What delivers it:** the menus are small and readable, the cross-Facet rule is simple, and the Soul menu shows off priest, druid, oracle and paladin shapes very well (Table 5–3 is the best table in the book).
- **What undercuts it:**
  - The main-track rule quietly pushes you back into "pick Steel or Spell and commit". Hybrids are allowed but taxed, and the tax is hidden in a tie-break.
  - The twelve presets are named Fighter, Rogue, Barbarian, Monk, Wizard, Priest and Druid, so the book signals that the real answer is "5e classes, reassembled".
  - With only five talent picks in ten levels, the freedom is real but shallow.

It feels less like "no classes" and more like "three chassis and a class you assemble from five cards". That's a fine game, but the pitch oversells it slightly.

**Would I want to play it?** Yes, for a short campaign or a run of one-shots. I'd happily play the Priest again. As a long campaign I'd worry about the dead even levels and the thin progression from 5th to 10th.

**The single most confusing thing:** the **main-track system**, and specifically that **three different gates** decide what your talents give you:
- depth alone decides Martial Weapons, Spellcasting and talent upgrade lines;
- depth plus main track decides Martial Training, Extra Attack, Full Casting, Veteran and Deep Magic;
- "Steel wins a tie" means one weapon talent can silently cost a caster their higher slots.

Everything else in the book I understood on first read.

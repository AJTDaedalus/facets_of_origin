# Facets of Origin — Bestiary

*Nineteen things worth meeting, and what happens when you do.*

---

## What This Book Is

Something is on the road ahead and the party has stopped walking. You have about four seconds before somebody asks what it looks like.

This book is the answer to that. Every creature in it arrives finished: what it looks like at first sight, what it wants, how it fights, when it stops fighting, and what a party can do besides fight it. Nothing here is a rule — Conditions, postures, reactions, Resolve, and Threat Clocks all live in the Player Handbook, and these entries use those words without re-teaching them. What this book adds is the thing a stat line cannot hold, which is behaviour.

The Mirror Master's Manual teaches you to build enemies. This is the book for the nights you would rather not.

**Every entry ends in a way out.** Not an escape hatch for the party — an alternative for the table. Every creature in these pages has at least one resolution that is not a fight, and where a creature genuinely has none, the entry says so and explains why. That is not softness. A monster with only one solution is a door with only one key, and doors like that are why players stop asking questions.

## Everything Here Is Original

Every creature in this book was invented for Facets of Origin. None of them is derived from, named after, adapted from, or mechanically modelled on any proprietary bestiary, and no stat block, name, or piece of lore has been carried over from a published game. The project's copyright policy is in `CLAUDE.md`; this book is written to it.

That has a design consequence worth naming. You will not find the familiar roster here, and the omission is deliberate. The creatures in this book were built backwards from the mechanics — from Resolve that depletes, from Conditions that expire, from the fact that NPCs never roll, and from the three-tier outcome that means a partial success is the commonest thing that happens at your table. A chalk hound cannot strike from a standstill because standing still is a decision a player can make. A latchman states its instruction aloud because listening is a thing players do, and rewarding it is cheaper than any trap.

Bring whatever else you like to your table. Reskin these freely. But these were grown in this soil.

## The Setting Question

**None of this is Shattered Origin canon.** These creatures are deliberately placeless. They live on roads, in archives, at thresholds, and in the half-finished work people leave behind — furniture that fits almost any world because it is about almost any world's leftovers.

Every family entry that could plausibly be reskinned therefore ends with an **Adaptation** line: what to change to seat it in a particular setting, and what to keep so the entry still works after you have changed it. (The chicken does not get one. The chicken is a chicken.) Take the line seriously. A glassback with its antlers swapped for antlers of something else is still a glassback; a glassback that will start a fight is a different creature wearing the name.

The one exception is the **Archive Guardian**, which was already canon before this book existed. It appears here as the Boss expression of the Latchmen, because that is what it is.

## How to Read This Book

> **Reading the Entries — creatures**
>
> Entries are grouped in **families**, and a family ships its whole ladder: the Mook expression, the Named expression, and — where the fiction supports one — the Boss. They share one lore section, because they are one thing at three sizes.
>
> ***Italic block, first thing in the entry.*** Read-aloud. What the characters perceive on first contact and nothing else — no mechanics, no name, no hidden lore. If your party approached from somewhere the text did not expect, change it; it is a sketch, not a script.
>
> **The lore.** What is true about this creature, stated as fact. Where it hedges — *some say*, *nobody agrees* — the hedge is deliberate and marks a genuine mystery you may resolve however you like.
>
> **The stat block.** Generated from the creature's `.fof` file, so the book and the tool cannot disagree. Fields run in the order you need them: identity and Threat Rating, then what you consult when the players act on it, then what you consult when it acts, then when it stops.
>
> **What Characters Can Know.** A three-tier box keyed to the outcome tiers. Call for whatever roll the fiction supports — Lore, Survival, Insight, Investigate — and read the row the result lands on. Each row is written to be said aloud. **6−** is what anyone notices. **7–9** is what a knowledgeable character recalls. **10+** is the thing that changes the plan.
>
> **Encounters.** Two to four ready rosters, labelled with the actor-count recipes from Table MM1–7 and honest about which band they land in at Party Strength 3 — including when that band is "below Standard", which a lone Named or a Mook swarm always is. At least one in each entry is not a fight.
>
> **Ecology and Adaptation.** Where it lives, what it does to a region that hosts it, and how to move it into your setting.
>
> A field that does not apply is left out rather than written as "none". On a Named or Boss entry, an absent **Negotiation** line means there is no *deal* to be had — which is not the same as no way out, because the **Morale** line is always there and always fires. Mooks never carry a Negotiation line at all; a Mook's way out is its morale, and a Mook's morale is written to fire early.

## Finding Something

`Finding_Aids.md` lists every creature that has a stat block twice: sorted by Threat Rating, and sorted by tier. The hushfall is not in either list, because it deliberately has no stat block — it runs on a Threat Clock instead. Both are generated from the stat files, and the Threat Rating list comes first because it is the one you actually use.

The short version, for the nights you need a number now:

- **TR 1–5** are Mooks. Three to seven of them is a Skirmish at Party Strength 3, which is to say a scene, not a danger.
- **TR 8–11** are Named. Three of them plus one Mook is a Standard fight; each additional Mook moves it one band harder.
- **TR 13–17** are Bosses. Every one of them in this book can be resolved without being defeated, and three of the four would rather.

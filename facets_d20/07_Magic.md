# Magic

Magic in Facets d20 is SRD 5.2.1 spellcasting: spell slots, cantrips, concentration, rituals, upcasting. What changes is where it comes from and how much of it you have to track. You become a caster by taking a Spell talent, and your spells come from two places: the **Common list**, which every caster has, and your **domains**, each a short list of SRD spells about one thing. You don't prepare spells. If a spell is on your lists and you have a slot for it, you can cast it.

If nobody at your table has a Spell talent, you can skip this chapter.

---

## The Two Traditions

There are two traditions of magic, and each belongs to a Facet.

**Table 7–1: The Traditions**

| Tradition | Facet | Casting ability | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thaumaturgy | Mind | Intelligence | Scholarly: studied, written down, worked out |
| Invocation | Soul | The higher of Wisdom and Charisma | Intuitive: felt, called on, answered |

**Thaumaturgy** is magic built from principles the caster understands. **Invocation** is magic that is felt before it is understood. A Soul caster uses the higher of Wisdom and Charisma, so there is nothing to choose.

**Body has no tradition.** A Body character who takes a Spell talent names Invocation or Thaumaturgy with the first one, and casts with that tradition's ability from then on (Chapter 03).

A character casts from **one tradition**, whatever menus its Spell talents came from.

*Thaumaturgy* is also the name of an SRD cantrip, which sits on two Invocation domains. An Invoker can cast that spell; the name is a coincidence.

## Becoming a Caster

You become a caster by taking a **Spell** talent: any talent tagged Spell, from your own menu or (as a cross-Facet pick) another's. There is no separate spellcasting talent. The number of Spell talents you hold is your Spell depth, and it buys the ranks in Table 2–3 (Chapter 02):

- **Spell 1, whatever your main track: Spellcasting.** You cast your tradition's spells from the Common list and **one domain**, which you choose when you take the talent. Your slots use the **Half** table.
- **Spell 2, Spell your main track: Full Casting.** Your slots use the **Full** table.
- **Spell 3, Spell your main track: Deep Magic.** You gain **one more domain**, and it may be a prismatic one.

*Wider Study* (Chapter 02) adds one more domain whenever you take it.

If Steel is your main track, you stay on the Half table however many Spell talents you hold. That is what a paladin-shaped character pays for Extra Attack and heavy armor.

## Casting

**Your lists** are the Common list and every domain you have. You can cast any spell on them of a level you have a slot for, with that slot or a higher one. You know every cantrip on them. There's nothing to prepare and nothing to pick each morning.

**Spell save DC** = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your casting modifier. **Spell attack** = your proficiency bonus + your casting modifier.

**Slots** come from Table 7–2 or 7–3, read by your **character level**, whenever you took your first Spell talent. A character who takes one at 5th level casts from the 5th-level row at once. Slots return on a long rest, as the SRD says.

**Rituals.** You can cast any spell on your lists that has the Ritual tag as a ritual: it takes 10 minutes longer and uses no slot.

**Concentration** is the SRD rule: one concentration spell at a time, and damage calls for a Constitution save.

**Focus.** Any object that suits your tradition works as a spellcasting focus: a notebook of formulae, a staff, a holy symbol. You don't track a component pouch; the only components you need are ones with a gold cost, which you must own.

**Hands.** With Steel depth 1 or more, a weapon or shield you hold can be your focus, and you can perform somatic components with the hand holding it. A sword-and-shield caster never has to sheathe anything to cast.

**Armor.** You can cast only in armor you're trained with, as the SRD says. If a pick costs you an armor training (Chapter 02, *Ranks can lapse*), change armor before your next fight or you can't cast in it.

## The Common List

Every caster of either tradition has these fifteen SRD 5.2.1 spells on their list. It gives every caster a cantrip that deals damage, area damage at every spell level, and *Mage Armor*.

**Table 7–4: The Common List**

| Level | Spells |
|---|---|
| Cantrips | *Fire Bolt*, *Light*, *Message* |
| 1st | *Burning Hands*, *Magic Missile*, *Thunderwave*, *Detect Magic*, *Mage Armor* |
| 2nd | *Scorching Ray*, *Shatter* |
| 3rd | *Fireball*, *Lightning Bolt*, *Dispel Magic* |
| 4th | *Ice Storm* |
| 5th | *Cone of Cold* |

A spell on the Common list is on no domain's list.

## Domains

A **domain** is what your magic is about. The twenty-one domains are the ones the core rules name, each belonging to one tradition; Facets d20 gives each a list of SRD 5.2.1 spells. The spells' rules are the SRD's. This book only says which domain each lives in.

You choose your first domain with your first Spell talent, from your tradition's domains. *Wider Study* and Deep Magic each add one more. A spell on two of your domains is still one spell. When you retrain (Chapter 02), a domain is one of the things you can swap.

**Prismatic domains.** Six domains (The Undying, Fate and The Living World for Invocation; The Arcane, The Constructed Mind and Chronomancy for Thaumaturgy) are the pinnacle kinds of magic. You can learn one only as the domain Deep Magic gives you, which means three Spell talents with Spell your main track, at 5th level at the earliest. No character starts with one.

**No smite spells.** No list holds a spell that adds damage to your next weapon hit for a slot, or that triggers on your next weapon hit at all. *Sworn Strike* (Chapter 05) is the one way to buy that. *Hunter's Mark* (Beasts) adds damage to each hit while you concentrate on it and isn't a rider.

| Verdance | Invocation | control | Living plants: growing, shaping, animating |
| Binding | Invocation | control | Oaths, pacts, and spiritual constraints |
| Presence | Invocation | support | Projecting spiritual weight, emotional aura |
| The Tide | Invocation | support | Vitality flowing between living things |
| The Undying *(prismatic)* | Invocation | offense | Death, spirits of the departed, undead |
| Fate *(prismatic)* | Invocation | support | Probability, luck, threads of possibility |
| The Living World *(prismatic)* | Invocation | support | Animating current through all living things |
| Illusion | Thaumaturgy | control | False sensory experience: images, sounds, smells |

The **role** is a rough guide to what the list is best at. With the Common list underneath, no domain leaves you without damage, so pick the one your character would study or be drawn to.

## Example: Zahna's First Spells

Zahna is Mind, with Intelligence 16 once his Guild Apprentice background is added. His player takes *Evoker* at 1st level. It's a Spell talent, so Zahna is a caster from the start, and he names his domain: Inscription.

→ **Spellcasting** (Spell 1): Thaumaturgy, the Half table, two 1st-level slots. At 1st level the Full and Half rows are the same.
→ **Spell save DC** 8 + 2 + 3 = **13**. **Spell attack** +5.
→ **Cantrips:** *Fire Bolt*, *Light*, *Message* (Common) and *Mending*, *Prestidigitation* (Inscription). *Evoker* adds his Intelligence modifier to one damage roll of each damaging spell, so his *Fire Bolt* deals 1d10 + 3.
→ **1st-level spells:** the Common list's five, plus *Comprehend Languages*, *Identify* and *Illusory Script*. He can cast any of them with either slot.

**MM:** At 3rd, if you take a second Spell talent, you're on the Full table: four 1st-level slots and two 2nd.

**Zahna:** And *Fireball* at 5th, if I'm still a Spell-main caster. I'm aware of the table.

**MM:** (He has read the table more closely than whoever wrote it.)

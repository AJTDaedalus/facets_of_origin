# V2. The Magic of Val'loh

Val'loh has the same magic as the rest of the game. Same 2d6, same Domain plus Intent plus Scope, same difficulty by scope, same Sparks, same Fatigue for Significant and Major workings. There is no Val'loh-only limit, count, or tempo rule. If you know how to cast in Facets of Origin, you know how to cast here.

What differs is only **where a domain comes from**, and there are two answers.

---

## Gifts

A Gift is carried in the blood (Chapter II.5), and it is two things: a **gift knack** — *Orthaen gift*, *Phern gift* — that covers exactly what your lineage's Gift line describes, and **Minor workings in one domain**, which **you choose.** Any Soul or Mind domain in the core catalog that is not prismatic. Your lineage does not pick it; your lineage says how it *shows itself*.

So an Orthaen's gift always comes through grown crystal — but one Orthaen's crystal burns (Fire), another's holds a working for later (Inscription), and a third's grows walls across a corridor (Warding). Two Orthaen at the same table can be as different as a Soul mage and a Mind mage. A Phern's gift is always a prickle before danger; what that prickle is *made of* is up to the player.

Three things are the same for every gift, whatever you choose:

**It is Minor, for life.** Small, local, brief; never damage, never deciding anything; in a fight it is a stunt. It costs no Fatigue, and it does not grow on its own.

**The knack is the gift's real reach.** When a roll is about the thing your Gift line describes — reading crystal, feeling trouble coming — the gift knack's +1 applies, whether or not you work any magic at all.

**It is the same domain everyone else uses.** A caster in Shattered Origin can learn the domain your gift is. A gifted character who wants full workings — Significant and Major, paid in Fatigue — takes a casting talent at creation or at a level-up, like anyone else (Chapter II.4), and the gift's domain is the natural one to reach for where the tradition allows it. The difference between you and a caster from elsewhere is where the magic came from and how it looks, never what it can do.

## Spellcraft

The other answer is study, and it works as Chapter II.3 already writes it: the Thaumaturgy talent, cast with Mind, from the Mind list of domains — on the Mind menu, or from another Facet's with a teacher found in play.

The fiction of Val'loh says spellcraft is a patient art. A spellform is drawn thread by thread; the useful ones take a scholar hours, and the great ones take seasons; the histories are full of workings that collapsed because someone tried to finish one in a hurry. Every word of that is true in the world.

**None of it is a rule.** A spellform caster in play rolls what any caster rolls, as often as their Fatigue allows, at the difficulty their scope sets. The patience is where the fiction lives — in what a character has spent their life on, in why the Church guards its scholars, in why nobody in a burning hall is drawing threads — and the difficulty ladder is where the game lives.

A gifted member of a tribe may learn spellcraft as anyone may. Nothing about the blood interferes with the study, and several of the more irritating figures in Val'loh's history have had both.

---

## Crystal

Orthaen crystal holds finished workings. This is the fact the whole economy of Val'loh rests on, and it is why the walls of Rekuzan are wealth and not decoration.

In play, a **charge** is a **curio** (Chapter IV.2): one stored working, used once, released at a touch by anyone — gifted or not, trained or not. There is no roll. It counts against the three curios a character can carry, and releasing one does not make its bearer a caster.

That last part is the point. A charged crystal is magic that the ungifted can carry and spend, which is the only route to a working most people in Val'loh will ever have, and it is why a small pink splinter is worth a season's wages in the wrong district.

**Growing one** is an ordinary working in the grower's own domain, done in downtime, under the normal rules of Chapter II.3 — the working is whatever the charge will hold, and the crystal is how an Orthaen's gift shows itself. Nothing extra is tracked: the character makes the roll their scope calls for, and if it lands they have a charge. A gift alone grows Minor charges; growing a Significant or Major one needs a casting talent and costs the Fatigue any working of that size costs.

**Releasing one is not a working**, so it spends nothing — no Fatigue, no roll. That is what makes a charge worth a season's wages: it is magic grown on a quiet day and spent on a bad one, carried past the night's rest that would otherwise have cleared the grower's Fatigue.

**Spending one** takes no roll at all. The charge is in the inventory or it is not. When the fiction makes a *release* chancy — fumbled in the dark, jostled in a crowd, held near something that eats magic — that is a Soul roll at Standard, which is a thing the table already knows how to do.

The six charges every gifted Orthaen has heard of are **steady light**, **a sealed door**, **a veil of quiet**, **a chime at a threshold**, **warmth**, and **a held image**. All Minor. All curios. A setting can grow the list; the rules do not change when it does.

> **MM Note — crystal and the things that eat it**
>
> **Default:** a charge releases when it is touched, and it works.
>
> **The dial:** you can rule that a specific thing in your scene *suppresses* charges — they gutter, they will not take, the light dies as it leaves the stone.
>
> **The cost:** almost nothing, and it is the best tool this setting hands you. It is a legible, physical signal that something is badly wrong, and it costs a player no resource to receive. Use it where you would otherwise have narrated dread. Do not use it so often that players stop growing charges.

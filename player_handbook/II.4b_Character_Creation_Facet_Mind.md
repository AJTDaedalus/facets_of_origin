# Character Creation: Facet of the Mind

Zahna has been looking at the same ledger for six minutes and has not turned a page. He is not stuck. He is waiting for the thing he already noticed to finish arriving.

## The Mind Facet

Characters of the Mind Facet solve problems by understanding them. They are scholars and detectives, navigators and arcane theorists, the person who notices the detail no one else caught and the person who already knows what that detail means. Where a Body character reshapes the world through presence and force, a Mind character reshapes it through perception, knowledge, and the quiet advantage of being correct before anyone else realizes the question was asked.

Their tools are attention, memory, and analysis. Their superpower is that they are almost never the most surprised person in the room.

## Facet of the Mind — Technique Tree

The Mind tree has three branches: **Clarity** (Intelligence), **Instinct** (Wisdom), and **Archive** (Knowledge).

---

### Clarity Branch

Clarity Techniques represent the trained precision of a disciplined mind — the ability to take a situation apart, find the load-bearing piece, and know exactly what to do with it.

---

#### Tier 1

**Sharp Analysis** *(Clarity, Tier 1 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** At will.

When you spend time examining an object, mechanism, or scene — at least a few focused minutes — you may ask the MM one question about it that must be answered honestly. *What holds this together? What is this for? What happened here?* The MM cannot deflect with "you're not sure." You spent the time. You get an answer. *(The question must concern something your character can directly observe or has directly interacted with — not speculation about future events or the inner lives of people you haven't met.)*

**Normal:** What examination turns up is an Investigate roll, and "you are not sure" is an answer the MM may give.

**Dissecting Failure** *(Clarity, Tier 1 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** Passive.

When you fail an Investigate roll, you still learn one true fact about what you were examining. Your analysis fell short — but your mind does not come up entirely empty. The MM chooses what you learn; it is always real, and it always matters.

**Normal:** A 6− means things go wrong — the story moves forward, but not in your favor (see *Failure (6−)*, III.1).

---

#### Tier 2 *(requires one Clarity Tier 1)*

**Structural Weakness** *(Clarity, Tier 2 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** Once per scene.

When you have had the chance to observe a plan, structure, mechanism, or system in action, you may declare one specific flaw or vulnerability within it. The MM must confirm the flaw exists and describe what exploiting it would require. You cannot invent a weakness that isn't there — but if one is there, you will find it.

**Normal:** Whether a plan or a structure has an exploitable flaw is the MM's to decide and yours to find through play.

**Working Theory** *(Clarity, Tier 2 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** Once per scene.

After you have gathered at least one piece of evidence about a situation, you may state a conclusion about it as a theory. The MM tells you whether your theory is *correct*, *partially correct*, or *wrong* — and if wrong, gives you the one fact that most directly contradicts it. You do not need to have all the evidence yet. You just need enough to reason.

**Normal:** The MM narrates what your character observes; drawing the right conclusion from it is yours to do, unconfirmed.

---

#### Tier 3 *(requires one Clarity Tier 2)*

**The Deduction** *(Clarity, Tier 3 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** Once per session.

You may make a reasoned deduction about a situation, location, or person you have not directly observed — working only from second-hand information, physical evidence, or inference. State your conclusion clearly. The MM tells you whether it is correct. If it is, everything that follows in the fiction treats your conclusion as established fact.

**Normal:** You reason from what your character has observed (see *Acting on Unnarrated Details*, III.1).

**Pressure Point** *(Clarity, Tier 3 — Intelligence)*

**Use:** Once per session.

When you have identified a specific weakness, flaw, or vulnerability in an obstacle (using Structural Weakness or by other means), you may describe exactly how to exploit it. The MM must treat this as the optimal approach — the difficulty of acting on your analysis is one step easier for any character who follows your instructions, for the rest of the scene.

**Normal:** Difficulty is Standard by default, and the MM adjusts it one step from the situation (see *Difficulty*, III.1).

---

> **Example — buying an answer instead of rolling for one**
>
> **MM:** The ledger's last four pages are in a different hand.
>
> **Zahna:** Sharp Analysis. What is this for?
>
> **MM:** *(who would much rather have been asked something vague)* It's a duplicate. Someone copied the last four pages of a ledger that already existed, and copied them badly enough that the totals don't reconcile with the earlier hand.
>
> **Zahna:** Then where is the original?
>
> **MM:** That's a second question, and you get one. Roll Investigate like everybody else.

### Instinct Branch

Instinct Techniques represent a mind so attuned to pattern and environment that it operates ahead of conscious thought — the gut sense that something is wrong, the read on a room that arrives before you've identified what you're reading.

---

#### Tier 1

**Never Surprised** *(Instinct, Tier 1 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Passive.

You are not caught off guard. If the MM would call for a roll to notice an ambush, trap, or sudden threat before it lands, you automatically succeed. You may not always be able to prevent what's coming — but you always see it coming.

**Normal:** Noticing an ambush or a trap before it lands is a roll the MM calls for.

**The Wrong Note** *(Instinct, Tier 1 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Passive.

When something in your environment is deliberately concealed, falsified, or out of place, the MM will tell you that something feels off — even if they do not tell you what. This is not a roll. You simply notice the dissonance. What you do with that feeling is still yours to decide.

**Normal:** Noticing that something has been concealed or falsified is an Investigate or Insight roll.

---

#### Tier 2 *(requires one Instinct Tier 1)*

**Immediate Threat** *(Instinct, Tier 2 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Once per scene.

You may ask the MM: *"What is the most immediate danger here?"* They must answer honestly and specifically. Not *"the room feels dangerous"* — the actual threat, named. You can act on this information as if you had observed it directly.

**Normal:** What a character notices about a room is an Investigate or Insight roll.

**Assess the Situation** *(Instinct, Tier 2 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Once per scene.

When you enter a social situation — a negotiation, a gathering, a tense confrontation — you may ask the MM two questions about the emotional or political dynamics in the room. *Who holds the real power here? Who is afraid? What does this person actually want?* Both answers must be honest.

**Normal:** Reading the dynamics of a gathering is an Insight roll.

---

#### Tier 3 *(requires one Instinct Tier 2)*

**First Move** *(Instinct, Tier 3 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Once per session.

Your instincts extend to everyone around you. You may declare that the party acts on your read of the situation. This exchange, the opposition does not act until every party member's action has resolved — no ambush springs, no prepared trap goes off, and an enemy defeated or disrupted before its moment never gets that moment at all. No roll required.

**Normal:** In an exchange everyone acts and reacts at once. There is no initiative, and nobody acts first (see *Exchanges*, III.3).

**Pattern Recognition** *(Instinct, Tier 3 — Wisdom)*

**Use:** Once per session.

You may name a pattern you have observed across multiple scenes or sessions — a repeated behavior, a structural similarity, a tell that keeps appearing. The MM must confirm whether the pattern is real. If it is, you understand something about the situation, the antagonist, or the world that no amount of direct investigation could have uncovered. The MM tells you one true implication of that pattern that the fiction has not yet revealed.

**Normal:** Connections across scenes are the players' to make and the fiction's to confirm, not the MM's to certify on request.

### Archive Branch

Archive Techniques represent the depth of accumulated learning — not just knowing a lot, but knowing *the right thing* at *the right moment*, and understanding how to bring that knowledge to bear when it matters.

---

#### Tier 1

**Field of Mastery** *(Archive, Tier 1 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Passive.

**Choose:** A field of knowledge (e.g. history, arcane theory, natural sciences, theology, law, languages, geography).

Choose one field of knowledge: history, arcane theory, natural sciences, theology, law, languages, geography, or another domain with MM approval. Lore rolls that fall within your chosen field are treated as one difficulty step easier. You are not broadly competent here — you are genuinely expert, and the fiction should reflect it.

**Normal:** Difficulty is Standard by default, and the MM adjusts it one step from the situation (see *Difficulty*, III.1).

**Arcane Study** *(Archive, Tier 1 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Passive.

**Choose:** One domain from the Domains of the Mind list.

**Roll:** Knowledge.

You have developed a formal magical domain through scholarly practice. Choose one domain from the Domains of the Mind list (Chapter II.3). You may use magic within that domain using the Domain + Intent + Scope framework. Roll Knowledge when doing so.

**Normal:** A domain's origin is a Background, and it is limited to Minor scope until a Facet Technique unlocks it (see *Acquiring a Domain*, II.3).

---

#### Tier 2 *(requires one Archive Tier 1)*

**First Encounter** *(Archive, Tier 2 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** At will, once per subject.

When you encounter something for the first time — a creature, a location, a historical figure, an artifact, a faction — you may ask the MM two questions about it that must be answered truthfully. *What is its weakness? What does it want? What is its origin? What does this symbol mean?* The answers represent what a scholar of your depth would already know. You do not need to have met this thing before. You just need to have read the right things.

**Normal:** What your character already knows about something new is a Lore roll.

**Cross-Reference** *(Archive, Tier 2 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Once per scene.

When you are presented with a new piece of information, you may immediately connect it to something you already know. Tell the MM what you think the connection is. They must tell you whether you are right — and if you are, describe one additional implication that the connection reveals.

**Normal:** Connecting new information to old is a Lore or Investigate roll.

---

#### Tier 3 *(requires one Archive Tier 2)*

**The Knowledge That Saves** *(Archive, Tier 3 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Once per session.

Your depth of knowledge prevents a consequence the party would otherwise face. Name what you know — the compound's weakness to salt water, the governor's standing debt to this guild, the creature's aversion to open flame, the treaty clause that voids the arrest warrant — and the MM confirms whether it applies. If it applies, the consequence is negated. You do not roll. You simply know the thing that matters, because you have always been the kind of person who reads everything.

**Normal:** A consequence the fiction has established lands. Knowing about it is a Lore roll, not a cancellation.

**Deep Archive** *(Archive, Tier 3 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Once per session.

You may recall something you have no direct reason to know — a piece of esoteric knowledge from a tradition you weren't trained in, a fact from a period of history outside your specialty, a technical detail from a craft you've never practiced. Describe what you're reaching for. The MM tells you whether your character, given their depth of study and their habit of reading broadly and well, could plausibly have encountered this knowledge. If yes: you know it, and it is accurate.

**Normal:** You know what your character has plausibly learned, and a Lore roll settles the uncertain cases.

**Second Domain** *(Archive, Tier 3 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Passive.

**Choose:** A second standard domain from the Domains of the Mind list — it must differ from your first, and prismatic domains are excluded.

**Roll:** Knowledge, one difficulty step harder than normal for that domain.

*Requires an existing Mind domain (Arcane Study).* Your study has opened onto a second territory — an adjacent field whose principles your first domain taught you to read. Choose a second standard domain from the Domains of the Mind list (prismatic territories require Ascendant Domain, below). You may use magic within that domain using the Domain + Intent + Scope framework, rolling Knowledge as normal. Effects within your second domain are treated as one difficulty step harder than normal for that domain. A character holds one Second Domain.

**Normal:** A character has one magical domain, and its origin is a Background (see *Acquiring a Domain*, II.3).

**Ascendant Domain** *(Archive, Tier 3 — Knowledge)*

**Use:** Passive.

**Choose:** One prismatic domain from the Domains of the Mind list.

**Roll:** Knowledge, on the Broad difficulty table.

*Requires an existing Mind domain (Arcane Study).* Your practice has outgrown a single territory. Choose one prismatic domain from the Domains of the Mind list (Chapter II.3). You may work within it using the Domain + Intent + Scope framework, rolling Knowledge. The Broad difficulty table applies — Hard at Minor scope, Very Hard at Significant and Major — and its Major-scope ceiling cannot be moved by Sparks. Your original domain is unchanged; the prismatic territory is a wider practice built on everything it taught you. A character masters one prismatic territory — Ascendant Domain is taken once, however many Facet trees they eventually climb (Chapter II.3).

**Normal:** Domains are Focused or Standard; prismatic territories are not otherwise available (see Table II.3–2).

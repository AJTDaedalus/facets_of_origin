## Summary: the principles that made pre-3e light

1. **The player sheet was a handful of single numbers.** Class, level, HP, AC, a to-hit number, a few saves, a spell count. Almost nothing on it needed interpretation.
2. **Complexity lived on the referee's side as procedure**, mostly as quick die rolls: reaction, morale, surprise, encounter distance, wandering monsters, loyalty. Players never learned these; they felt their effects.
3. **"Rulings, not rules."** The referee adjudicated the unlisted case instead of the book enumerating it ([Finch primer via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance)). This kept the rulebook short *and* made description (not sheet options) the player's main tool.
4. **The reward loop pointed away from fighting.** Most XP came from treasure recovered, so avoiding, tricking or bribing a monster was the rational play ([Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4131/roleplaying-games/reactions-to-odd-experience-points)).
5. **Morale and reaction rolls made most encounters non-binary.** Monsters talked, hesitated, fled and surrendered, so combat rarely ended in slaughter and often didn't start ([Alexandrian on morale](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/2477/roleplaying-games/thinking-about-morale)).
6. **Combat was short and abstract.** Side initiative, one attack roll, one damage roll, HP as a pool of luck and stamina rather than wounds ([Philotomy](https://save.vs.totalpartykill.ca/grab-bag/philotomy/)).
7. **Time was a resource, tracked in turns**, and every turn spent risked a wandering encounter. That one lever created pressure without any "difficulty" rules ([Alexandrian, dungeoncrawl](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15140/roleplaying-games/game-structures-part-3-dungeoncrawl)).
8. **One reusable micro-mechanic (the X-in-6 roll)** covered doors, searching, listening, surprise, getting lost: announce the chance, roll, move on ([Delta](https://deltasdnd.blogspot.com/2021/09/d6-usage-in-od.html?m=1)).
9. **Class was the whole build.** Picking a class (and, in Basic, your race *was* your class) settled almost everything; choices during play were about the world, not the sheet ([Wikipedia, character class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_class_(Dungeons_%26_Dragons))).
10. **Tables quietly ignored the heavy bits.** Weapon-vs-armor, weapon speed, grappling and segment initiative in AD&D were widely skipped, so the "light" memory is partly selective ([Dungeon Fantastic](https://dungeonfantastic.blogspot.com/2020/09/ad-rules-weapon-armor-class-adjustment.html); [Tenkar](https://www.tenkarstavern.com/2012/12/weapon-speed-do-i-need-it-in-ad-1e.html)).

## 1. The editions: player-facing load vs referee procedure

Dates and structure: [Wikipedia, Editions of D&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editions_of_Dungeons_%26_Dragons); comparative histories at Leyline Press ([B/X](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-and-dragons-basic-and-expert-b-x-d-d-1981), [AD&D 1e](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-dragons-ad-d-1977), [BECMI](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-and-dragons-becmi-1983), [Holmes](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-dragons-holmes-basic-d-d-1977)).

### 1.1 Summary table

| | OD&D (1974 + supplements) | Holmes Basic (1977) | B/X (Moldvay/Cook 1981) | BECMI / Rules Cyclopedia (1983–91) | AD&D 1e (1977–79) | AD&D 2e (1989) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What a player had to know** | 3 classes (later 4+), 6 scores with tiny modifiers, HP, AC, a to-hit lookup, 5 saves, spells/day | OD&D tidied into one book; every weapon d6 | 7 classes incl. race-as-class; ±1–3 ability mods; 5 saves; d6 side initiative | B/X plus optional weapon mastery and general skills; up to level 36 | Race separate from class; ~10 classes incl. sub-classes; 9 alignments; class-specific matrices; weapon proficiencies | THAC0 formula; class groups; weapon + non-weapon proficiencies; kits; later *Player's Option* point-buy |
| **Referee procedures** | Wandering-monster d6, surprise, reaction, loyalty (hidden 3d6), X-in-6 checks, hex travel, strongholds | same, simplified | Reaction (2d6), morale (2d6 vs score), retainer morale from CHA, encounter distance, wilderness procedure | same + War Machine mass combat, dominion rules | All of the above plus a huge DMG of tables | Morale/reaction kept but less central; DM-facing "story" advice grows |
| **Combat** | 1-minute rounds, one roll to hit, d6 damage originally, side initiative | d6 damage for all weapons | Side init d6; variable weapon dice; morale check in sequence | Same; optional weapon mastery adds detail | d6 side init plus segments, weapon speed, weapon-vs-AC adjustments, grappling tables | THAC0; individual initiative variants; specialization |
| **HP & recovery** | Small HD; slow natural recovery | same | 1d3 per full day of rest ([EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/becmi-bx-natural-healing.676391/); [ref summary](https://www.enworld.org/threads/example-of-basic-d-d-combat.250815/)) | same | 1 per day, more with extended rest | similar |
| **Spells** | Vancian: prepare, cast once, per-day counts by level | same | same; first-level M-U has one spell | same | same; long lists, material components, casting time in segments | same; specialist wizards; spheres for priests |
| **XP** | Mostly treasure (1 gp ≈ 1 XP); monsters minor | same | same | same | same, plus class-specific training costs | GP-for-XP made optional; class-activity XP ("individual awards") |
| **Levels** | Name level ~9–10 → stronghold; open-ended | 1–3 | 1–14; demihumans capped | 1–36 + Immortals | Open-ended; racial caps | Racial caps; name level stays |
| **Where it got clunky** | Scattered, ambiguous text | Very short | Few gaps | Weapon mastery, skills bolted on | Weapon-vs-AC, speed factors, psionics, grappling, segment initiative | THAC0 subtraction, NWPs, kits power creep, psionics handbook |

### 1.2 Notes per edition

- **OD&D.** Three booklets; the referee filled gaps. Nearly all exploration checks were d6 rolls, and in most of them a *low* roll meant "something happens" ([Delta, d6 usage](https://deltasdnd.blogspot.com/2021/09/d6-usage-in-od.html?m=1)). Searching and listening used 1-or-2-in-6 style chances, better for elves and dwarves ([Grognardia](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2010/10/most-overlooked-rules.html); [Rancourt survey](https://rancourt.substack.com/p/a-survey-of-searching-for-secret)). Hirelings got a *hidden* loyalty score the referee rolled on 3d6, which is why some were brave and some deserted ([Necropraxis, loyalty](https://necropraxis.com/2012/08/22/odd-loyalty-morale/)). HP was abstract: stamina, luck, nerve and skill being used up over a minute-long round of feints and near-misses, not literal wounds ([Philotomy's Musings](https://save.vs.totalpartykill.ca/grab-bag/philotomy/)). XP came overwhelmingly from treasure; the Greyhawk supplement actually made monster XP *less* rewarding ([Grognardia, Gold as Experience](https://grognardia.blogspot.com/2022/06/gold-as-experience.html)).
- **Holmes (1977).** OD&D reorganised into one approachable book for levels 1–3; all weapons dealt d6, which kept arithmetic trivial ([Smoldering Wizard](https://smolderingwizard.com/2022/05/23/musings-on-weapon-damage-in-old-school-dd/); [Leyline, Holmes](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-dragons-holmes-basic-d-d-1977)).
- **B/X (1981).** The OSR's favourite baseline. Race-as-class (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling are classes, with level caps), three alignments, levels 1–14, variable weapon damage, a wilderness procedure and *Isle of Dread* as a sample hexcrawl. Deliberately left room for the referee to adjudicate ([Leyline, B/X](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-and-dragons-basic-and-expert-b-x-d-d-1981)). Combat round: roll d6 per side, winners move/shoot/cast/melee, with morale checked when things go badly; morale is 2d6 against a score of 2–12 ([Jeffro, sequence of play](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2013/05/13/the-bx-sequence-of-play-for-combat/); [Alexandrian, morale](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/2477/roleplaying-games/thinking-about-morale)). CHA set how many retainers you could keep and their morale, instead of OD&D's per-hireling loyalty roll ([Spriggan's Den](https://spriggans-den.com/2016/08/19/reading-through-moldvay-basic-1981/); [Necropraxis](https://necropraxis.com/2012/08/22/odd-loyalty-morale/)).
- **BECMI / Rules Cyclopedia.** Same core, extended to level 36 and Immortals. The Companion set is the first clean presentation of the **endgame**: strongholds, dominions, mass battle ("War Machine") ([Grognardia, Companion retrospective](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2022/12/retrospective-dungeons-dragons.html); [Leyline, BECMI](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-and-dragons-becmi-1983)). Optional weapon mastery and general skills show the pull toward sheet-side detail even in the "Basic" line. Grognardia's verdict on Companion: many good ideas, not all well executed.
- **AD&D 1e.** Race split from class; the four core classes gained **sub-classes** (fighter → paladin, ranger; cleric → druid; magic-user → illusionist; thief → assassin), with stat prerequisites and alignment strings ([Wikipedia, character class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_class_(Dungeons_%26_Dragons)); [Leyline, 1e](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-dragons-ad-d-1977)). Also added: weapon speed factors and segment initiative, weapon-vs-armor-type adjustments, grappling/pummeling tables, psionics. Many tables ignored these ([Dungeon Fantastic, weapon vs AC](https://dungeonfantastic.blogspot.com/2020/09/ad-rules-weapon-armor-class-adjustment.html); [Dungeon Fantastic, speed factor](https://dungeonfantastic.blogspot.com/2020/09/ad-rules-weapon-speed-factor.html); [Tenkar](https://www.tenkarstavern.com/2012/12/weapon-speed-do-i-need-it-in-ad-1e.html)).
- **AD&D 2e.** Cleaned up content, dropped assassin/half-orc, formalised **THAC0** ([Wikipedia, editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editions_of_Dungeons_%26_Dragons)), grouped classes into four **class groups** sharing HD, to-hit progression and saves ([Wikipedia, character class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_class_(Dungeons_%26_Dragons))). Non-weapon proficiencies were widely felt as rigid and oddly bolted-on; kits were often unbalanced and multiplied through splatbooks ([EN World, NWPs](https://www.enworld.org/threads/views-on-2nd-ed-non-weapon-proficiencies.301104/); [Dragonsfoot, NWPs](https://www.dragonsfoot.org/forums/viewtopic.php?t=90353); [EN World, broken kits](https://www.enworld.org/threads/ad-d-2e-broken-kits.276218/)). THAC0 and descending AC are mathematically fine but required subtraction and counter-intuitive "lower is better" armor ([EN World, descending AC](https://www.enworld.org/threads/it-made-sense-at-the-time-descending-ac.695998/); [Points of Light](https://daegames.blogspot.com/2023/06/ascending-ac-is-superior-to-descending.html)).

### 1.3 Precedent for "Facets as parents of classes" (owner constraint)

Pre-3e D&D tried **three** ways to put concrete classes under broader parents. All three map directly onto Body / Mind / Soul:

| Precedent | How it worked | What it teaches us |
|---|---|---|
| **1e sub-classes** | Paladin and ranger *were* fighters with extra requirements and powers; druid under cleric, illusionist under magic-user, assassin under thief ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_class_(Dungeons_%26_Dragons)); [Dungeoneers Guild](https://www.dungeoneersguildgames.com/post/first-edition-ad-d-character-classes)) | The parent carries the chassis; the child is a named identity plus 2–4 distinctive abilities. Prerequisites and alignment strings were the clunky part — skip them |
| **2e class groups** | Warrior / Wizard / Priest / Rogue each shared hit dice, attack progression and saving throws; individual classes differed only in abilities and restrictions (same source) | **The strongest precedent.** Put *all* numbers (HP die, attack bonus growth, save/defence growth) on the Facet; let a class be a short ability list. One progression table per Facet, three in the whole game |
| **BECMI at-level branching** | A neutral cleric could become a druid at 9th level; Companion added paladin/avenger options for fighters at name level ([Leyline, BECMI](https://leyline.press/blogs/leyline-press-blog/a-comparative-history-of-dungeons-and-dragons-becmi-1983); [Grognardia](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2022/12/retrospective-dungeons-dragons.html)) | Specialisation can arrive *later*: start as "a Body character", pick the concrete class at level 2–3 once the player knows what they like. Good for onboarding |
| **Race-as-class (B/X)** | Elf = fighter/magic-user blend with its own table | Hybrid classes that straddle two Facets are cheap if they're just a named ability list; avoid separate progression tables for each |

2e's lesson is that grouping *reduces* rules: once the group owns the numbers, a new class costs one paragraph. 1e's lesson is that sub-class gating (stat minimums, alignment, level caps) becomes friction fast.

## 2. The OSR's interpretation

The OSR crystallised around 2004–2008 (Dragonsfoot, OSRIC 2006, Labyrinth Lord 2007, Swords & Wizardry 2008, *Fight On!* 2008) ([Wikipedia, OSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance)).

### 2.1 Principles, with sources and one-line implications

| Principle | What it means | Source(s) | Implication for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rulings, not rules** | Most situations are adjudicated by the referee from the fiction, not looked up | Finch, *Quick Primer* (2008), summarised in [Wikipedia, OSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance), [Gnome Stew review](https://gnomestew.com/review-of-the-a-quick-primer-for-old-school-gaming/), [Revenant Quill](https://www.revenant-quill.com/2020/10/in-praise-of-rulings-not-rules.html), [Grumpy Wizard on its subtleties](https://grumpywizard.home.blog/2023/07/13/subtleties-of-rulings-not-rules/) | The PHB can be short *only if* the MM Manual teaches how to rule. Our 2d6 three-tier roll already is a ruling engine; stop enumerating cases |
| **Player skill over character skill** | Outwit the problem; describe what you do | Finch primer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance)) | Drop the skill list; a good description should often succeed with no roll |
| **"The answer is not on your character sheet"** | Look at the situation, not your options list | [Kate Korsaro](https://kateplays.substack.com/p/the-answer-is-not-on-you-character); critique: [Prince of Nothing](https://princeofnothingblogs.wordpress.com/2023/09/09/the-answer-is-not-on-your-character-sheet-a-correction/) argues it was a reaction to 3e bloat and turns into a "cargo cult" that devalues higher-level play where spells and items *are* the tools | True at low level; at higher level the sheet should hold a few *open-ended* tools (domain magic, items), not a menu of narrow feats |
| **Heroic, not superheroic** | Competence comes from experience and cleverness, not powers | Finch primer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance)) | Keep power bands narrow (2d6 does this for free) |
| **Game balance is not the referee's job** | The world isn't levelled to the party; information lets players choose | Finch ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_Renaissance)); [Mythic Mountains, "Myth of OSR Lethality"](https://mythicmountainsrpg.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-osr-lethality): "OSR games are about *agency*, not *lethality*." | Encounter recipes become *signal* tools (tell players it's dangerous), not balance tools |
| **Combat as war vs combat as sport** | War: stack the odds before the fight, or skip it; Sport: fair tactical contests | Daztur, EN World 2012 ([thread](https://www.enworld.org/threads/very-long-combat-as-sport-vs-combat-as-war-a-key-difference-in-d-d-play-styles.317715/)); [Greyhawk Grognard](https://www.greyhawkgrognard.com/2012/02/05/combat-as-war-vs-combat-as-spor/); [Wandering Gamist](https://wanderinggamist.blogspot.com/2012/02/combat-as-war-vs-combat-as-sport.html) | A light system has to be combat-as-war: cheap rules, decided by preparation and position. Our 10.8k-word combat chapter is a combat-as-sport artefact |
| **Decisive combat** | Two or three big decisions per fight; fights end in 2–3 rounds; exits at every stage | McDowall, [Decisive Combat](https://www.bastionland.com/2017/05/decisive-combat.html): "It's a combat of two or three major decisions, rather than a dozen minor ones." Also [auto-hit in Into the Odd](https://www.bastionland.com/2015/03/describing-auto-hit-in-into-odd.html) | Matches the fun audit's "two decisions per exchange" finding; accept it and make fights short rather than adding options |
| **HP as abstract grit/luck** | HP loss = stamina, luck and nerve spent; real wounds come late | [Philotomy](https://save.vs.totalpartykill.ca/grab-bag/philotomy/); Kemp's Death & Dismemberment (0 HP → injury table, not death) via [Fen Orc](http://www.fenorc.co.uk/death--dismemberment.html), [THAC0 blog](http://thac0rpg.blogspot.com/2020/02/why-i-made-switch-to-death-dismemberment.html) | "HP is grit, injuries only once it's gone" (§7.1 of the audit) is squarely in the tradition; keep the existing Condition list as the injury table |
| **Gold-for-XP / treasure as the reward loop** | You advance by bringing loot home, so avoiding fights is rational | [Alexandrian, OD&D XP](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4131/roleplaying-games/reactions-to-odd-experience-points): "What you wanted to do was get the treasure *without* fighting the monsters." [Grognardia](https://grognardia.blogspot.com/2022/06/gold-as-experience.html); [DMDavid](https://dmdavid.com/tag/the-fun-and-realism-of-unrealistically-awarding-experience-points-for-gold/) | Reward the thing the game is about. For an adventure-register game: XP for *discoveries, treasure recovered, goals met*, never for kills or rolls |
| **Spend-to-advance variants** | Carousing: spend loot in town for XP with a mishap table | Rients, [Party like it's 999](http://jrients.blogspot.com/2008/12/party-like-its-999.html) (2008); history at [Yore](https://www.martinralya.com/tabletop-rpgs/debauchery-and-dragons-carousing-for-xp-1977-to-2015/) | A great app feature: a between-sessions "carouse" button that spends gold, grants XP and rolls a social complication (worldbuilding fuel) |
| **Exploration / negotiation / combat** | Three modes of play; old-school design makes the first two primary and communication the default | [Principia Apocrypha site](https://lithyscaphe.blogspot.com/p/principia-apocrypha.html) ("old school D&D favors communication over violence as a default") | Reaction rolls + morale + treasure-XP are what make negotiation viable; adopt all three together or none work |
| **Procedures over rules** | Give the referee repeatable loops (turn, watch, encounter check) rather than more rules for players | Alexandrian, [Game Structures](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15126/roleplaying-games/game-structures), [Dungeoncrawl](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15140/roleplaying-games/game-structures-part-3-dungeoncrawl): the dungeon gives players an answer to "What do I do next?"; [Hexcrawl](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/17308/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl) | This is exactly what software is good at |
| **Don't prep plots, prep situations** | Prep places, factions and tools; let events follow PC action | Alexandrian, [Don't Prep Plots](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4147/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots), [Tools not Contingencies](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/37422/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots-tools-not-contingencies) | The adventure builder should store situations, factions, tables — not scene sequences |
| **Meaningful, informed choice; fair deaths; never fudge** | Tell players enough to decide; let results stand; use morale/escape instead of fudging | Kemp, [Advice for OSR DMs](https://goblinpunch.blogspot.com/2020/05/advice-for-osr-dms.html) | Rolling in the open in the app, with morale/escape as the pressure valve |
| **Adventures must be usable at the table** | Evocative-but-terse keys; context for choices; multiple routes | Bryce Lynch, [Review Standards](https://tenfootpole.org/ironspike/?page_id=1201); summary at [Into the Dark](http://into-the-dark-rpg.blogspot.com/2016/07/bryce-lynchs-adventure-design-tips.html) | A standard for Oraga Night and every module format |
| **Principles as a primer** | Short AW-style principles for referee and players | [Principia Apocrypha](https://lithyscaphe.blogspot.com/p/principia-apocrypha.html) (Milton, Lumpkin, Perry) — **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**: *not* GPL-compatible (non-commercial clause). Read for ideas only; do not copy text | Write our own one-page "MM principles" |

### 2.2 Modern OSR simplifications worth knowing (openly licensed)
- **Into the Odd / Bastionland** (McDowall): no to-hit roll, damage always lands, 0 HP → damage to STR and a save to stay up ([search summary + Bastionland blog](https://www.bastionland.com/2015/03/describing-auto-hit-in-into-odd.html)). Has its own open SRD/licence ("Mark of the Odd") — verify terms before borrowing text.
- **Knave** (Milton): classless, gear-as-build, inventory slots; **CC BY 4.0** for both editions ([itch](https://questingbeast.itch.io/knave)).
- **Cairn** (Gal): Knave + Into the Odd; **CC BY-SA 4.0** ([itch](https://yochaigal.itch.io/cairn)).
- **Unified saving throw**: collapse the five save categories to one number plus contextual stat ([Azorynian Post](https://azorynianpost.substack.com/p/the-case-for-a-unified-saving-throw)).

## 3. Procedure catalogue (our own words)

Each entry: what it does, what problem it solves, whether the app can own it.

| Procedure | How it works (paraphrase) | Problem it solves | App can roll it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reaction roll** | On meeting creatures whose intent isn't obvious, the referee rolls 2d6 (± charisma-ish modifier): low = hostile, middle = uncertain/wary, high = friendly or open to talk ([Spriggan's Den](https://spriggans-den.com/2021/06/03/a-take-on-reaction-rolls-and-the-charisma-modifier-anomaly/); [Alexandrian, encounters](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46192/roleplaying-games/5e-hexcrawl-part-5-encounters)) | Stops every encounter defaulting to a fight; surprises the referee too, so NPCs feel alive | **Yes**: one click on spawn; result shown only to MM |
| **Morale** | Each monster has a morale number; when it takes first loss or half its side is down, roll 2d6: over the number → it breaks, flees or surrenders ([Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/2477/roleplaying-games/thinking-about-morale)) | Ends fights early; creates prisoners, parley and chases; removes the slog of killing the last goblin | **Yes**: app auto-prompts at trigger points (first casualty, half down, leader down) |
| **Surprise** | Each side rolls d6; 1–2 = surprised, losing the first moment ([Alexandrian, encounter distance](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46466/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-encounter-distance)) | Rewards stealth and scouting; seeds ambush fiction | **Yes** |
| **Encounter distance** | Roll how far apart the sides are when they notice each other (dice × 10 ft in dungeons, more outdoors) (same source) | Tells the MM whether talk, flight or ambush is possible before anyone acts | **Yes** |
| **Wandering-monster check** | Every few turns (dungeon) or each watch (wilderness), roll a d6; a 1 means something shows up, rolled on a local table ([Alexandrian, dungeoncrawl](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15140/roleplaying-games/game-structures-part-3-dungeoncrawl)) | Makes time a cost; creates urgency with zero extra rules | **Yes**: the app tracks the clock and rolls |
| **Overloaded encounter die** | One d6 per turn whose every face means something: encounter, a sign of a nearby creature, a local change, fatigue, light source dwindling ([Necropraxis](https://necropraxis.com/2014/02/03/overloading-the-encounter-die/)); generalised into the Hazard System | Replaces several separate timers (torches, rest, wandering monsters) with one roll that almost always produces content | **Yes, and it's the single best fit** for our Trouble Table / Threat Clocks |
| **Exploration turn** | Dungeon time in ~10-minute chunks; each turn the party does one thing (move a set distance, search a room, rest); check for encounters ([Alexandrian, Game Structures](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15126/roleplaying-games/game-structures)) | Makes caution cost time; gives the table a rhythm and a default action | **Yes**: a turn counter the MM advances |
| **Wilderness watch / hexcrawl** | Travel in watches; each watch: move, navigate (chance of getting lost), forage, check encounters ([Alexandrian, Hexcrawl](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/17308/roleplaying-games/hexcrawl); [Necropraxis](https://necropraxis.com/2012/06/20/alexandrian-hex-crawling/)) | Turns overland travel into play instead of a montage | **Yes** |
| **X-in-6 checks** | The referee states a chance out of six (search 1–2, listen 1, etc.) and rolls ([Delta](https://deltasdnd.blogspot.com/2021/09/d6-usage-in-od.html?m=1)) | One universal micro-roll for "might it happen?"; the chance *is* the explanation | We already have 2d6 tiers for player actions; X-in-6 suits *world* checks the MM makes (the Oracle) |
| **Retainer hiring & loyalty** | Recruit via a reaction-style roll; each hireling has a morale/loyalty value; test when danger exceeds what they signed up for ([Necropraxis](https://necropraxis.com/2012/08/22/odd-loyalty-morale/); [Spriggan's Den](https://spriggans-den.com/2016/08/19/reading-through-moldvay-basic-1981/)) | Gives the social game stakes; lets small parties go deep; retainers become replacement PCs | **Yes**: app holds retainer sheets and prompts loyalty tests |
| **Treasure-as-XP accounting** | Loot recovered to safety converts to XP | Points play at exploration and risk-weighing | **Yes**: automatic when loot is marked "banked" |
| **Carousing** | Spend loot in town, gain XP, roll a mishap ([Rients](http://jrients.blogspot.com/2008/12/party-like-its-999.html)) | Sinks gold, generates NPCs and hooks | **Yes** |
| **Domain / stronghold turn** | At name level, build a keep, attract followers, run a domain turn (income, events) ([Grognardia, Companion](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2022/12/retrospective-dungeons-dragons.html)) | A late-game goal that turns wealth into world change | Later module; app is ideal for income bookkeeping |
| **Reaction point (framing)** | Describe the scene, leave the thing that demands a response for last ([Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/45297/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-the-reaction-point)) | Keeps momentum; players act on full information | MM advice, not a roll |

**Why OGL text is off-limits for us:** the OGL restricts claiming compatibility and carries Product Identity carve-outs; a review on Freedom Defined notes these would likely count as further restrictions under GPLv3 §7 ([freedomdefined.org](https://freedomdefined.org/Licenses/OGL)). **Bottom line:** for a GPLv3 project, **re-express every concept in our own words**; the only old-school texts we could legally adapt verbatim are CC BY / CC BY-SA 4.0 ones (BFRPG 4e, Knave, Cairn, SRD 5.x), with attribution. We don't need to: our 2d6 chassis means we'd rewrite anyway.

## 5. Pitfalls modern players hit in old-school play (with evidence)

| Pitfall | Evidence | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Random, uninformed death** ("one of four identical doors kills you") | Players quit after early goblin deaths; lottery-style danger is the complaint, not danger per se ([Mythic Mountains + comments](https://mythicmountainsrpg.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-osr-lethality); [Dining Table Adventures](https://diningtableadventures.com/2025/09/27/dont-fear-the-reaper-why-character-death-is-the-feature-not-the-flaw-in-osr-games/): lethality is "the single greatest psychological barrier") | Telegraph danger (Lynch's *context*; Kemp's *information*); death only after a visible 0-HP → Injury → Broken ladder; keep the existing death choice |
| **Death is dramatically dead-end** | Critics argue death is the least interesting outcome and turns RPG into board game ([Pits Perilous](http://pitsperilous.blogspot.com/2025/05/tripping-death-cult-osr.html); [RPGnet thread](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/why-is-death-so-important-in-the-osr-why-not-challenging-or-unforgiving.927646/)) | Our adventure register already says no. If death happens, "make it easy to live again" ([Prismatic Wasteland](https://www.prismaticwasteland.com/blog/a-better-approach-to-deadly-games)): retainers step up, one-minute character creation |
| **Save-or-die** | Old poison and petrification were instant death on one failed save ([Mythic Mountains comments](https://mythicmountainsrpg.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-osr-lethality); [Taking the Sting Out of Poison](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22327009-taking-the-sting-out-of-poison)) | No single roll ends a character; worst case is an Injury |
| **1-HP wizards, one spell a day** | Low-level casters get one spell and then nothing; widely patched with spell points or skill-casting ([RPGnet, non-Vancian options](https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads%2Fnon-vancian-options-for-b-x-osr-magic.908477%2F=); [Azorynian, magic problems](https://azorynianpost.substack.com/p/new-magic-mechanics)) | Keep free Minor magic (we already have it); HP floor per Facet |
| **Confusing saves and odd categories** | Five save categories unrelated to stats confuse newcomers ([Azorynian, unified save](https://azorynianpost.substack.com/p/the-case-for-a-unified-saving-throw)) | One "avoid it" roll on the relevant stat (audit §7.3 already does this) |
| **Counter-intuitive math** (THAC0, descending AC) | [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/it-made-sense-at-the-time-descending-ac.695998/); [Points of Light](https://daegames.blogspot.com/2023/06/ascending-ac-is-superior-to-descending.html) | Higher is better, always; the app does arithmetic |
| **Fiddly exception rules** (weapon-vs-armor, speed factor, grappling) | Widely ignored in practice ([Dungeon Fantastic](https://dungeonfantastic.blogspot.com/2020/09/ad-rules-weapon-armor-class-adjustment.html); [Tenkar](https://www.tenkarstavern.com/2012/12/weapon-speed-do-i-need-it-in-ad-1e.html); [19th Level](https://19thlevel.blogspot.com/2012/06/speed-factors-and-weapons-vs-armor.html)) | If a rule is routinely skipped, it wasn't a rule. No subsystems for single actions |
| **"Nothing to do but attack"** | Melee players just attack; binary HP means focus-fire is the only rational play ([Castle Grief](https://castlegrief.substack.com/p/5-ways-to-make-combat-more-fun-in); [Twilight Dreams](https://twilightdreams.substack.com/p/theater-of-the-mind-combat)) | Fixes are *referee-side*: morale, environment, clear positioning, enemies that retreat. Plus our 7–9 "pick a cost" gives each roll a choice. Don't answer it with a menu of maneuvers |
| **Slow levelling / little growth** | Constant level-1 death means little continuity ([Mythic Mountains comments](https://mythicmountainsrpg.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-osr-lethality)); gold-XP thresholds need big hauls | Short level track (1–10), first levels come quickly; XP for discovery and goals, not only gold |
| **Rigid or unbalanced options bolted on** (NWPs, kits) | [EN World NWPs](https://www.enworld.org/threads/views-on-2nd-ed-non-weapon-proficiencies.301104/); [EN World kits](https://www.enworld.org/threads/ad-d-2e-broken-kits.276218/) | Background lines instead of a skill list; classes are short, fixed ability lists within a Facet |
| **Cargo-culting the slogans** | "Not on your sheet" misapplied at higher levels ([Prince of Nothing](https://princeofnothingblogs.wordpress.com/2023/09/09/the-answer-is-not-on-your-character-sheet-a-correction/)); "rulings" becomes arbitrariness without guidance ([Grumpy Wizard](https://grumpywizard.home.blog/2023/07/13/subtleties-of-rulings-not-rules/)) | Give the MM worked examples of rulings; make players' open-ended tools (magic, items, gear) grow with level |

## 6. Implications for a lean Facets of Origin

Opinionated. Read alongside `RESEARCH_complexity_audit.md` §7.3; these either confirm or sharpen that sketch.

### 6.1 Adopt as concept (in our own words)

1. **Treasure-and-discovery XP.** The single most important old-school lesson. XP from (a) loot banked, (b) discoveries/secrets found, (c) goals achieved. **Nothing** for kills or rolls. This is what makes talk, stealth and cleverness rational, and it deletes the marks-for-rolls contradiction the audit found.
2. **Reaction roll, morale, surprise/encounter distance** as MM-side 2d6 rolls. We already use 2d6, so the curve and bands are native: reuse our three tiers (6− hostile / 7–9 wary / 10+ open). Monsters get **one morale number**.
3. **The overloaded encounter die** as the backbone of the Trouble Table / Threat Clock: each exploration "turn" the app rolls one die whose faces are *encounter / sign / local change / fatigue / supplies / quiet*. One roll, always content, replaces several timers.
4. **Exploration turn and wilderness watch** as the pacing loop, advanced by the MM in the app.
5. **HP as grit, then Injuries.** HP for everyone (monster HP = current Resolve renamed), recovered fully by a night's rest (more generous than B/X's 1d3/day on purpose, to suit our adventure register). At 0: an Injury from the current Condition list; second Injury: Broken. No save-or-die anywhere.
6. **Decisive fights.** Aim for 2–3 exchanges. Morale plus low-ish HP make that happen; do not add options to make fights "interesting" — use terrain, morale and objectives instead.
7. **Retainers.** Named hirelings with one loyalty number; they are also the "easy to live again" answer if a PC falls.
8. **Carousing / downtime spend** as a between-sessions app feature: gold → XP (or reputation) plus a complication roll that feeds worldbuilding.
9. **One universal save** ("avoid it": roll 2d6 + relevant stat), not categories.
10. **Rulings guidance in the MM Manual** with worked examples, since "rulings" only works if the MM has seen good ones.

### 6.2 Facets as parents of classes (owner constraint)

Follow **2e's class-group model, not 1e's sub-class gating**:

- **The Facet owns all the numbers.** HP die, attack/defence growth, save growth, and the level table live on Body, Mind or Soul. Three tables in the whole game.
- **A class is a name + 4–8 abilities**, chosen within its Facet (e.g. Body → Warrior, Scout; Mind → Scholar/Thaumaturge; Soul → Speaker/Invoker). Adding a class later (in a setting Facet or homebrew) costs one page, never a new table — which is exactly the open/homebrew goal.
- **Optional late specialisation (BECMI precedent):** a new player can start as "a Body character" and commit to a class at level 2 or 3. Good onboarding.
- **Skip 1e's friction:** no stat prerequisites, no alignment requirements, no level caps by lineage.
- **Hybrids** (1e multi-class / B/X elf) are just a named class that borrows abilities from two Facets but uses one Facet's numbers.
- Magic stays tied to Facets as now: Mind → Thaumaturgy, Soul → Invocation; Body has no magic.

### 6.3 Avoid

- Anything a real table would skip: per-weapon speed, weapon-vs-armor tables, grappling subsystems, segment initiative, psionics.
- A skill list (2e NWPs) and kits that stack on classes. Background lines do this job.
- Separate save categories; subtraction math; "lower is better".
- Random, unsignalled lethality; instant-death effects; 1-HP starting characters.
- Vancian one-spell-a-day casters. Our free Minor magic plus a small per-rest count for full workings is already the fix the OSR keeps reinventing.
- OGL text of any kind; CC BY-NC-SA (Principia) text.
- Treating combat balance as the MM's job. Encounter recipes should become "how dangerous does this *look*" signals.

### 6.4 How a digital app changes the calculus

Old-school depth lived in referee procedures, and the reason many modern tables drop them is **bookkeeping**: tracking turns, torches, rations, wandering checks, retainer morale, loot-to-XP conversion. That is precisely what software absorbs. So:

- **Procedures that were "too much hassle" become free.** The app can run the turn clock, roll the encounter die, prompt morale at trigger points, roll reactions on spawn, convert banked loot to XP, and track retainer loyalty — each one click, results private to the MM until revealed.
- **Keep them MM-side and optional.** The player-facing rule count stays ~25; the MM toolbox can hold dozens of procedures without costing players anything. This is the audit's "variety from the MM's side" made concrete.
- **Roll in the open.** Show players the dice the MM chooses to reveal (reaction and morale results are dramatic; showing them builds trust — Kemp's "never fudge").
- **Tables as content.** Encounter, rumour, loot, carousing-mishap and reaction tables are data the app stores per module/Facet, so homebrewers add variety by adding tables, not rules.
- **Guard rails.** The app must not bring the complexity back through the side door: if the engine can track weapon speed, someone will want it. Rule: the app automates *procedures from the MM Manual*, never adds player-side options the book doesn't have (consistent with the CLAUDE.md "simulator may not re-implement a rule" principle).

### 6.5 One-line verdict

The OSR's lightness came from **plain numbers on the sheet, rich procedures behind the screen, and a reward loop that makes fighting optional**. Our 2d6 core already fits; adopt treasure/discovery XP, reaction + morale + an overloaded encounter die, HP-as-grit with injuries, and 2e-style Facet class groups — and let the app carry the procedural weight that made real tables abandon them.

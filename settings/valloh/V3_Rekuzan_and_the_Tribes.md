# V3. Rekuzan and the Tribes

*(MM-facing. Everything here is public knowledge in Val'loh — a player character raised on the continent knows all of it and would be surprised to be told any of it. Nothing in this chapter is a secret; the secrets belong to the adventures.)*

---

## Val'loh, 3164 PG

The continent is in perpetual conflict and has been for as long as its stories reach back.

To the **west**, the mage tribes skirmish with Mazaa in the sacred mountains, where the power of the gods draws nearer to the land than anywhere else and the ungifted Mazaaians dig for god-touched stones. Desecration, the tribes say — disturbing things that should not be disturbed.

To the **east**, the Blackwatch keeps its vigil over the mists that roll in from the sea like tides. The Church calls them a warning against pride and blasphemy, left over from sins old enough that nobody can name them any more.

Those two threats unite the tribes under the gods and their Church. Nothing else does. The tribes war over territory as their grandparents did, and the Church blesses it: the strong are chosen by Fraden, and so the strong deserve what they take.

### The recent wound

This year the eastern mists have **receded to record lows**. The coast is clearer than living memory.

The Blackwatch, whose whole disposition is a preference for the predictable, are quietly unnerved: an unexplained low tide is not a gift, it is a held breath. Inland the news travels as a curiosity — a line of gossip for people who enjoy eerie stories, repeated at dinner and forgotten by the next course.

Keep it in the players' peripheral vision. It matters.

## Rekuzan

The first city of Val'loh and still by far its largest and most stable: the crown jewel of the Orthaen, the dominant tribe of the central farmlands.

In the deep past Rekuzan was nine walled districts — one for each of the eight sects of the Orthaen, and a ninth at the centre for trade. The city long since swallowed the gaps between them with wood-and-stone homes, taverns and industry, but the old district walls still stand: walls of **pink crystal**, grown over generations by the Orthaen gift, luminous at dusk. The sect palaces are crystal too. Everything else in the city is dull by comparison and knows it.

The eight sects of the Orthaen, in the order their banners hang in the Chiefs' Concourse: **Boranis, Draunel, Morrain, Vaskarin, Solvane, Kethaun, Tessarin, Ilvane.** House Boranis holds the chieftaincy and has for generations.

## There Are No Books

By god-ordained law, ancient beyond memory, the written word belongs to the Church and to nobody else. The Church writes what its necessity requires. Everyone else remembers.

Val'loh's knowledge lives in memory, in apprenticeship, in the Scora's sanctioned cloth-strip rite, and in crystal. Histories are recited. Accounts are kept in tally-cords and in stewards' heads. Testaments are sworn aloud before witnesses.

The exceptions are jealously narrow, and there are two:

- **A great house's formal invitation** — a few lines under a chief's own hand, among the few writings the Church suffers outside its walls. This is part of why a genuine invitation is worth so much to the wrong person.
- **Private slate scratch-work**, wiped before anyone leaves the room.

A player raised on other settings needs to hear this early, because it silently rewrites a dozen assumptions: no libraries, no letters, no notes passed under a door, no forged documents, no reading a dead man's journal. What there is instead is people who remember, and the Scora, who remember for everybody.

## The Oraga Nights

The harvest festival — three nights at the turn of the season, and the emotional peak of the Val'loh year. Oraga celebrates the yield, steels the community for the harder season ahead, and honours those lost along the way. The living feast so the year's dead can hear it.

On the final night, Rekuzan wears masks. By old custom the masks are **spirit-faces**: the dead of the year, the spirits of the harvest, the luck and grief of the season made wearable. To wear a mask on the last night is to lend your body to something that no longer has one — a courtesy to the departed, and a licence for the living, since nothing said or done behind a spirit's face is entirely *yours*.

At midnight the bells ring the **Unmasking**. The masks come off, the spirits are thanked and sent home, and the living toast the year to come with their own faces.

It is a beloved, boisterous, slightly dangerous custom. Affairs begin behind masks. Debts are forgiven and grudges declared. And every Oraga somebody uses the custom for business that cannot stand daylight — which is why the great houses post extra guards, and why nobody is ever entirely comfortable at the Unmasking bell.

## Everyone Carries a Knife

Weapons are worn openly and constantly, at every social level and every occasion including the most formal. A blade at a ball is not a threat; it is dress. Going unarmed is the remarkable choice, and it is usually read as either a statement or a disability.

**Drawing** is the crime. The distance between wearing a knife and drawing one is the whole of Val'loh's public order, and it is enforced by everyone present rather than by any authority. A room will move on someone who draws before the guards get there.

This is worth stating to a table early and once, because it inverts an instinct most players bring with them: the swords in the room are not foreshadowing.

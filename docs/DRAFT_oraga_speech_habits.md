# DRAFT: mid-tier speech habits for Oraga Night (TP22)

*Worker draft, WP11 T11.10, 2026-10-25. For owner approval. Nothing here is in the chapters yet:
applying it is T11.11, which waits on the owner.*

TP22 asks for one speech habit each for Kovaun, Callun, Draunel, Essin, Maiven, Anha, Tavva and
the two Bought officers, drafted strictly from printed traits, with no sample quotes in the book.
Each habit below is one or two lines and cites the printed text it rests on (chapter VII entry
unless another chapter is named; quoted, because line numbers drift).

Each habit also has an **example sentence**. The examples are here only to show the owner how a
habit sounds. TP22 rules out sample quotes, so T11.11 would print the habit lines and leave the
examples out, unless the owner says otherwise. The examples name no new person, place or fact.

**Derivation key.** *Pure derivation:* the habit restates behavior the book already prints, as
voice. *Adds something:* the habit adds a small mannerism the book does not print. Each one is
marked.

---

### 1. Prelate Damaris Kovaun
- **Habit:** she calls everyone by name and station before they have given either, and she ends
  an exchange by repeating the other person's answer back once, as if filing it.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Kovaun* ("works the room like a census-taker of souls"; "a career
  diplomat in vestments"); **Wants** ("to file Raunu Boranis under something"); 04 Movement III
  Church line ("the Warden knows the character's name without having been told it").
- **Example:** "You came in with the border cousins, I think. And you say he seemed tired. Tired.
  Thank you."
- **Marked:** the names are pure derivation. Repeating the answer back *adds something*.

### 2. Mistress Rhaza Callun
- **Habit:** a compliment first, then the question, then an exact sum. She never rounds a number
  and never names a price she won't pay.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Callun* ("cordial, terrifyingly numerate"; "pays on delivery"); 04
  Movement III Circle line ("Callun's compliments and a quiet question, 'What did he say?', with
  10 GP behind it"); her block's **Tells** (chapter X); **Secret** ("check the arithmetic").
- **Example:** "What a lovely mask. What did he say? Ten gold, and that is a fair price for one
  sentence."
- **Marked:** the compliment, question and coin are pure derivation. "Never rounds" *adds
  something*.

### 3. Lord Essar Draunel
- **Habit:** he talks around what he wants. He never says "seat" or "chieftaincy" aloud, and he
  praises Vorlain's year in a way that is really a question.
- **Rests on:** **Fears** ("being seen wanting it"); *What Draunel Knows* ("wants to know if he
  liked the taste"); his block's **Breaks** (chapter X: "the first to say it was a
  misunderstanding among friends"). VOICE R19 proposed the same method.
- **Example:** "Vorlain ran the house for a year, and ran it well. I only wonder whether he misses
  it."
- **Marked:** pure derivation.

### 4. Essin Boranis
- **Habit:** he never says no. He moves: a step between, a hand at an elbow, a glass refilled,
  and the talk turned to the other person's mask. (This replaces his "forgettable on purpose" with
  a physical tic, as VOICE P2-2 asked.)
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Essin* ("always somehow between Vorlain and whoever is working
  him"); 04 Movement II Boranis line ("Essin does not threaten; he places people"); the Boranis
  Cousin's Blade **Tells** (chapter X: "Admires a character's mask from the side nearest
  Vorlain").
- **Example:** "What a mask. Come and let me see it in better light. Vorlain will keep."
- **Marked:** pure derivation (the blades' printed behavior, given to Essin himself).

### 5. Maiven Nolonaire
- **Habit:** short, plain demands, and the same question asked again rather than rephrased. When
  she is refused she stops talking instead of arguing.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Maiven* ("thirty degrees hotter-tempered"); **Secret**
  ("border-raised and direct"); 04 Movement II ("asks, with thinning patience, when the delegation
  will be received"); her block's **Tells** (chapter X: "She says nothing to Corval at all, which
  is worse than anything she might have said").
- **Example:** "When will the delegation be received? I asked an hour ago. I am asking again."
- **Marked:** pure derivation.

### 6. Anha
- **Habit:** flat facts, one at a time, and never a guess. When a fact runs out she stops, and
  says she doesn't know what it means.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Anha* ("she deals only in facts"; "Everything she knows is true and
  none of it is the answer"); **Fears** ("giving the wrongness a name, in case naming is what
  makes it real").
- **Example:** "Meals for two go up. Three plates come down. I wash them. That's all I know."
- **Marked:** pure derivation.

### 7. Tavva
- **Habit:** she talks like someone quoting a job: courteous, unhurried, in costs and times.
  Cornered, she puts her one offer on the table at once.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying Tavva* ("unhurried, courteous"; "bargains fast and honestly when
  cornered, and abandons any prize that starts costing blood"); **Wants** ("a professional
  opportunity that will not come twice").
- **Example:** "You want me out, and I want the door. Names and routes for the door. It's the only
  offer I have."
- **Marked:** the bargaining is pure derivation. The "quoting a job" register *adds something*.

### 8. Sergeant of the Bought
- **Habit:** he answers with the terms. Asked anything, he says what the contract says, calmly,
  and says out loud any deal he is about to take.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying the Sergeant* ("opens by naming the terms aloud and expects to be
  believed"); *The negotiation surface* ("he will say so before he takes it"; a better offer
  "made in front of his Blades, which he will take openly"); the Bought Sergeant **Tells**
  (chapter X: "opens by reading the contract's terms aloud").
- **Example:** "Terms are, the gate stays shut until the last bell. Make me a better offer in front
  of my Blades and I'll hear it."
- **Marked:** pure derivation.

### 9. Captain-under-Contract
- **Habit:** he says little and asks more, slowly, while the fighting goes on. His questions are
  about what the party is protecting and what the fires mean.
- **Rests on:** *Roleplaying the Captain* ("nothing tonight hurries him"; "watching who the
  party protects, and on his second he starts talking while the attacks continue"); *The
  negotiation surface* ("He contracted for diversions, not for this"); *DM Note — who hired them*
  ("this has begun to bother him a great deal").
- **Example:** "Diversions, they told me. Does that look like a diversion to you?"
- **Marked:** the slow talk is pure derivation. Leading with questions *adds something*.
